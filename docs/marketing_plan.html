The rule, in one breath

Run two weeks of Meta app-install ads to Tier-1 Android players with three to five gameplay videos. We are viable when a Tier-1 install costs under about one dollar, four in ten installers play again the next day and one in ten is still there a week later. Over three dollars an install, or under three in ten back on day one, and we stop and think again. In between, we fix the thing the numbers point at (the ad, the store page, or the first five minutes) and run one more week.

Minimum spend to hear the answer: about $500. Comfortable: $1,500 to $2,500. Nothing above that is worth spending before the retention numbers are in.

What a CPI test is, and why CPI alone does not decide

CPI is cost per install: what one new player costs when bought with ads. On its own it only says whether people like the look of the game enough to tap and install. A game that installs cheaply and is deleted the same evening is not viable; a game that installs dear but keeps people for weeks might be. So the test measures the whole funnel, from the ad to the seventh day, and the decision reads three numbers together: the install price, the share who come back on day one, and the share still there on day seven.

Ad metrics (the platforms report them)

  • CPM: price per thousand impressions. Set by the audience and the market, not by us.
  • CTR: clicks per impression. The creative's job.
  • IPM: installs per thousand impressions. CTR times store conversion; the single best number for comparing creatives.
  • CPI: spend divided by installs. What we are here for.

Store metrics (the Play Console reports them)

  • Store CVR: installs per store-page visit. The listing's job: icon, screenshots, the trailer, the first sentence.
  • Install to open: installs that opened the game at least once. Should be above 85%; lower means a download size or a crash problem.

Game metrics (PostHog, our events)

  • D1 / D3 / D7 retention: the share of a day's new players who play again one, three, seven days later. The number the decision hangs on.
  • Playtime on day 0, runs per session, how many reach the first boss, the first portal, stage 2.
  • Revive ad acceptance: how often a dead hero takes the ad. Our first revenue proxy.

Money metrics (later)

  • ARPDAU: ad revenue per daily active user. With rewarded revives plus a few interstitials, survivor-likes earn roughly $0.05 to $0.20 a day per US player.
  • LTV: what a player earns over their life; about ARPDAU times the sum of the retention curve. Viable means LTV at ninety days beats CPI with room to spare.

The method: a store listing, three ads, two reads

  1. Put the game where an ad can send people. A Google Play listing in open testing (anyone with the link can install; no review beyond the basic policy checks) or a production release. Ads need a store URL; a Drive link cannot be measured and looks like malware to most people.
  2. Wire the measurement before the first dollar. The PostHog events from the telemetry plan, plus one new one: install_attributed, sent once on first launch with the campaign, ad set and creative names read from the Google Play Install Referrer (a free Android API; the ad's link carries utm_ tags, Play passes them to the app on first open). This is how we know which ad brought which player and whether that player came back, without an attribution company in the middle.
  3. Read one: the creative test (3 days, about $150). Three to five videos in one Meta campaign, one ad set per video, same audience, same budget. After roughly 50,000 impressions each we know the CTR and IPM of each video to within a fifth. Keep the best two, drop the rest.
  4. Read two: the install and retention test (7 days, the rest of the money). The two winning videos, the Tier-1 audience, a steady daily budget so the installs spread over the week (retention needs a day-one cohort that is a week old by the end). The CPI is known after three days; D1 after four; D7 after the second week.
  5. Decide with the table below. Green on all three: build on. One amber: fix what it names, run one more week. Any red: stop spending; the game needs a different first hour or a different pitch, not more ads.
Two ways to count installs. Route A, no SDK (the $500 test): the ad sends people to the store page; Meta reports clicks, the Play Console reports installs by referrer, and the CPI is spend over installs by hand. Meta cannot optimise towards installs this way, so the CPI reads a little worse than it could; that is fine for a go/no-go read. Route B, with install events (the $2,000 test): an attribution partner on a free tier (AppsFlyer Zero or Adjust's free plan) posts installs back to Meta, which then optimises for them and typically brings the CPI down a third. Route B needs a small Android plugin in the Godot export; worth it only once Route A has said "promising".

Channels: which, in what order, and why

Meta Ads (Facebook + Instagram) first

The cheapest place to learn, by a distance: self-serve, any budget, the best creative testing tools, the broadest reach in every market we care about, and the audience that plays survivor-likes on a phone is on Instagram. Use Advantage+ app campaigns once Route B is in; a plain conversion campaign to the store link for Route A.

TikTok Ads second

Cheaper impressions, a younger audience, and gameplay videos do well there without any polish. Run the two winning videos as a second cell in read two if the budget is the comfortable one; it tells us whether our CPI is a Meta number or a game number.

Google Ads app campaigns later

Automated, needs install volume to learn (fifty a day at least), and it hides the creative-level read we want now. The right tool once the game is proven and the question is scale, not viability.

Unity Ads, ironSource, AppLovin later

In-game inventory in other games, where survivor-like players actually are, and often the best CPI for mid-core. But they want their SDK in the build and minimum spends from the low thousands. Belongs to the scaling plan, with the ad revenue side of the same companies.

Free channels always

A devlog thread on r/AndroidGaming and r/incremental_games, the survivor-like Discords, a short trailer on YouTube and TikTok. Not for the CPI test (we cannot buy a controlled sample there) but for a baseline of organic installs to compare retention against: organic players retain better, so paid D1 under organic D1 by ten points is normal.

The creatives

Five vertical videos, 15 to 20 seconds, captured from the phone build at 1080 × 1920, no voice, big captions, the first two seconds the busiest frame we have. Each is one idea, so the test tells us which idea sells:

  1. The horde. Minute twelve of a run, the screen full, the hero carving through. Caption: "One thumb. Survive the desert."
  2. The satisfying one. Barrels bursting into gems, the level-up reveal, a chest landing. Caption: "Every barrel is a present."
  3. The choice. The portal: Extract with the gold, or Continue into stage 2. Caption: "Take the gold or push your luck."
  4. The bosses. The Gila's rumble lines, the Brander's ring of fire closing in. Caption: "Three bosses. Then hell stays."
  5. The heroes. Astro, the Drifter and Calavera, ten seconds each of their special weapon. Caption: "Three heroes, three ways to die."

Two static images as well (the hat icon on sand, a horde screenshot) because statics are cheap and sometimes win on CTR. Every ad links to the same store page with its own utm_content so the referrer tells the game which video brought the player.

Budget: the minimum and the comfortable

$500the minimum that answers the question: ~$150 on the creative read, ~$350 on Tier-1 installs (300 to 450 installs at a $0.80 to $1.20 CPI), one channel, Route A
$2,000the comfortable test: two channels, three markets, a second creative round, Route B attribution, 1,200 to 2,000 installs, D7 known to within three points
$0the organic baseline: a store listing, a trailer, three forum posts; worth doing in the same weeks for the comparison

Why these sizes: an install price is known to within about 20% after 250 installs; a day-one retention of 35% is known to within six points after 300 day-zero players, within three after 1,200. The minimum test can tell 30% from 40%; it cannot tell 36% from 40%, which is why an amber result sends us back for one more week rather than to a decision.

Deciding

The rules, written out

  • Viable: CPI at or under $1.00 and D1 at or above 40% and D7 at or above 12% (or D3 at or above 22% if the week is not up). Keep building; the next question is monetisation, and the scaling plan starts.
  • Promising: CPI under $2.00 and D1 at or above 35%, D7 at or above 8%. Fix what the amber names, spend the second round, decide again. A game can live here for a month of iteration; it should not live here for three.
  • Not yet: D1 under 30% whatever the CPI. Ads are not the problem; the first run is. Stop spending, change the first five minutes, come back.
  • Stop: CPI over $3.00 with every creative, or D7 under 5% with a good D1 (people try it and leave). The pitch or the game does not hold; more money will not change that.

How the funnel will read, with numbers for the shape of it

Before day one: the checklist

  1. A Google Play developer account $25 once. The app registered, a signed release build uploaded to open testing. The current keystore is the one we keep forever: a Play app's signature cannot change.
  2. The store listing The hat icon, five screenshots with one-line captions (the horde, the level-up reveal, the portal, a boss, the Upgrade board), a 20-second trailer, a short description that says the genre in the first eight words ("A one-thumb survivor shooter in a desert that hates you"). Play's free store listing experiments can A/B the icon and the first screenshot during the test.
  3. A privacy policy page Covering PostHog, the EU region and the in-game switch, linked from the listing, and the Data safety form filled in.
  4. PostHog live Per the telemetry plan: at least app_open, run_start, run_end, player_died, revive_decided, and the new install_attributed from the Play referrer. Retention insights built before the first install lands.
  5. A Meta Business account The ad account funded, the Play app registered under it; the pixel is not needed. For Route B, AppsFlyer Zero connected to Meta and the attribution plugin in the build.
  6. The five videos Captured from the phone build (the capture tools in the repo give clean frames; the phone's own screen recorder gives the real thing), cut to 15 to 20 seconds, captions burnt in, 1080 × 1920.
  7. The budget cap set on the ad account So an unwatched weekend cannot spend the comfortable test's money on the minimum test's question.
