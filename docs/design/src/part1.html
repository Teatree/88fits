88fits / design bible

Drag to run. The guns aim themselves. A portrait, third-person survivor-shooter in the Megabonk mould: anime-styled heroes in a painted desert on 3D ground, a crowd of creatures on screen, level-ups that hand you three cards, chests that spin a reel, builds that grow mutations and evolve, structures and totems worth crossing the map for, and a meta that keeps you unlocking between runs.

version 0.88 pass 103 date 2026-10-24 status playable, iterated from the phone engine Godot 4.7.2
How to read this

This describes the game as it is built, not a plan. Every number is the number in the content files, every screenshot is a capture of the current build, and every creature and item pictured was painted by the art pipeline (section 15b).

Each section opens with what is true now. Where a rule changed, the old version and the reason sit in a folded How this got here block at the end of the section; open it when you want the story. A change from passes 80 to 103 is labelled with its pass number. The full log of every pass is docs/HISTORY.md.

01

Vision & pillars

The question this project answers: does a Megabonk-style run work in portrait with one thumb? The build says yes. What follows is the game that answer became, and the rules that keep it growing without turning into code.

The pitch in three sentences

You are a small goofy hero in a very big, very hot desert, and the desert hates you. Goblins, fire ants, skeletons, scorpions, mummies and beetles pour in from every side while your weapons fire on their own, so your only job is to steer through the mess, hoover up XP gems, reach the structures and totems on the map, and pick the best card every time you level up.

Each stage is its own run (pass 87): survive to its boss, then walk into the portal and bank your gold, or stay for Endless and see how long you last. Gold comes home whichever way the run ends.

One thumb, zero menus

Every in-run action is hold, slide, or tap. If a feature needs a second finger or a button cluster, it is cut or automated.

Portrait is the point

The tall screen shows more ahead than beside. Threat comes from the top of the screen most, and steering is the skill. We design around it, not despite it.

Cards go brr

Vampire Survivors stacking: five weapons, five cards, passive items without a cap, path mutations and evolutions. Runs feel different because builds feel different.

A crowd, not a queue

Dozens of creatures from the first minute and up to a few hundred late in Endless, every one of them a little different. The screen should look like a problem you can read.

Goofy and readable

Anime-style sprites for characters and scenery (crisp line art, cel shading, flat saturated colours), real 3D for weapons, pickups and the ground. Silly on purpose, clear at a glance on a phone.

Content is data

A new weapon, passive, creature, structure or pickup is a new folder, never new code, and it is visible and editable in the Godot editor and the Level Editor. See section 14.

What it is not

  • Not a parkour game. There is no jump button; the only jumps are the ones a perk throws the hero into (Fadeaway's Timeout trampoline).
  • Not an aiming game. No crosshair, no manual fire. Facing still matters for cone weapons and the Dragon's Breath, which gives steering a combat purpose.
  • Not a shop-between-waves game like Brotato. Progression inside a run is level-up cards, chests, mutations and whatever structures you reach.
02

Reference games

Each reference is named for one thing we borrow, so we can say "Archero-style cards" or "Megabonk-style portal" and both know exactly what is meant. When a design detail is open, we do what Megabonk and Vampire Survivors do and say so.

Megabonk

3D · third person · goofy

We take
Tone and humour. Stages ending in a boss and a portal. Characters and items unlocked by in-run challenges. A permanent upgrade board between runs. 3D pickups and weapon effects around a flat character. Landmarks worth a detour, and totems and shrines that hand out small boosts. Its sand.
We skip
Parkour, wall jumps, manual jumping, landscape framing.

Vampire Survivors

2D · the genre template

We take
Auto-firing weapons. Five weapon slots plus five card slots, and passive items without a cap (Megabonk's weapons, tomes and items). Weapons to level 5. An evolution for a maxed weapon, opened at a chest. XP gems and the magnet. Pick 1 of 3 on level up. Reroll, skip and banish. A pause screen that lists every item you hold. Chests that spin before they reveal.
We skip
The 30-minute timer, the arcana system, the sheer item count for now.

Archero

portrait · one thumb · mobile pacing

We take
Portrait HUD conventions. Full-width level-up cards sized for thumbs. Short chapters: a stage-run is 7 to 13 minutes with its boss at the end. A title screen that is one big PLAY button.
We skip
Stop-to-shoot. Rooms and doors. Equipment gacha.

Brotato

stat transparency

We take
A stats screen that shows every number with where it came from (the pause screen's Stats tab).
We skip
Shop between waves, discrete waves, top-down 2D.

Survivor.io

portrait Vampire Survivors

We take
Proof that the genre reads fine in portrait. The slim top strip with the timer and the item rows, the round pause button. The floating joystick and the camera that never rotates: the scheme that shipped.
We skip
Monetisation layers.

The one idea without a reference, hold-to-run, slide-to-steer, was the feel test's hypothesis. It lost to the joystick on the first phone session; section 04 records why.

03

The screen

The HUD is a thin strip over the world, Survivor.io's proportion: everything below it belongs to the thumb. Whatever must be read mid-fight sits either in that strip or on the ground next to the hero.

HUD anatomy

XP rail
A 65 px ink rail across the top edge with a purple fill (HUD.RAIL_H, the fill 11 px in). The level-up screen's rainbow rail is the same height. When a mutation offer is due, MUTATION NEXT sits on the rail's right end: the next level-up will be a mutation pick (pass 79, section 10).
top row
Paper plates: the LV badge, the clock in the middle and gold with its coin, and the round brass pause disc at the right end. The clock is the stage's own clock (pass 87): 0:00 at the landing, the stage's length at its boss. After the boss it counts the portal's 60 seconds down with ENDLESS IN under it, and in Endless it shows Endless's time with the wave number (WAVE 3). Tapping anywhere on the strip pauses.
slots
One row of ten equal 72 px slots: five weapons against the left edge, five cards against the right (WEAPON_SLOTS, LINE_SLOTS). Each held item is a see-through plate in its level's rarity colour with a level tag; a weapon shows a dot per mutation and glows when it is ready to evolve. Empty slots are dashed. Passive items are not on the HUD; the pause screen's Build tab lists them.
buffs
A row of plates under the slots while a timed effect runs (Berserk, Sprint, Shield, the Magnet, the War Drum), each with its seconds left.
boss
While a boss lives, its name (paper with an ink outline) and its bar sit under the slots.
banners and toasts
A banner pops in at 38% of the screen's height for 2 s (NUKE, SWARM!, PHOENIX FEATHER); toasts queue under the clock (PORTAL OPEN: extract before Endless).
on the hero
His health bar under his feet, drawn by the HUD at a fixed spot under him and scaled with the zoom; the Energy Shield's thin blue bar above it. When he is hurt the bar jiggles instead of the screen shaking (pass 82, HUD.shake_hp_bar). "+N XP" and "+N" gold rise from him, batched so a sweep through a gem field reads as one reward.
pointers
When the boss, an Elite or the portal is off screen, a chevron rides the screen edge pointing at it, with its name and distance ("BOSS 44 m", "PORTAL 45 m"): red for the boss, orange for an Elite, green for the portal. It disappears the moment the target is in view.
badges
A small painted icon floats over every creature that matters: a gold crown over the Elites, a white skull over a boss, a steel gear over the special types (the Vulture Raider, the Saiga Sniper, the Harpoon Troglodyte). It bobs, is drawn over the world so a cactus never hides it, and grows a little as the camera pulls out. Which one is data (badge; empty takes its role's); its height is measured from the painting (head).
thumb zone
Everything below the strip. Nothing interactive lives between y 700 and 1600 of the 1920 px canvas, and nothing is drawn there that needs reading.
safe areas
The HUD keeps clear of the camera cutout (W.safe_top()) and the gesture bar. Gameplay renders edge to edge.
How this got here (history)
  • The XP rail was 18 px, then 36 (pass 56, "twice as thick"), then 65 (pass 61, "1.8 times bigger"); everything under it moves down with it.
  • The slots were six weapons and six passives (72 px squares and circles), then six and six lines (pass 47), and five and five since pass 52, equal and see-through since pass 48, each group against its own edge since pass 53. Passive items left the HUD (Garry: "Only the Weapons and Line Items").
  • The health bar was once a camera-facing quad hung off the hero that swung round him as he turned; since v0.23.6 the HUD draws it at a fixed spot under his feet (CameraRig.hero_anchor()). Until pass 82 a hit shook the screen.
  • The pointers (v0.23.3) moved to the portal when the boss died and got one chevron each, so a portal and an Elite can both show. The badges (v0.23.3): an ordinary creature wearing an elite modifier once got a crown too; since v0.23.6 it never does.
  • BOSS IN 0:45 counted down under the strip while one run held all three stages; with a stage's boss at its end (pass 87) the clock itself says how long is left.
04

Controls

The whole game is one thumb on the lower part of the screen: a floating joystick, the scheme every portrait survivor game settled on (Archero, Survivor.io, Vampire Survivors mobile). Touch anywhere, the stick appears under the thumb, drag to run in that direction, lift to stop.

The rules of the thumb

touch down
The joystick base appears under the thumb, anywhere below the top strip. No fixed position to find, no acceleration ramp: the first frame of drag already moves. Touch is raw screen touch, never mouse emulation.
drag
Direction of the stick = direction of travel on screen (north is up, because the camera never turns). Dead zone 6 px. Throttle ramps to full speed at about half deflection, so a small nudge creeps through a gap and a full push sprints. The hero faces where he runs.
long drag
Past the rim the base trails the thumb, so a thumb that wandered keeps steering from where it is. Joystick size is a slider in Settings (0.6 to 1.4).
lift
Stop on the spot. Standing still is a legitimate tactic. Weapons keep firing.
auto-fire
Every weapon fires on its own cooldown and aims itself: the nearest creature, the strongest nearby, the thickest crowd, the nearest healthy creature for poison, or a cone at the nearest. Weapons take the nearest special, Elite or boss within 10 m first (pass 73, the map's target_priority). Ranged weapons fire only at what is on the screen (a metre of margin); homing ones still hunt off it. Every hit pushes: each weapon carries a knockback stat, and heavier creatures budge less.
stand to use
Structures and totems are used by standing inside their ring; a wedge fills on the ground and drains if you walk out. Chests and pickups are used by walking over them.
tap a card
Level-up, mutation, the chest's Take, Skip or Banish, the shop's three items, a totem's gifts. Big targets, no drag, no confirm step. After a pick the game stays paused until the next tap ("TAP TO GO"), and the hero is immune for a moment after that tap, so a card is never claimed straight into a bite; the tap that resumes can be the thumb landing to move.
pause
The round brass disc at the right end of the HUD strip (tapping anywhere on the strip works too): the build, the stats and the options (section 12). The run also pauses when the phone sends the app to the background. It does not pause on Android's back gesture: a thumb steering near the edge made one by accident. Settings lists the last system events that paused a run or could have.
keyboard, PC only
WASD or the arrow keys move the hero like a full stick push; the four actions (move_left/right/up/down) live in the project's Input Map. Escape pauses or closes an overlay. Phones never see them.
reserved
Double-tap dash and a second-thumb ability button are ideas for later.
What the feel test found

The first scheme was hold-to-run, slide-to-steer, with the camera behind the hero. Turning the character was a chore, not a decision: the thumb fought the rotating camera and every dodge needed a two-step steer. The joystick makes a dodge one motion, the way the benchmark games do it, and leaves the decisions to positioning, which is where this genre keeps them.

How this got here (history)
  • Auto-jump at marked ledges and jump pads belonged to Dry Gulch's mesas; Red Canyon has none, so it is gone with them.
  • The back gesture paused the run until v0.23.1; it read as the game pausing at random.
  • On-screen fire (v0.40): ranged weapons used to shoot at whatever was in range, off the screen too. A Debug switch turns the rule off. Pass 95 added a debug switch, Settings.fire_forward, for the Sabre, Spear, Hammer, Frisbee, Tumbleweed and Basketball: fire where the hero faces until a creature is within 75% of range.
05

Camera

A fixed camera, north always up, the way Archero and Survivor.io frame the fight: the world never rotates, so the stick maps straight to the screen and the map stays learnable. It is bolted to the hero (no lead, no lag) and only big blasts and slams shake it; the hero's own hurt jiggles his health bar instead (pass 82).

fixed (default)
Yaw locked to north, the focus 3.3 m north of the hero, so he stands a little below the screen centre and sees more of what is coming. The hero's sprite switches between back, back-diagonal, side, front-diagonal and front views from the direction of travel, diagonals mirrored: eight directions in all.
follow (option)
Yaw follows the hero's facing with a short lag and a dead zone (pitch 50°, 21.5 m, FOV 60°); the hero is seen from behind. Switch in Settings at any time, even mid-run. Not the default.
zoom
The camera pulls out as the hero runs faster and as he levels: speed adds the log of how many walking speeds he moves at over log 4, level adds 2% a level past the first (zoom_per_level), and 70% of that pull is used (zoom_amount 0.7), so the widest view is 2.12 times the base distance (zoom_max 2.6). Eased, so a sprint start does not jump.
far zoom
Past twice its distance every creature gets a dark-red rim and a faint warm tint, so the crowd reads when everyone is a speck (section 07).
aspect
Keep-height projection: every phone sees the same distance ahead; wider phones see a bit more to the sides.
terrain
The camera rides the terrain height under the hero, smoothed, so dunes and the riverbed do not bounce the view.
readability
Creatures far up the screen are small. The Elites are about three times a goblin's height and the bosses five, so they read at distance; the edge pointers cover the rest.
How this got here (history)

Since v0.17 the distance follows the hero's speed; v0.23.7 took 30% of the pull-out away (the widest view had been 2.6 times the base). The chase camera of the feel test is kept as "Follow".

06

Red Canyon

One map, a golden-hour desert. The ground is real 3D terrain; almost everything standing on it is a painted 2.5D billboard in the cast's anime line. The layout rolls per run from the run's seed, so the arena is always the same shape with a different desert in it.

What is on the map

the ground
Gentle noise dunes, one dry river with cracked earth that enters and leaves somewhere new each run, a flat centre for the bosses and the portal, and a calm painted sand. Five sands exist (map key sand: pass81, dunes, dunes_pebbles, ripples, ripples_pebbles; pass 82); Red Canyon plays ripples_pebbles (pass 83).
oases
Two or three a run (pass 80, oases: min 2, max 3, a third 35% of the time, 10 m apart). The water is a cheap water shader with damp earth and green round it (pass 81), fan palms at the shore. Everyone can wade in, and water slows walking by 10% (water_slow), hero and creatures alike.
the edge
Sand runs right up to a western sandstone fort wall (pass 80, CanyonWalls.gd: battlements, posts, seamless painted stone) with the red-rock cliffs behind it; an invisible fence on the wall's inner face stops the hero at the stone. The rim and the wall take their colours from the map (rock_color, wall_colors).
scenery
Twenty painted plant, rock and bone kinds (art/sprites/props/props.json: saguaros, barrel cactus, agave, ocotillo, fan palms, Joshua trees, mesquite, palo verde, bushes, rocks, skulls, bones...), placed by region: palms and green round the water, cactus and tree stands, bush patches, dead trees and shrubs on the river's banks with bones in its bed, rock fields near the rim. Many kinds have painted variants (pass 83, variant_of, picked by a hash of the spot) and a natural random tint per spot (pass 85, tint). Every prop, structure, barrel and sign stands on a ground shadow (pass 83). Cacti, trees and rocks collide through a cylinder smaller than the painting, so the hero can stand behind one and be read through it; bushes and bones do not collide.
structures
Nine landmarks drawn by weight, at least 22 m apart (the map's structures), plus 3 or 4 Teleports (4 a third of the time, 40 m apart, landing 15 m clear of any; pass 85) and 10 to 12 Totem Poles (pass 73) placed on their own. All are paintings with a spent painting swapped in once used. Section 07 has what each does.
barrels and signs
Wooden barrels stand about the map and burst into XP gems when walked into; black ones hold 1 to 5 coins (pass 87). Four to six weathered signposts point their arrow boards at the nearest structures not used yet. Both are 3D.
moving bits
Tumbleweeds stay 3D because they roll; the dust devil is a 3D cone of sand that roams the flats and throws creatures and hero alike; gusts of dust sweep across the sand now and then (a look, nothing else). The greenery sways in the vertex shader.

Stage looks

Each of the three stages has its own look over the same map (pass 87, StageLook, the stage's filter in stage_list): Sundown plays the plain golden hour, Nightfall lays a blue filter over the world (#3D55C8 at 0.48), and Blood Moon a dark red one (#6A1A28 at 0.6) with hell: the sand turns red and the hell crowd comes on top of the regular one. The look is set before the hero's spawn beam lands, so the landing is already in the stage's light (pass 100).

Theme rules

time of day
Golden hour in stage 1: warm orange light from one side, long soft shadows baked into the paintings, a purple sky at the top. It flatters painted sprites and hides low polygon counts. The later stages darken it with their filters.
scale joke
The hero is small. Cacti are twice his height and the skull is a bench. Oversized props sell the goofy tone and the third-person camera.
lighting budget
One directional light, no real-time shadows on mobile, ambient from the sky colour, a painted ground shadow under every figure and prop. The portal gets a single omni light; nothing else does on a phone.

Blender's role

  • The movers and a few solid things. Tumbleweeds, barrels and signposts are Blender meshes (tools/blender_props.py, blender_barrel.py, blender_sign.py); the structures and the scenery are paintings.
  • Weapon meshes. Saw blades, guns, grenades and dynamite, the turret, the basketball, the salt shaker, the gem, the coin, the portal ring, mostly built from Garry's reference models in _REFERENCES/.
  • References and drivers. Characters and creatures are 2D painted sprites. Blender renders their pose skeletons, depth drivers and icon references (section 15b).
How this got here (history)
  • v0.11: Red Canyon replaced Dry Gulch (mesas, ramps, a gas station and a water tower), which is shelved with an earlier meadow map; their files are backed up for a content pack.
  • v0.18: the layout began rolling per run; the fan palm, the ocotillo and the yucca joined. v0.35: the yucca and the signpost prop were hidden ("hidden": true in props.json). v0.40 to v0.47: the sand and the river were lightened and made less orange several times, bush patches came (5 to 7 a run), signposts arrived (v0.40, with name tags; v0.41 "just let them be pointers") and grew 1.4 times taller.
  • The lake: one small oasis from v0.18, wadeable since v0.19, a flat half-transparent disc since v0.21 (a wavy shader read as a patterned lid); the hero wades as deep as a creature since v0.42. Pass 80 made two or three of them and slowed walking in them; pass 81 gave them the water shader and the damp shore.
  • The boundary: a stacked-stone wall with battlements (v0.15) in front of a ring of noise-height cliffs; pass 80 replaced it with the sandstone fort wall in the west and real red rock behind (Garry: "make the edges of the map just sand ... Make the walls look like sandstone castle walls (but western)").
  • The sand: pass 81 made it calmer and Megabonk-like; pass 82 painted four more from Garry's reference; Garry chose ripples with pebbles in pass 83 ("Sand - I am choosing Rip+P").
