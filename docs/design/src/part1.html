88fits / design bible

Drag to run. The guns aim themselves. A portrait, third-person survivor-shooter in the Megabonk mould: clean anime-styled heroes in a painted desert on 3D ground, a few dozen creatures on screen, level-ups that hand you three cards, chests that spin a reel, poison and fire builds that never mix, structures worth crossing the map for, and a meta that keeps you unlocking between runs.

version 0.17 date 2026-09-21 status playable, iterated from the phone engine Godot 4.7.2
How to read this

This is a description of the game as it is built, not a plan. Every number is the number in the content files, every screenshot is a capture from the current build, and every creature and item pictured was painted by the art pipeline in section 15. Where a rule was tried and dropped, the section says so in one line and moves on. 01

Vision & pillars

The question this project answers: does a Megabonk-style run work in portrait with one thumb? The build says yes. What follows is the game that answer became, and the rules that keep it growing without turning into code.

The pitch in three sentences

You are a small goofy hero in a very big, very hot desert, and the desert hates you. Goblins, fire ants, spitting cacti, mummies and beetles pour in from every side while your weapons fire on their own, so your only job is to steer through the mess, hoover up XP gems, find the structures scattered over the map, and pick the best card every time you level up. Survive the boss, walk into the portal, and decide whether to bank your gold or push into a harder stage.

One thumb, zero menus

Every in-run action is hold, slide, or tap. If a feature needs a second finger or a button cluster, it is cut or automated.

Portrait is the point

The tall screen shows more ahead than beside. Threat comes from the front and steering is the skill. We design around it, not despite it.

Cards go brr

Vampire Survivors weapon stacking: five weapons, five lines, passive items, evolutions. Runs feel different because builds feel different.

A crowd, not a queue

Two dozen creatures on screen from minute one, sixty by the end, every one of them a little different. The screen should look like a problem you can read.

Goofy and readable

Anime-style sprites for characters (crisp line art, cel shading, flat saturated colours), real 3D for weapons, pickups and the world. Silly on purpose, clear at a glance on a phone.

Content is data

A new weapon, passive, creature, structure or pickup is a new folder, never new code, and it is visible and editable inside the Godot editor. See section 14.

What it is not

  • Not a parkour game. Jumps happen automatically at marked ledges and pads. There is no jump button.
  • Not an aiming game. No crosshair, no manual fire. Facing still matters for cone weapons, which gives steering a combat purpose.
  • Not a shop-between-waves game like Brotato. Progression inside a run is the starter pick, level-up cards, chests, and whatever structures you reach.
02

Reference games

Each reference is named for one thing we borrow, so later we can say "Archero-style cards" or "Megabonk-style portal" and both know exactly what is meant.

Megabonk

3D · third person · goofy

We take
Tone and humour. Stages ending in a boss and a portal. Extract-or-continue tension. Characters and items unlocked by completing in-run challenges. Permanent upgrade shop between runs. 3D pickups and weapon effects around a flat character. Landmarks on the map that are worth a detour.
We skip
Parkour, wall jumps, manual jumping, landscape framing.

Vampire Survivors

2D · the genre template

We take
Auto-firing weapons. Five weapon slots plus five line slots (v0.38; six and six from v0.33), and passive items without a cap (Megabonk's weapons, tomes and items). Weapon levels to 8. Evolution by pairing a max weapon with its passive. XP gems and magnet. Pick 1 of 3 on level up. Reroll, skip and banish as meta unlocks. The pause screen that lists every item you hold. Chests that spin before they reveal.
We skip
The 30-minute timer, the arcana system, the sheer item count for now.

Archero

portrait · one thumb · mobile pacing

We take
Portrait HUD conventions. Full-screen level-up cards sized for thumbs. Chapter pacing where a run is 10 to 15 minutes with bosses on a schedule. A title screen that is one big PLAY button.
We skip
Stop-to-shoot. Rooms and doors. Equipment gacha.

Brotato

starter choice · stat transparency

We take
Picking a starting weapon before the run. A stats screen that shows every number with where it came from.
We skip
Shop between waves, discrete waves, top-down 2D.

Survivor.io

portrait Vampire Survivors

We take
Proof that the genre reads fine in portrait. The slim top strip with timer and item rows, the round pause button, the navy-and-gold look of the menus. The floating joystick and the camera that never rotates: the scheme that shipped.
We skip
Monetisation layers.

The one idea without a reference, hold-to-run, slide-to-steer, was the feel test's hypothesis. It lost to the joystick on the first phone session and section 04 records why.

03

The screen

The HUD is a thin strip over the world, Survivor.io's proportion: everything below it belongs to the thumb. Whatever must be read mid-fight sits either in that strip or on the ground next to the hero.

HUD anatomy

v0.47 · the XP bar 1.8 times thicker

The XP rail along the top edge is 65 px (36 since v0.42; HUD.RAIL_H, the fill 11 px in from each edge), and everything under it follows: the plates at 77, the slots at 173, the band 267 px. The level-up screen's rainbow rail is the same height and still runs its colours along it.

top strip
250 px of 1920, 13% of the screen. Row one: LV badge, XP bar, stage name. Row two: kills and alive count left, the timer big in the middle, gold right. Row three: the twelve item slots. A gradient that fades into the world, no panel, every piece of text outlined so it reads over sand and sky. Tapping the strip pauses.
slots
Six weapon squares on the left, six passive circles on the right, 72 px each, a level badge in the corner that turns gold at max level. Empty slots are faint outlines. Same icon files as the cards and the pause screen.
pause
A round gold button at the right end of the strip, where Survivor.io keeps it. Two bars drawn as shapes, so no font can break it.
buffs
A row of chips under the strip while a timed effect runs (Berserk, Sprint, Shield, the War Drum), each with its seconds left.
on the player
Health bar under the character. v0.23.6: it used to be a camera-facing quad in the world hung half a metre off the hero, and since the hero turns with his facing, the bar swung round him (above him when he faced down the screen, off to the side when he ran sideways). Now the HUD draws it, at a fixed spot on the screen under the hero's feet: the camera is bolted to him north-up, so his place on the screen only moves as the camera zooms out, and CameraRig.hero_anchor() projects his feet through the camera without its shake and says how many pixels a metre spans there. The bar is 1.5 m of that wide, so it shrinks with the zoom exactly as the hero does; the Energy Shield's thinner blue bar sits right above it. Both disappear when the hero is down.
XP
Gems collected within a quarter second are batched into one rising "+N XP" label over the hero and the bar pops, so a sweep through a gem field reads as one reward, not forty.
boss
BOSS IN 0:45 counts down just under the strip in the last 45 s (banner at 30, pulse under 10); the boss name and bar take the same spot while a boss is alive.
where the big one is
Whenever the boss or the Elite is alive and off screen, a chevron at the screen edge points at it with its name and distance ("BOSS 42 m"), red for the boss, orange for the Elite. It disappears the moment the creature is in view, so it never covers a fight. v0.23.3: when the boss dies its marker stays on the portal it leaves behind, in green ("PORTAL 38 m"), until the hero walks into it; and each target gets its own chevron, so a portal and an Elite both show at once.
badges
v0.23.3. A small icon floats over the head of every creature that matters: a gold crown over the two Elites, the Dune Scorpion and the Sun Prophet, and over nothing else (v0.23.6: an ordinary creature wearing an elite modifier used to get one too; whatever its stats or colour, it never does now), a white skull over a boss, a steel gear over the specials, the Harpoon Troglodyte and the Rocketeer. Each is a painted icon with a thick outline and a soft halo in its colour, bobbing and rocking a little, drawn over the world so a cactus never hides it and a little bigger as the camera pulls out. Which one a creature wears is data (badge in its JSON; empty takes its role's), and the height it hangs at is measured from its painting (head, tools/measure_heads.py).
thumb zone
Everything below the strip, 86% of the screen. A faint ring appears where the thumb lands and fades after a second. Nothing is ever drawn here that needs reading.
safe areas
HUD respects the notch and gesture bar insets from the OS. Gameplay renders edge to edge.
04

Controls

The whole game is one thumb on the lower two thirds of the screen: a floating joystick, the scheme every portrait survivor game settled on (Archero, Survivor.io, Vampire Survivors mobile). Touch anywhere, the stick appears under the thumb, drag to run in that direction, lift to stop.

The rules of the thumb

touch down
The joystick base appears under the thumb, anywhere below the top bar. No fixed position to find, no acceleration ramp: the first frame of drag already moves.
drag
Direction of the stick = direction of travel on screen (north is up, because the camera never turns). Dead zone 6 px. Throttle ramps to full speed at about half deflection, so a small nudge creeps through a gap and a full push sprints. The hero faces where they run.
long drag
Past the rim the base trails the thumb, so a thumb that wandered keeps steering from where it is. Joystick size is a slider in settings (0.6 to 1.4).
lift
Stop on the spot. Standing still is a legitimate tactic. Weapons keep firing.
auto-fire
Every weapon fires on its own cooldown and aims itself: nearest, random, the thickest crowd, the nearest healthy creature for poison, or a cone pointed at the nearest creature (the shotgun family), so shots always leave towards something. The facing only decides where the held gun points when nothing is in range. Every hit pushes: each weapon carries a knockback stat (metres per second of shove on a 1 m creature; heavier creatures budge less, the boss barely).
stand to use
Structures are used by standing inside their ring for three seconds; the progress fills on the ground. Walking out drains it. Chests and pickups are used by walking over them.
auto-jump
Running off a marked ledge or onto a jump pad triggers a fixed arc. The player cannot fall off the map. Raised paths hold chests and shortcuts.
tap a card
Level-up, starter choice, the chest's Take, Skip or Banish, the shop's three items. Big targets, no drag, no confirm step. After a level-up pick or the reel the game stays paused until the next tap ("TAP TO CONTINUE"), and the hero is immune for one second after that tap, blinking, so a card is never claimed straight into a bite; the tap that resumes can be the thumb landing to move.
pause
The round gold button at the right end of the HUD strip (tapping the strip works too). Full build and stats readout, options, abandon run. See section 12. The run also pauses itself when the phone sends the app to the background. It does not pause on Android's back gesture (v0.23.1): that gesture is a swipe in from a side edge, and a thumb steering near the edge made one by accident, which read as the game pausing at random. Settings lists the last six system events that paused a run or could have ("back gesture, ignored", "the app went to the background"), so a pause nobody asked for can be traced from the phone.
keyboard, PC only
WASD or the arrow keys move the hero exactly like a full stick push; no ring is drawn. The four actions (move_left/right/up/down) live in the project's Input Map, so they can be rebound in the editor without code. Escape pauses, or closes the shop. Phones never see them.
reserved
Double-tap dash and a second-thumb ability button are ideas for later.
What the feel test found

The first scheme was hold-to-run, slide-to-steer, with the camera behind the hero. Turning the character was a chore, not a decision: the thumb fought the rotating camera and every dodge needed a two-step steer. The joystick makes a dodge one motion, the way the benchmark games do it, and leaves the decisions to positioning, which is where this genre keeps them.

05

Camera

A fixed camera, north always up, the way Archero and Survivor.io frame the fight: the world never rotates, so the stick maps straight to the screen and the map stays learnable. It is bolted to the hero: no lead, no lag, so the stick never feels like it is also steering the view. Only hits shake it. Since v0.17 the distance follows the hero's speed: at walking pace it is the 22.5 m above; faster, it backs off by the log of the ratio, doubling at four times walking speed and never more (CameraRig.zoom_max, zoom_at_ratio), eased so a sprint start does not jump.

fixed (default)
Yaw locked to north, position locked to a point north of the hero, so they stand a little below the screen centre and see more of what is coming. Screen shake on every hit the hero takes (and on evolutions, big explosions and boss slams). The hero sprite switches between back, back-diagonal, side, front-diagonal and front views from the direction of travel, diagonals mirrored, eight directions in all.
follow (option)
Yaw follows the hero's facing with a short lag and a dead zone so turns feel weighty; the hero is seen from behind. Switch in Options at any time, even mid-run.
aspect
Keep-height projection. Every phone sees the same distance ahead. Wider phones see a bit more to the sides.
terrain
The camera rides the terrain height under the player, smoothed, so dunes and the riverbed do not bounce the view. Mesa tops raise the camera with the player.
zoom
The camera pulls out as the hero runs faster and as he levels (speed: 1 + log of how many walking speeds he moves at, over log 4; level: 2% a level past the first, at most 70%), so a fast, strong build sees a wider fight. v0.23.7: the pull-out was too extreme, so 30% of it goes (CameraRig.zoom_amount 0.7): the widest view is 2.12 times the base distance instead of 2.6.
readability
Creatures far up the screen are small. The Elite is three times a goblin's height and the boss five, so they read at distance; the edge pointer covers the rest.
06

Red Canyon

One map, the golden-hour desert, rebuilt in v0.11 as Red Canyon: nothing in it is a 3D object except the ground and the mountain wall. The ground is not a plane: gentle dunes, one sunken dry river with cracked earth, one small oasis lake, a flat centre for the bosses, and a rim that climbs into broken red rock towards the cliffs. Since v0.18 the layout rolls per run: the dunes take a fresh noise seed, the river enters and leaves the rim somewhere new and wanders between with a middle that bends around the flats, and the lake lands somewhere off both; the wall, the flats and the rim never move, so the arena is always the same shape with a different desert in it. Everything standing on it is painted: cacti, trees, bushes, rocks, skulls and bones, the seven structures, the tumbleweeds and the dust devil are 2.5D sprites in the same anime line as the cast. They go down by region now, like with like: fan palms and green around the water, three green stands elsewhere (a cactus stand or a tree stand with the low plants around it), dead trees and shrubs on the river's banks with bones in its bed, rock fields near the rim with skulls among the stones, and a few lone bushes in the open. Three plants joined in v0.18 (the fan palm, the ocotillo and the yucca), and bushes have no collider: you walk through them. v0.35: the yucca and the signpost are kept out of the desert ("hidden": true in art/sprites/props/props.json; the paintings stay, a deleted line brings either back), the other plants filling their share of the stands; and the Uber Magnet is painted again as a plain red horseshoe on its plinth with nothing on it that looks as if it ought to move (its v0.34 painting had lost the corners of its silver caps to the blue screen's keyer; it is keyed off green now, and a plinth keeps its shaded corner). Dry Gulch, with its mesas, ramps, gas station and water tower, is shelved with the meadow. A second map (a meadow) was built in an earlier pass and has been shelved to keep the focus on making one map excellent; its files are backed up and it will come back as a content pack.

v0.47 · paler sand, a sand-coloured river, bush patches, taller signs (Garry's sixteenth list)

The sand is lighter again (the texture's palette (214,176,130) to (250,228,182), the shader's flat colour (0.90, 0.78, 0.58)), and the dry river is no longer the old brown: it is the sand's own colour a step browner ((176,142,104) to (230,198,154), cracks (158,122,86), flat (0.80, 0.67, 0.50)); its shape, banks and dish are exactly as they were. A new region, bush patches: 5 to 7 a run (more than the three cactus or tree stands), each 4 to 6 desert bushes, creosote and dead shrubs spread at least 2.5 m apart over 7 m of open sand, placed last so every other region lands where it did; no bush has a collider, and structures keep 3 m clear of them (RedCanyon.bush_patches(), StructureManager.BUSH_CLEAR). The signposts are 1.4 times taller (SignField.STRETCH; post and boards stretched upwards, the boards' lean kept at 8 degrees, colliders and the depth sorting unchanged). The launcher icon's sand is a touch lighter too (tools/icon_sand.py: only the sand, the hat untouched).

v0.40 · lighter sand, signposts, water that reads (Garry's eleventh list)

The sand and the dry river are a little lighter and less orange: the sand texture's saturation went from 0.50 to 0.42 and the river bed's from 0.63 to 0.56, both a touch brighter (tools/gen_textures.py, with the shader's flat colours to match; the red rock is untouched). Four to six signposts stand round the map by the run's seed, at least 25 m apart: a weathered post, thin, with a collider the crowd steers round, and one to four arrow boards, each pointing at one of the nearest structures not used yet. Its name hangs on a tag at the board's point that always faces the camera (TELEPORT, MAGNET, BANK, STRANGER, WELL, CACHE, SHOP, DRUM, BEACON), because from the run's camera a board pointing up or down the screen is seen edge on; two boards of one sign point at least 35 degrees apart, the tags are lifted clear of each other, and a structure once used blanks its board (core/systems/SignField.gd, tools/blender_sign.py). The oasis: creatures splash as they wade, like the hero, the ripples sit on the water instead of the lake bed under it, and no shadow shows under anyone standing in the water. The agave no longer blocks the way.

v0.41 · signs that only point

The signposts carry no names now (Garry: "just let them be pointers"): each arrow board still points at one of the nearest structures not used yet, and goes a shade paler once that structure is used. The name tags are kept in the code, switched off, for the day they come back.

v0.42 · the hero wades in

The hero sank into the oasis no deeper than his boots: the second drawing of his painting (the one that keeps his speed trails under him, v0.35) was drawn after the water and so painted his whole figure over it. That pass now stops where the water covers him, so he wades in exactly as deep as a creature beside him (Garry: "Should be submerged like all the enemies"). His shadow, and every creature's, also hides wherever the drawn water covers it (a dark plate showed through at the shore), and his see-through outline stops at the water too. It changes nothing in play.

Theme rules

time of day
Golden hour, always. Warm orange light from one side, long soft shadows baked into props, purple sky at the top. It flatters painted sprites and hides low polygon counts.
skybox
A panorama: gradient from dusk purple to orange, a big low sun, a far ring of mesa silhouettes, three lazy clouds. The sun is also the directional light.
ground
A 140 m heightmap terrain, not a plane. Dunes of about ±1.5 m, two riverbeds 2 m lower with a cracked-earth texture, sand elsewhere, and from 44 m out the ground rises two metres into broken red rock. Three textures blended by the terrain's vertex colour (sand, cracked earth, red rock), low-frequency path noise so the blend never looks like camouflage.
walls
A mountain wall: one ring mesh of cliff faces whose height wanders with noise, stone-textured, with two more ranges behind it, taller and darker, so the horizon has depth. An invisible fence a little inside it keeps the hero off the cliffs.
scenery
Twenty-one painted props (art/sprites/props/): saguaros big and small, barrel cactus, prickly pear, agave, dead shrub, dead tree, mesquite, joshua tree and palo verde, a desert bush and a creosote bush, three sizes of sandstone rock, a cow skull, a small skull, a bone pile and a signpost. Sixteen cactus clusters, fourteen trees with bushes round them, twelve lone bushes, ten rock groups, twenty-two bones and three signposts, never on the flats or a river. Cacti, trees, rocks and signs collide (a cylinder under each); bushes and bones do not. Colliders are deliberately smaller than the paintings (a big rock's is 0.6 m, a saguaro's 0.35 m), so the hero can stand behind a prop and be read through it.
the boundary
A stacked-stone wall right on the arena's edge (v0.15): a ring 1.7 m thick and about 3 m tall with battlements (every other block a step higher), both faces, the cap and the steps in one vertex-coloured mesh, boulders along the cap, four-metre posts every ten degrees and a skull on every fourth post. The invisible fence sits on the wall's inner face, so the hero stops at the stone. The mountains behind it stay; the wall is the line itself, so the hero stops at a thing and not at nothing.
moving bits
Tumbleweeds are the one thing that stayed 3D on purpose: a modelled ball that rolls with the wind, bounces off things and rides the terrain (a painted ball spinning on a billboard read wrong as soon as it turned). The dust devil is a 3D cone of sand for the same reason: it roams and throws creatures and hero alike. The greenery sways: each plant's top leans a few degrees back and forth in the vertex shader, the base stays put.
scale joke
The hero is small. Cacti are twice their height, the pickup is a building, the skull is a bench. Oversized props sell the goofy tone and the third-person camera.
lighting budget
One directional light, no real-time shadows on mobile, ambient from the sky colour, a fake blob shadow under every character. The portal gets a single omni light; nothing else does on a phone.

Blender's role

  • Only the movers, in Red Canyon. The structures are painted (st_<id>.png, drawn as billboards on their ring, with a spent painting st_<id>_off.png swapped in once used); the tumbleweeds and the dust devil stay 3D because they move. The Blender prop set (tools/blender_props.py) stays in the repo for the record and for a map that wants it.
  • Weapon meshes. Saw blades, the shotgun, grenades, the turret, the pea pod and the dart familiars, the gem, the coin, the chest, the portal ring. Small, chunky, built in the scene files.
  • Not Blender. Characters and creatures are 2D painted sprites. Blender only ever renders their pose skeletons (section 15).
