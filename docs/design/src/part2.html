The run

A run is 15 minutes in three 5-minute stages. The hero lands in a beam of light with their own weapon in hand. Each stage ends with a boss and a portal; halfway to each boss an Elite shows up. Walk through the portal to bank your gold with a bonus, or stay and face a harder stage. Death also ends the run and keeps what you earned.

v0.40 · getting hit, harder bites, where they come from

Every creature hits 25 % harder (creature_damage_mult 1.25 in the map's timeline: bites, shots, spit, rockets, blasts, every boss and caster attack and the Brander's ring). A hit now throws the hero back, away from what hit him, by that creature's own knockback number, set from its damage (skeleton 2.9 m/s, knight 3.4, fire ant 4.8, mummy 6.4, beetle 6.8, bosses 7: about 35 % over the old flat 2.5 for a melee creature; bosses and Elites shove 1.6 times as hard; the dwarf's shotgun and the rocketeer's rocket keep their own). The stick goes quiet for 0.12 s, the screen's edges flash red, the game stops for 45 ms and sand puffs at his feet; a shield's hit throws him too. Ranged weapons fire only at what is on the screen (a metre of margin); homing ones (the Rocket Pod, Killer Bees, the Tarantula Hawk, the seekers) still hunt off it, and a switch in Settings' Debug panel turns the rule off. And the crowd comes from the top of the screen most: 55 % from the top, 15 % from each side and from the bottom (the map's spawn_sides; the old rule, mostly ahead of where the hero faces, is kept as a mode).

Run rules

the landing
Before the timer starts, a 3 s beam of light: the hero comes down it (tap after 1 s to skip), lands in a ring of dust, the HUD fades in, the clock starts. See section 11. Slot 1 holds the hero's own weapon (start_weapon: Astro's Gravity Gun, the Drifter's Revolver, Calavera's Guitarra) at level 1.
length
About 21 minutes for a full clear since v0.32 (it was 15, benchmarked on Archero's early chapters and Survivor.io stages): Garry asked for progression at 0.7 times the pace, Megabonk's ten-minute stages in mind, so every clock of the map was stretched by 430/300 (each stage 7:10; the crowd, the arrivals, the Elites, the bosses and the mutation slots all come later) and every level needs twice the XP (xp_need_mult 2.0): the kills are limited by the spawns, not the cap, so a longer stage pays more XP, and twice the XP a level brings the hero to the first boss at about level 22 after 7:10, where v0.31 reached 21 after 5:00.
stages
Three per map. The stage timer drives the wave director. Each stage raises the difficulty multiplier, the live cap and creature speed. Stage 2 should feel like a wall to a build that coasted through stage 1. Stage 3 is hell (v0.29): the sand goes a shade redder, the hell crowd (imps, hellhounds, hammer giants; hobgoblins from stage 2) comes on top of the regular roster from a cap of its own (40% more bodies, 30% on stage 2), the stats stand at 20.6 of stage 2's so the stage is twice as hard with 60% of it on the stats and 40% on the count, two items (Brimstone Brand, Sinner's Greed) join the offers there and nowhere else, and the Brander waits at 15:00.
the Elite
Halfway to every boss (3:35, 10:45, 17:55 since v0.32; it was 2:30, 7:30, 12:30; and half a stage after a boss in endless) a mini-boss appears near the hero with nothing but a toast: no bar, no phase change, regular spawning carries on. On Dry Gulch it is the Dune Scorpion: a charger three times a goblin's height with 260 HP that scales ×1.8 per stage. It always drops a chest, and the HUD points at it while it lives.
bosses
Three of them. The Tumbleweed King closes stage 1; Gila Grande closes stage 2 and pays for the trip: two chests and thirty coins where the King pays one and twelve, so continuing through the portal is worth more than extracting (v0.29: its seven-line fan is a rumble, a wave of heaving sand running down every line that throws whoever it passes, instead of seven lobbed spits that each hit one point); The Brander (v0.29) closes stage 3 with a trick no data field could say: a ring of fire round the fight that closes in from 21 m to 8 over forty seconds and burns whoever stands outside it, hero or creature, so the crowd and the hero end up in one small circle; its iron leaves burning lines on the sand for nine seconds; a charge, a slam, rings of imps; three chests and forty coins. At the end of every stage, on the flats. Big silhouette, three telegraphed attacks, a bar under the HUD strip, and a chest where it dies. The moment it dies the reward comes to you: every gem, coin and chest on the map flies to the hero (BOSS DOWN), so the end of a stage is a shower, not a walk. Regular spawning slows to a trickle while the boss lives.
portal
Appears where the boss dies. Extract banks the run's gold and ends the run. Continue starts the next stage. After stage 3's boss the second road is Stay: hell keeps coming, ×1.12 tougher every wave (a minute), compounding, with a boss every five minutes (the King, the Gila, the Brander in turn) whose portal is the next chance to extract. (Until v0.29 extracting paid ×1.25 and the Continue card said the gold stayed at ×1.0; both are gone: gold is gold.) v0.34 (Garry's sixth list): the portal waits for you. When the boss dies one small wave comes (twelve, in packs from all round, portal_wave) and then nothing spawns until you walk through; you are no longer fed creatures while you make up your mind. Walking in opens the choice, and Not yet (or back) closes it without choosing: the portal asks again once you have stepped out and walked back in.
down
The hero falls, the death plays for a moment, then the game pauses behind the revive screen: a count of eight seconds, Watch an ad · free revive (no ads yet, so it is simply free), Revive · 150 gold (a flat price out of the run's gold, greyed when you cannot pay), and Give up. Nothing chosen when the count runs out is giving up. One chance per run, plus one per Revive tier from the shop; the hero comes back at half health with two seconds of grace and everything near him shoved away. A dim dbg revive button in the corner revives for free every time, for testing.
death
Ends the run. The run's gold stays in the sand (v0.30): only an extraction banks it, and only what a Strongbox put away on the way survives a death. The revive screen says so under the count, and the results screen shows the lost sum struck through next to what the Strongbox saved. The hero lies still and the health bar goes away.
barrels
Forty-odd wooden barrels (v0.30) stand about the map, placed by the run's seed a few metres apart, clear of the rocks, the water and the structures, and never behind a painting (the camera looks north from above, so each painting covers the ground just north of its foot for about its height, and no barrel goes there). Walk up to one and it bursts into splinters and dust and spills a handful of XP gems, more the deeper the run; the light ones are rarer and hold three times as much. They are the one thing on the map that rewards wandering.
wind
Gusts of dust sweep across the sand every ten seconds or so (v0.30), streaks along the wind with sand kicked up where they pass: a look and nothing else. The tumbleweeds are hidden since the same pass, their code kept.
spawning
Continuous, not discrete waves. A live cap grows with time. When the cap has room, the director spawns from the current roster on a ring outside the view, mostly ahead of the hero, in bursts of 2 to 6 packs so groups arrive as groups.

Chests and the reel

v0.32: the chest opens before the reel spins, after Garry's animated-chest reference: a painted walnut-and-brass chest (violet and gold with a skull lock for the boss's) drops in, jiggles three times with a knock on the lid, cracks open with light leaking out, throws its lid back in a burst of light, and sinks away as the reel spins up out of it (WChest, about a second; a tap skips to the spin). Coins everywhere in the game are a painted coin now (WCoin). v0.33: the chest is repainted in the icons' style (thick black lines, flat colours, warm gold light instead of the busy wood grain) and it opens on the lower part of the screen, its light rising into the reel; the coin is a third plainer (no faceted star, no shine, no notched rim).

who drops them
Every elite variant and the Elite always. Every boss. Regular creatures rarely, by a chest chance in their drop table: a goblin one time in 360, a mummy one in 70, a beetle one in 48, so a full run sees three or four from the crowd. The Dice Shrine's ambush leaves one as consolation.
what they hold
Decided before the reel spins: an evolution if a weapon is ready (level 8 and two mutations), otherwise one weighted draw (section 10, the odds) over the next level of everything held and every new passive item (v0.33: a chest can hand a new passive item, at a little under half the weight of an upgrade), otherwise 50 gold. The reel is honest: the result is fixed when the chest opens and the spin is the reveal.
skip and banish
v0.33 (Garry: no more coins for refusing): Sell is gone. Skip and Banish spend the same per-stage charges as the level-up cards (two skips and one banish a stage, more from the Upgrade board). Banish is locked on an evolution and on gold. With no skip left, a chest must be taken.
pause
The reel runs on the paused game like the card screen does; a pending level-up waits until the chest is closed, and after the last one the game waits for a tap before it resumes.

Structures

Nine small landmarks are scattered over the map at run start, one of every kind, at least 22 m apart. Each is single use: stand inside its ring and a wedge fills on the ground over three seconds (the Teleport one and a half; a dark hand on its leading edge, so it reads on any ground); step out and it drains. A used structure switches to its spent painting: the well runs dry, the cache stands open and empty, the crystal goes dark, the shop shutters, the drum skin tears, the candles go out, the magnet rusts, the Teleport's runes die.

v0.21 · Healing, cut back

A build could out-heal the desert. Mend ticked 2.4 HP a second for nothing, Thirst turned 8% of a laser's damage into health (and the laser hits forty creatures at once), the heart gave back a third of the bar and Astro patched 8% of it on every level. None of it had a cost, so past the fourth minute the hero simply could not die.

Now: regen waits three seconds after every hit (so it heals the gap between fights, not the middle of one) and Mend ticks 0.45 a rank. Lifesteal is capped at 4% of max HP a second however much damage a build pours out, and Thirst pays 1.5% a rank. The heart gives a fifth of the bar, Astro patches 5% a level, the healing well 35%. Vigour is 8% max HP a rank instead of 10%. The crowd's own numbers did not change: what changed is that staying alive is now about not being hit.

What killed you (v0.23.2). The revive screen's middle is a log of the last 30 seconds of hits, newest first, a line a hit: when, who (the creature's name, "(Elite)" on an Elite), how much, what was left, and what came with it (knocked back, roped, a charge, a pillar of light, the shield taking it, an evade). 💀 marks the hit that ended it; 🏹 a ranged hit, 🗡️ a melee one, 👑 anything a boss did. A button beside the count holds it while you read, the list scrolls under the thumb, and a button under it copies the whole log to the clipboard for a bug report. Every call that hurts the hero now says who and how (Player.take_damage(amount, from, info), EnemyManager.hit_info).

Standing back up costs the room (v0.22). A revive now detonates where the hero fell: a wave 30% of the nuke's radius that kills the crowd outright and leaves an Elite or a boss on 90% of its health. You never come back into the same pile that put you down.

Pickups

v0.40 · gems that stay, smaller and underfoot

XP gems no longer vanish next to the hero. Past 300 pickups on the ground the game used to fuse the hundred oldest gems anywhere into one big gem at their middle, so a gem a metre from him could disappear and its XP turn up somewhere else; now only gems farther than the magnet's reach plus 4 m fuse, cluster by cluster, each cluster into one big gem where it lay, with a pop and a glint, and coins have a cap of their own. Gems are half their old size and coins three quarters, and both draw under every painted figure: creatures, heroes, scenery and structures mark their pixels in the stencil buffer and the pickups skip those pixels, at no extra cost.

v0.42 · gems that look like gems

The XP gems are faceted octahedrons now (Garry: "make them look more like gems"; they were wedges), 5 % bigger as the camera sees them, the vacuum the size it was. They keep their slow float and, very rarely, a gem gives a little extra hop on top of it: each one once in 40 to 60 seconds, and never more than one starting on the screen every three quarters of a second, so a full field stays calm. The medium and large gems (5 and 25 XP) glint now and then with a small star, so the valuable ones catch the eye.

Power-ups drop from kills now, not from a clock: every creature has a small chance to leave one, scaled by how hard it was (its XP worth; elite variants count double, so a goblin drops one time in 75, a beetle one in 55, the Elite one in 18, a boss one in 7), and the rarer power-ups weigh more the harder the creature. The run sees about the same dozen as the old schedule gave, but they land where the fighting is and the big kills pay. A power-up waits forty seconds, blinking at the end (v0.20: twice what it did, since the fights now sprawl): a Heart (30% of max HP), Berserk (v0.23, was Rage: +50% attack damage and every weapon twice as fast for 20 s, so every cooldown is halved, orbiting blades spin twice as fast and turrets fire twice as often; red flames lick up round the hero's feet while it lasts; the flaming fist), Sprint (+40% speed, faster cooldowns, 15 s), a Shield (no damage for 6 s, a holographic sphere around the hero), a Nuke (v0.19, as rare as the shield: a wave from the hero that hits everything within 16 m for 400 and throws it back, drawn as a fat ring and a wall of light racing out over the sand with a flash and a long shake) or a Magnet Pulse (every gem on the map comes home). They are never pulled by the magnet: you walk to them. Two passives (v0.23.2) add drops of their own on every kill: Cake (v0.32, was Cheese: 0.3% a level, to 1.5%) leaves a slice of cake that heals 6% of max HP, and Pickpocket (0.4% a level, to 2%) a coin bag worth ten coins that the magnet pulls in like coins; both are small, quiet and never in the power-up table. Each is a folder under content/pickups/; the map's timeline carries the per-kill chance and each one's weight and hardness bias.

The rules in short (v0.34, Garry's sixth list). Three kinds of item. Weapons: five slots (v0.38, six before), all open from the first second, each levelling to 5 (a pick-up and four upgrades, each upgrade a bigger step that may raise several stats at once, the top as strong as the old level 8); its plate climbs the rarity colours with every level, and wears a brass dot along its top edge for each of its mutations taken (v0.35: none for the ones still open, so a weapon with all three shows three and one with none shows none). Each stage puts a weapon's open mutations on the table once (element, volume, motion, and a hero's own after them) and it takes one; a weapon at level 5 carrying all three of its mutations evolves at the next chest, and the evolution is built to be more than the three together. Mutations and evolutions taken off the table are kept in the content, hidden, to bring back. Lines: five slots beside the weapons (v0.38), four levels each (the plate climbs common, uncommon, rare, epic); a maxed line offers its evolution at a level-up and the evolution keeps the slot. Weapons and lines are the HUD's ten slots. Passive items: as many as the run finds, never on the HUD (the pause screen's Build tab and the Collection list them), each with a rarity, common to legendary, that the plate's colour shows and that weighs the offers (section 10), and at most five levels. Mutations

Every weapon has three mutations, cut the same way each time (the bee rule, after the Hornet Nest that started it): an Element that makes the weapon carry fire or poison, a Volume that gives a lot more of it for a little less each, and a Motion that changes what it does in the world.

  • A weapon's mutations go on the table together, at set times. Mutations never sit in a level-up. Each stage has mutation slots (seconds into the stage: seven on stage 1 from 79 s, eight on the later ones from 50 s, v0.33, so every weapon gets a turn and some a second; path_times in the map); at each slot the weapon with mutations still open (the highest level first) is offered all of its remaining mutations on one table, with the hero's own option after them, no reroll and no banish. As many slots as the cap and a spare, so every weapon sees its mutations once a stage and a weapon found late still gets a turn. (Run.path_due, Run.next_path_weapon, CardPool.path_offer, Game.present_path_offer.)
  • One per weapon per stage. Take one and the other two leave the pool until the portal; on stage 2 a second, on stage 3 the third. A weapon carried from the start can end fully mutated, one found on stage 2 carries two, a sixth weapon found on stage 3 carries one. Which one you take first is the build. (Run.mutation_stage, Run.can_mutate.)
  • No slot, no level, no stacking. A mutation is part of the weapon: dots on its icon in the strip, a line under it in the pause build, a row in the collection. Its effects fold into the weapon's sheet like a targeted passive, its params merge over the weapon's, and it may swap the weapon's behaviour or visual (WeaponInstance.mutations, params, behavior()).
  • Both elements can be taken (v0.26; until then the first fire mutation took every poison one out of the pool, and the other way round). Ten weapons carry a fire mutation, nine a poison one.
  • Evolutions keep them, and need two of them (above).
  • The Stranger's loophole. The only way past exclusivity: among his wares can be a mutation of a weapon whose pick you already spent this stage, at 20% of max HP instead of 15.

The small print under each is what it looks like in the world. Forty of the fifty-seven are stat effects or a behaviour parameter; the rest ride on twelve primitives added for them: a ground zone that ticks damage or slows (Static Field and the Drifter's dust), the Oil Slick's leak (v0.23: drops of burning oil fall from the bottle while it flies, one every 0.9 m after the first two, and each lands as a small spill that burns and slows, so the line draws itself behind the bottle instead of appearing where it lands), a shot that bursts into others (Sky Flare, Frag), a behaviour swap (Needle Rain, Homing Skulls, Walking Buddy), the boomerang, the sticky grenade, a pull on the hero's hits (Wide Orbit, Lasso), a slow and a mark on creatures (Rattle, Cauterize), the lunge (Strike, Lunge), the twister, the leaping mine, and poison passing on death (Blight). They live in EnemyManager (statuses), ProjectileManager (flight), Zone, and the behaviours.

Evolutions

A weapon at level 8 that carries two of its mutations evolves at the next chest: the reel lands on the evolved form, and Take it swaps the weapon out. The evolved form keeps the mutations (a Killer Swarm that twisted still twists), gets a gold-trimmed mesh and cannot level further. One evolution per weapon, listed with its weapon in the list above. Until v0.16 the key was a paired passive; the path is the key now, so the first evolutions land on stage 2, where the reel starts paying out anyway.

Passive slots

The lines are passives again (Garry, pass 46: "change all the Line items to be just Passives, same colour, no separate section"). Every item that builds the hero is now one kind with one colour (sage), one Collection tab and one rule: a run holds at most six passives, and the stage decides how many of the six are open: four on stage 1, five on stage 2, six from stage 3 (passive_cap: [4, 5, 6] in the map's timeline, Run.passive_cap()). The strip shows every slot from the first second: a held passive, an empty open slot (an aged plate with a dashed edge), or a padlocked slot with the stage that opens it, beside the weapons' own six (four open on stage 1, six from stage 2). With twelve plates the strip still fits one row on a phone at 90%.

  • What the old lines kept. Their trades (Brawn's slower step, Bristle's longer cooldowns, Thirst's smaller health bar) and their evolutions: once a former line is at its top level, the next level-up offers its evolution, weighted three times, and the evolved passive takes the same slot (evolves_into / evolves_from on PassiveDef, Run.evolve_passive).
  • What changed. No more "two lines a run": any passive can be taken while a slot is open, so a build can be all toughness or all damage. Levels, not ranks.
  • The favourite. A hero's favourite can now be a passive: Ironwood's arrives with a head start the moment he takes it, from a card, the shop or the Stranger (v0.33: Brawn at level 2; in v0.32 the Cake at its top level).
  • Desperado (v0.32). Attack damage rises as health falls: +12% a level at the brink of death, scaled by the share of health missing (+60% at level 5 on one hit point, nothing at full health); the sheet is refreshed in 10% steps (desperado stat, Run.note_hp).
  • Cake (v0.32, was Cheese). 0.3% a level that a kill drops a slice of cake that heals 6% of max HP. Same numbers, new picture.

Unlocks

Since v0.30 half the items sit behind unlock quests: 23 of the 45 base weapons, passives and lines. Sixteen can be done on stage 1 (kills of the stage-1 creatures, the Elite, minutes survived, a level, a few chests, the first boss, totals of kills, runs and gold), six take stage 2 (the Gila, an evolution, ten minutes, level 20, the stage-2 creatures) and one takes stage 3 (reaching it). The Collection shows a locked item's quest and its progress and opens a page with the stage that can do it; the run shows an UNLOCKED banner with the item's icon the moment one opens, and a save's old totals complete what they already prove on the title, quietly. The other half is available from the first run. Upgrades on the Tommy Gun add rounds to the burst and damage; on the Land Mine damage, area, a second mine, and faster laying. Since v0.25 a companion gun aims every round from where it is circling, not along the hero's line to the target: the Revolver's tight rounds went a metre wide of it, and the Tommy Gun hits more often too.

Elements: poison and fire

Poison is a debuff creatures carry: they turn bright green and take a tick of damage every two seconds, with a green-white flicker and a puff so it is never quiet, and only a nudge of knockback. A poisoned creature keeps one entry per item: the same item hitting again resets the clock instead of stacking, a different poison item adds its own. Levelling a poison item raises its tick damage and, at a couple of levels, adds a stack; never the speed. The base pace is deliberate: a saw's worth of damage takes a poison ten seconds. Poison weapons prefer creatures that are not poisoned yet, so the debuff spreads instead of piling up on one goblin.

v0.8 · fire, the design

Fire is poison's loud cousin. Where poison is patient (a small tick every two seconds for ten seconds), fire is a burst: a creature that catches fire takes 4 damage every 0.6 seconds for 1.8 seconds, three ticks and done, 12 damage in all against poison's 15. Hotter per second, gone sooner, worth less on one body. Its worth is in the crowd: every tick, every burning creature rolls to set alight each creature it is touching (touching means the two bodies' radii plus a hand's width). A fire lit by a weapon jumps with the weapon's fire_spread chance, 50% at level one; a fire caught from another creature is a child fire and jumps at 40% of that, so 20%, and so do its children, so a pack goes up like a fuse and then peters out at the edges. A child fire never jumps back to the creature it came from, so no pair of goblins keeps each other burning; the fire has to find new bodies or die. Bosses do not catch fire from a neighbour (they can be lit directly).

The rules match poison's where it matters. One entry per item on a creature: the same item hitting a burning creature resets its clock instead of stacking; a second fire item adds its own. Levelling a fire item raises its tick damage and, at a couple of levels, its spread chance or its burn time; never the tick rate. Fire weapons aim at creatures that are not burning yet (nearest unburnt, thickest unburnt crowd), so the flames go where there is something left to burn.

You can wield both (v0.26). Until then a run committed to one element the moment it held a poison or fire item, and the cards, the shop, the reel and the Stranger never offered the other; the rule is gone, so the Molotov and the Skull Rain can burn and poison the same crowd. Each item still carries its element in its data (element on a passive, derived from fire_damage / poison_damage on a weapon, Run.element_of): a fire bomb still looks for the crowd that is not burning yet, a poison bomb for the one that is not poisoned.

Bombs spread (v0.26). Every bomb weapon, and every bomb of a volley, picks a crowd that no other bomb is headed for or came down on in the last two seconds (ProjectileManager.claimed_spots, EnemyManager.densest_point keeping away from them); they double up only when there is no other crowd in range, and then land round it rather than on one spot. With the Grenade, the Molotov and the Skull Rain against four packs, the share of bombs landing within two metres of another fell from 47% to 24% (the rest is more bombs in the air than packs). The Singularity's black holes take part too.

How it looks. A burning creature turns ember red and flickers, tongues of flame lick up it the whole time it burns, each damage tick flashes it orange (poison's tick is green, a plain hit white), and catching fire flares three tongues at once with a ring of sparks and a match-strike sound. The Molotov leaves a spill: a barely-there orange pool on the sand, see-through and gone at its lobed rim, with tongues of fire licking up all over it (v0.23.2: for one build it was a dark oil stain, which read as a shadow).

v0.23.4 · the spill is black

The Molotov's spill (and every drop of the Oil Slick) went from a faint orange to black oil, still see-through: 55% at the middle fading to nothing at its lobed rim, the flames licking over it. The orange pool read as sand in the sun; black reads as a spill on any ground.

v0.23 · fire that looks like fire

Fire used to be orange balls: two alpha spheres rising off a burning creature, six more bobbing over a saturated orange disc for the Molotov's pool. Every flame is now a tongue, a camera-facing quad in one pooled MultiMesh drawn by a small cel fire shader, built from two published recipes: Minionsart's stylised fire (a flame mask eaten away by scrolling noise, the erosion growing as the particle ages; the Godot port is "Stylized fire (3D)" on godotshaders.com) and the toon step bands of the papaya-games devlog "How to make a stylized fire shader". The mask is a teardrop worked out in the shader (a round base drawn to a point), two scrolls of one baked 128 px tileable noise at different sizes and speeds eat into it harder towards the tip, the tongue sways more at the top, and three hard bands colour it: a pale gold heart, an orange body, a dark red rim, each edge one smoothstep wide so it sits in the game's cel look instead of glowing like a photo. A burning creature sends up one tongue every 0.18 s, drawn a little towards the camera so it burns in front of its sprite. The spill under a pool is about half as opaque as the old disc and a paler orange: the colour is mostly the flames' job. The poison puddle got the same treatment in v0.23.1: a muted, see-through green spill that fades at its rim, and its puffs of smoke at a third of their old strength.

The items. Three weapons, one passive and three evolutions (the Flare Gun and the Branding Iron are hidden since v0.24), mirroring poison's set so the two families are the same shape and the choice is about feel: Flare Gun (nearest unburnt, the Peashooter's slot in a fire build), Molotov (a fire pool, the Lob Grenade's slot), Branding Iron (a burning orbit, the Rattlesnake's slot), Kindling (+15% burn damage per level on every fire item, pairs with the Flare Gun), and Roman Candle, Firestorm and Ring of Fire as the evolutions (six damage a tick, longer burns, 70% spread). Numbers are in the table above and in content/weapons; the engine side is one debuff next to poison in EnemyManager (ignite, _tick_fire), the elemental cloud both bombs share, and the orange branch of the flash in the billboard shader.

Why this shape. Poison rewards spreading it thin and waiting; fire rewards herding. A player who can bunch a pack before the flare lands gets the whole pack for one shot, and a player who cannot gets a few goblins and a lesson. The two builds should feel like different games on the same screen, (until v0.26 a no-mixing rule kept them from collapsing into "all debuffs on everything"; both can be held now).

Passive mechanisms: what each hook does

Until v0.13 every passive was a number on the stat sheet, and a level-up read like a spreadsheet. These seven each add one reusable hook in the systems (a stat the sheet carries but a system consumes), so the data can vary them and later items can reuse them. What each one does, exactly, and why it is in the pool:

crit (Deadeye)
crit_chance and crit_damage on the player sheet. When a weapon hit lands, EnemyManager.damage rolls the chance and multiplies the damage (×2 by default); poison and fire ticks never crit. A crit squashes the creature harder and pops a gold flash. It makes a single-shot build spiky. v0.33: Deadeye is a line, four levels of +8% (+32%), evolving into Eagle Eye (+45% chance and crits for ×2.5).
execute (Guillotine)
execute_frac. After a weapon hit that leaves a creature alive but below the fraction of its max HP, the creature dies anyway, with a red burst. Bosses are exempt (the mini-boss is not). It is the answer to the fat late creatures: a mummy at 20% is dead to a pea. Three levels because 21% is already a fifth of every creature's health.
thorns (Cactus Coat)
thorns. On every hit the hero takes, every creature within 2.3 m takes that damage and a knockback. It is the tank's passive: it does nothing for a kiting build and everything for a hero who stands in the crowd (Astro's suit, Dad). Six a level, thirty at max, which kills goblins outright.
adrenaline (Adrenaline)
adrenaline_speed and adrenaline_might per stack. Run.add_kill adds a stack (twenty at most) and restarts a 4 s clock; when the clock runs out all stacks go together. The stacks fold into the sheet as one "Adrenaline ×n" line, rebuilt at most four times a second so a crowd does not cost frames; the HUD shows the count under the buff row. It rewards staying in the fight the way momentum rewards not being hit.
overkill (Overkill)
overkill. When a hit takes a creature below zero, the overflow times the fraction is dealt in a 2.5 m radius around the body, once (a splash cannot splash). It turns big single hits (mines, grenades, the Boomstick) into crowd damage, and does nothing for chip-damage builds, which is the point.
ricochet (Ricochet)
ricochet. Replaced Trigger Happy in v0.14: that one took a slice off every cooldown per kill with no ceiling, so in a crowd it turned every weapon into a hose and the item was either useless or broken. A ricochet is bounded by construction: when a straight shot (never a lob, never a missile) kills, it rolls the chance and fires one more shot of the same kind from the body at the nearest creature not yet hit, at 60% damage, and that shot cannot bounce again. It rewards precision weapons in crowds without scaling past them.
homing agility (Guidance Fins)
homing_turn, a multiplier on the turn rate of every missile and hornet. The support item for the homing family.
weapon tags (Payload)
A weapon's JSON may carry tags (homing, explosive), and an effect may aim at a tag (target: "tag:homing") the way it aims at one weapon or all of them. The player's sheet carries such effects for the weapons to pick up but never folds them into the hero's own numbers (a scoped sheet), so Payload's extra missile reaches the pod and the nest and nothing else.
XP gain (Field Notes)
xp_gain, a multiplier applied in Run.add_xp. The plainest of the seven and the one every survivor game needs: it decides whether a run is card-rich or card-poor. v0.33: a line, four levels of +8% (+36% at max), evolving into the Almanac (+50%).

Balance pass in the same version: Multishot had five levels of which two did nothing (fixed: three levels, one projectile each); the flat passives that had eight levels now have five; the Tommy Gun's rounds do 3 instead of 2, so at its double range it is a real alternative to the shotgun rather than a toy. In v0.14 Astro's suit lost half its armor (+1, not +2) and heals 8% a level instead of 10: two armor took a third off a goblin's bite in the first minute, which was the whole first minute.

Builds we cannot make yet

Paths the slots point at, with the item each one is missing (v0.33 made three of them: Elite hunting with Big Game Hunter, luck with Clover, longer debuffs with Ducktape):

  • Crit. Only Deadeye's evolution, Eagle Eye, raises crit damage; a crit damage passive item would make crit a build from stage 1.
  • Companion. The turret, the mines and the companions have nothing to grow them: a passive for companion count and damage (turrets, mines, familiars) would.
  • Duration. Ducktape lengthens what sticks to creatures; clouds, fire pools, zones, the beam and the turrets' lifetime still have nothing: a duration passive.
  • Crowd control. No knockback, stagger or slow on hit: a passive that adds knockback and a short stagger.
  • Power-ups. Nothing lengthens Berserk, the shield or the magnet, or makes them drop more: a power-up passive.
  • Big shots. Reach widens blasts but not the shots themselves: a projectile size passive.

Four heroes, about forty weapons and evolutions, eighty-odd mutations taken one a stage per weapon, fifteen lines into five slots, twenty passive items on top, and two or three perks each: a three-stage run is in the hundreds of millions of builds. Every hornet is a homing seeker with no blast; when the boss died, every hornet in the air lost its target at once and dived into the sand, and each dive "exploded" with a blast radius of zero, spawning an elemental cloud of radius zero whose sphere meshes logged hundreds of engine warnings apiece: thousands of stack traces in one frame. Seekers that reach the ground now just fizzle, a cloud is never smaller than a step, a missile turning at a target straight behind it yaws instead of dividing by zero, and two chests arriving together queue for one reel. The game also writes a breadcrumb line every second (the run's clock, stage, level, crowd, build) that Settings shows as "Last run's last second", so the next crash comes with a where.

Reading a charge

Pace (v0.34, Garry: everyone 20% slower, one number to tune it). The hero and every creature move at move_pace (0.6 since Garry set it by hand before v0.38; 0.8 in v0.34; in the map's timeline): walks, charges, rolls and the walk cycles' frame rates. To keep dodging as fair as before, every attack meant to be dodged slows with them: hostile shots, the rocket, boulders and the Gila's rumbles fly at the same pace, and every red warning lasts 1/0.8 as long (a 1 s ring now warns 1.25 s), so a slower hero still covers the same ground before it lands. Charges and rolls run longer at the slower speed so they still end where their red line ends. Attack cooldowns and spawns do not change.

Every mark on the ground that means damage is red (v0.15): the cactus's landing ring, the dwarf's wedge, the harpooner's line, the beetle's charge, the bosses' rings and fans, the toad's burst. Green is kept for the two things that are not an attack: your own poison clouds and the shaman's mend.

A charger's wind-up only warns you if you can see where it points. The Bull Beetle and the Dune Scorpion carry side, back-diagonal and front-diagonal sets besides front and back (stills edited from the front painting, a walk clip each), and while winding up or charging they face their charge direction rather than their drift. The charge direction is fixed the instant the red line appears and the creature runs that line even if the hero has moved: the telegraph is what makes the dodge fair. Any creature gets eight directions by setting "directions": 8 in its JSON. v0.34: the Bull Beetle is a rhino beetle walked on Garry's rigged model and its own walk and death clips, in four looks from serious to cartoony, the cartoon mostly in the eyes: BB-A a realistic mahogany beetle, BB-B glossy with big shiny eyes, BB-C a cartoon with angry white eyes (in play), BB-D huge googly eyes and a grin. The arena's ENEMY tab shows all four; the JSON's variant picks one. v0.36 (Garry kept BB-C): the beetle's charge carries itself. Winding up, it crouches, its legs scrabble in place faster and faster, it rocks and shakes harder towards the launch and paws sand back behind it; it launches stretched long out of a burst of sand, runs with its legs at two and a half times their walk, bouncing at every step and leaning into the run, with a trail of soft dust behind it, and digs in squashed at the end. Whatever stands in its way is shoved out to the side, about half a metre, and walks on; nothing is thrown off its course, and a boss, an Elite or anything bigger than the beetle is never shoved. All of it is numbers in its charge block (shake, crouch, legs, lean, dust, shove, shove_walk), so another charger can take any of it; the Dune Scorpion has none and charges as before. The beetle also stands on its shadow now, as the scorpion does since v0.33: the blob is wider and slid up under the body, and the painting sunk onto it ("shadow"; its painted legs ended above the cell's floor, so it hovered).

v0.21 · frames with a hundred and fifty creatures

The crowd is cheap to think about and expensive to draw: at the new cap the whole simulation costs a third of a millisecond a frame on a desktop, so the frames the phone drops are fill, not maths. Three things were cut. Every creature used to lay down a soft blend-mode shadow quad, and a hundred and fifty of them overlapped into a wall of blending: past seventy creatures only the ones near the hero cast one. The effect pool now spends less the fuller it is (a third of the particles once it is three-quarters full), so a hundred hits a second no longer each pay for a puff. And a screen full of shots drops every other trail bead, which halves that layer while the line still reads. The steering's per-creature stat lookups were also hoisted to one a frame.

v0.23 · the shards hit

The Idol's shards flew straight through the hero. A creature's straight shot keeps the height it left the muzzle at (so dunes never swallow it), and a shard off a three-metre creature's staff flies at head height, 1.3 m up; the hit test was a sphere under a metre across around the hero's feet, so every shard passed over his head, and even the dwarf's buckshot only landed inside a narrow window. The test is now a flat distance with a height band as tall as the hero, so a shot that visibly crosses him hits him (a regression test fires one at head height).

v0.22 · One shove a second

The dwarf's buckshot, the ranged Elite's shards, the scorpion's charge, its tail slam and its burrow all throw the hero now, which makes a crowd a thing you are pushed around inside rather than a wall you stand in. Stacking them would be a catapult, so every shove goes through one gate: one lands, and the next second's are ignored, and the force itself is capped. Knockback the hero's own build causes (a harpoon rope pulling him, a tornado) is not gated.

v0.39 · waves, overrides and the Level Editor (Garry: "a tool which is all about setting up the pacing and progression of the waves")

Three layers now feed the crowd, all in the map's timeline_data (docs/waves_schema.md is the whole schema). The cap curve says how many may be alive at each second. The roster rows are drawn by weight while open, as before, and each row may now set a pack, a pattern (a pack at one spot, scattered one by one, or lone), an art variant and stats overrides. Waves are explicit windows on top: from t, a burst of burst creatures every every seconds until count or until, one of the wave's alts rolled afresh every run (Garry's example: "50% chance that they will all 20 be skeletons with 10 hp, 15 atk melee, and a 50% chance that all 20 will be goblins with 15 hp, 10 atk melee"), spawning into free cap room unless respect_cap is off, with an elite modifier on all of them if asked. Events gained weighted lists of creatures, a per-run chance, a t_jitter, random elite_every intervals ([min, max]) and swarm patterns. Overrides (stats on a row, an alt or an event) change one spawn's HP, damage, speed, size, bite cadence (contact_cd), attack rate, projectile speed, knockback, pack, cap share, XP, drops and power-up table, and switch a boss's or Elite's attacks on and off; a missing key keeps the creature's own number, and the override is a cached copy of the creature (EnemyDef.with_overrides) with its own type under the hood.

The Level Editor (tools\level_editor.cmd) is a local page that edits exactly this: a calendar of the run with the cap curve, the roster bars, the wave blocks and the event flags on a time axis, an inspector for whatever is selected (every override with the creature's own value as its placeholder), a Settings tab for the scalars, a Creatures palette, and an Instructions tab with a worked example. Save writes the JSON, keeps a backup and runs the JSON → .tres import, so the editor binary plays it on the next launch; the JSON is what gets committed. The first setup is today's timeline as it stands.

The pacing itself (v0.39, Garry): skeletons and knights come together from 0:00 as one group at three skeletons to a knight; the Lesser Scorpion is the second creature met (1:00); the fire ants left the opening for 5:00 and are 0.6 of a mummy now (36 HP, bite 8, size 1.15, packs of eight); the Red Goblin, the Red Runt and the Werewolf wait in the content and the editor's palette until he places them.

v0.14 · the roster looked at hard

Before this version the roster was a list of arrival times with fixed weights: the goblin arrived at 0:00 with weight 10 and kept it to the end, so at minute nine the crowd was still a third goblins, stage 2 brought nothing new, and the boss at every stage was the same boss. Megabonk's answer, and Vampire Survivors' before it, is a roster that changes shape with the minute: early creatures fade as harder ones take over, some creatures exist only in the later part of the run, and each stage has its own boss so continuing is a decision with something on the other side.

The roster rows now carry a second weight, weight_late, reached at weight_late_t (10:00, the end of stage 2), and the weight slides between the two from the row's arrival. Goblins go from 10 to 3, runts from 6 to 2; mummies climb from 3 to 5, toads from 2 to 4. Four creatures arrived: the Horned Toad late in stage 1, then the Trapdoor Spider, the Jackalope and the Dust Shaman, all three only in stage 2 (three of eleven, about the 30% that a first run never sees). Stage 2 also got a jackalope swarm at 8:30 and a spider swarm at 14:00, and its own boss.

  • Live cap. Starts at 25 and climbs linearly with the run's clock to 150 at the end of stage 3, six times the start (v0.19: 8.35 a minute, no jump per stage, so stage 2 opens at 67 and stage 3 at 108; bursts of three to twelve every 0.7 s keep it full). Swarm rings temporarily ignore the cap up to a hard limit of 180.
  • Strength. The crowd grows with the hero as well as the clock (v0.19): every creature spawns with +5% HP and +3% damage per hero level above the first (level_hp_per_level, level_damage_per_level in the timeline), on top of the time curve (steeper now: hp_growth 0.14) and the stage multipliers (1 / 2.2 / 3.6). A level-30 hero at the end of stage 3 meets goblins with about nine times their first-minute HP.
  • Variety. The roster keeps arriving through all three stages (v0.18): seven creatures in stage 1 (the Sand Crab from 3:20), five more in stage 2 (mummies, beetles, toads, the Harpoon Troglodyte at 7:00, jackalopes), the Dust Shaman only from stage 3; so the mix on the ground widens at the same pace as the count.
  • Live limits. A creature JSON may say max_alive: the director skips its roster row while that many stand (the harpooner is one at a time, the rocketeers five).
  • Roster. The timeline says which creatures are eligible at a given time and with what weights. The director rolls from that roster whenever the cap has room, spawning in bursts so packs arrive together.
  • Scaling. Creature HP grows with elapsed time (+10% a minute, compounding gently), then HP and damage jump with the stage multiplier (×2.2, ×3.34) and speed rises 10% a stage. Endless multiplies by 1.12 every wave of a minute, compounding (v0.29).
  • Scripted events. The Elite, elite variants, swarm rings and bosses are timestamped entries in the map's timeline.
  • Culling. Creatures more than 30 m behind the hero are teleported back onto the spawn ring ahead. Nothing is wasted, and the pressure stays in front. The boss and the Elite are never culled.
  • Crowd behaviour. Separation steering on a spatial grid so sixty goblins flow around cacti and each other instead of stacking into one sprite.
