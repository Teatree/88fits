The run

A run is one stage of Red Canyon. A fresh level-1 hero lands in a beam of light with his own weapon, survives the stage's clock (Sundown 7:10, Nightfall 10:00, Blood Moon 13:00) and kills its boss. Then a portal counts down 60 seconds: Extract ends the run, or Endless starts and goes on until he falls. Every end of a run banks the gold, a death too.

pass 87 · stages are their own runs

Garry: "Now Player Unlocks Stages and is only able to enter them after having completed the previous one ... the Portal that appears shows a 1 minute timer before the Endless Mode will activate. There is only an Extract button now in the Portal." There is no Continue to the next stage any more. Beating a stage's boss unlocks the next one, and once stage 1 is beaten every PLAY opens the Stages menu.

The three stages

The map keeps one timeline on one clock (timeline_data), and stage_list cuts it into three windows. A stage run is a fresh run (level 1, the hero's start weapon, the Upgrade board, nothing else) whose clock starts at its window's start, so a Nightfall run opens with the rows of 7:10. The HUD, the pause screen and Down! show the stage's own clock from 0:00.

the Stages menu
Opens on every PLAY once stage 1 is beaten (ui/StagesScreen.gd): "Choose a Stage", one card per stage with its picture in the stage's look, "Difficulty: Easy / Medium / Hard", the length to the boss and the boss's plate, which stays a silhouette reading "Boss not encountered yet" until the hero has met it. A locked stage says "Beat Stage k" and shakes when tapped. PLAY AGAIN on the Results replays the same stage. Unlocks live in Save.stages_beaten.
the landing
Before the clock starts, a 3 s beam of light: the hero comes down it (tap after 1 s to skip), lands in a ring of dust, the HUD fades in. Since pass 100 the stage's look is on before the beam, and the beam draws over the filter so it reads as light in Nightfall and Blood Moon. Slot 1 holds the hero's own weapon at level 1 (start_weapon: the Drifter's Revolver, Astro's Gravity Gun, Calavera's Guitarra, Ironwood's Spear, Solenne's Sun Scepter, Fadeaway's Basketball, Jeżyk's Frisbee, May's Big Mouth).
levels
Every level asks the map's XP curve (15 + 6L + 0.5L², xp_curve) times xp_need_mult (1.65 on Red Canyon since pass 79). A due mutation offer turns the next level-up into a mutation level-up (section 08); what grows with the hero's level counts only his card levels.
the stage's look
A stage's filter is two quads over the whole view (core/systems/StageLook.gd): the world at the filter's strength, the creatures and the hero at 0.35 of it so they stay brighter than the ground, and every ground mark, shadow, gem, coin, outline, effect and projectile drawn untinted on top. Sundown has none; Nightfall is a slate blue; Blood Moon a dark crimson over the red hell ground, dark so the red warnings still read.
hell
Blood Moon's entry says hell: true: the sand turns red, and the hell channel (imps from the first second, hellhounds from 1:49) spawns on top of the regular crowd from a cap of its own, 0.4 of the live cap (hell_cap [0, 0.3, 0.4] by stage).

Elites, bosses, the portal and Endless

the Elite
A mini-boss: Elite-sized with an outline, a toast and a HUD arrow but no bar; regular spawning carries on. Each Elite event rolls one of three: the Dune Scorpion (charges, burrows and bursts up under the hero, tail slams, a fan of barbs, and since pass 95 its tail_sniper: 7 turns in 10, never with the hero within 5 m, it freezes and fires three Saiga-style beams one after the other, each aimed afresh), the Sun Prophet (fights at range: a fan of gold shards, columns of light, a blink) or the Stone Golem (the slowest, most health: a smack, a half-disc of stalactites, a boulder thrown across the map). It comes at 215 s, 730 s and 1420 s on the map clock (3:35 into Sundown, 5:00 into Nightfall, 6:30 into Blood Moon) and always drops a chest. Its HP is its base × 1.8 for each stage after the first × the stage multiplier × creature_hp_mult, times the event's own hp_mult (0.8 in Nightfall, 0.45 in Blood Moon).
coloured Elites
The "Elites every" row (a roster row since pass 84) sends one every 129 s from 301 s: a creature drawn from the rows live at that second, wearing a random elite modifier (Explosive, Shielded, Speedy or Splitter, content/elites), alone, as its pack or as a ring. They also always drop a chest.
bosses
One per stage, when the stage's clock runs out. Big silhouette, telegraphed attacks, a bar under the HUD strip; while it lives the live cap drops to 0.6 of itself (boss_cap_frac). The Tumbleweed King: a roll down a red line, a volley of twelve tumbleweeds that trail dust (pass 95), a ring of tumbleweed minions, and the stomp: three hops, each landing a wave over the sand with a ring before it. Gila Grande: a lunge, the cactus rain (every circle on a huge area lights at once, then it spews them all from its mouth to land together: stand in a gap), a burrow (it sinks out of sight where it can be seen sinking, waits 3 s and erupts where the hero is heading, throwing him and the crowd) and a ring of summons. The Brander: a ring of fire round the fight that closes in from 21 m to 8 over forty seconds and burns whoever stands outside it, hero or creature; burning lines left by its iron for nine seconds; a charge, a slam, rings of imps. A boss's summons are never specials.
boss HP
Garry (pass 83): "at least 25 seconds of Player's FULL DPS on Tumbleweed king ... 40s for the next and a full minute to the last one". When a boss arrives its HP is the larger of its data HP (its hp × its per-stage growth × the stage multiplier, × the event's hp_mult) and the hero's full DPS on it right then × its seconds (25 / 40 / 60, boss_hp_scaling.seconds) × (1 − its damage reduction). A fresh hero's DPS usually rules. Pass 72's other mode, a peg to the last 45 s of real DPS, is one setting away (mode: "dps").
boss attacks
A boss's or Elite's attack list is data (boss.attacks, each row may carry a chance). Custom attacks are content folders under content/boss_attacks/ (kinds rain, volley, ring, slam, script), edited in the Level Editor's Boss attacks tab; the Gila's cactus_rain is one. The King's stomp and the Scorpion's tail_sniper are built-in scripts beside the older roll, slam, burrow and spawn ring.
boss down
BOSS DOWN: the stage is beaten and the next unlocks, the boss's purse is paid, its coins scatter (60, 150 and 200 on Red Canyon's three) and its violet chest lands (two chests from the Gila, three from the Brander). Opening the boss chest calls every gem, coin and chest on the map to the hero, so the reward is a thing you walk to and choose.
the portal
Appears where the boss dies, with one small wave of twelve (portal_wave) and then nothing. Its countdown (endless.portal_seconds, 60 s) shows on the HUD's clock plate with ENDLESS IN under it, and stands still behind every other screen (the cards, a chest, the pause menu). Walking in opens the stage clear: EXTRACT ends the run with its gold; the Endless card counts down beside it; NOT YET closes it and the count runs on. At 0 the portal closes and Endless starts.
Endless
A huge first wave (60 creatures), then a wave every 10 s of 30 + 2 per wave after the second, in packs from all round the hero, past the live cap (the hard cap holds), with the crowd kept up to the stage-end cap between waves. The creatures are the stage's late roster (the rows open in its last 60 s). Each wave fixes its numbers afresh: HP × 1.15 × 1.15k−1 and ATK × 1.095k−1 for wave k, both compounding (pass 103; they were 1.11 and 1.07), speed × 1.1 growing 1.5% a wave to at most ×1.6. A kill pays 0.35 of its XP (xp_share) and rolls its coin only 5% of the time (coin_chance); power-ups and chests drop as before; no bosses, Elites or specials. The HUD shows Endless's time with WAVE n. It goes until the hero falls, and that death banks the purse like any end.
Endless pushes back
Garry (pass 103): "make it so the enemies get more and more knockback resistance with each minute (by minute 1.5 enemies don't have any knockback from not explosive weapons at all)". Every push from the hero's side is multiplied by 1 − resistance, which rises in a straight line from none at Endless's start to full at 90 s (knock_resist_seconds, knock_resist_max 1.0). Explosions are exempt: weapons tagged explosive (the grenade, the mine, the rocket family and their evolutions), the sources nuke, revive, Powder Keg, a loose rocket and blast, and any hit flagged as a blast.
How this got here (history)
  • 15 minutes, then 21. Until v0.32 a run was 15 minutes in three 5-minute stages, benchmarked on Archero's early chapters and Survivor.io stages. Garry then asked for progression at 0.7 times the pace, Megabonk's ten-minute stages in mind: every clock was stretched by 430/300 (each stage 7:10) and each level asked twice the XP, so a full clear took about 21 minutes and the hero met the first boss at about level 22. Since pass 87 a run is one stage, and the later stages were stretched to 10:00 and 13:00 and retuned for a fresh level-1 hero (pass 87's lane TUNE: a quarter of the Upgrade board should clear Nightfall, half of it Blood Moon).
  • The Elite's times were 2:30, 7:30 and 12:30, then 3:35, 10:45 and 17:55 (v0.32), then 215 / 730 / 1420 s when the stages were stretched in pass 87. In the old endless an Elite came half a stage after each boss.
  • Stage 3 as hell (v0.29): the hell crowd came from a cap of its own (40% more bodies, 30% on stage 2, with hobgoblins and Hammer Giants), the stage stood at 20.6 of stage 2's stats, and the Brander waited at 15:00. The Gila's seven-line rumble (v0.29, a wave of heaving sand down each line) was replaced by the red cactus rain in pass 72; the Gila used to pay two chests and thirty coins against the King's one and twelve, so continuing was worth more than extracting.
  • Continue and Stay. Until pass 87 the portal offered Extract or Continue to the next stage; after stage 3's boss the second road was Stay: hell ×1.12 tougher every minute with a boss every five minutes. Until v0.29 extracting paid ×1.25 (gone: gold is gold). v0.34 (Garry's sixth list) made the portal wait for the hero instead of feeding him creatures while he decided, and added Not yet. Pass 87 replaced all of it with the 60 s countdown, EXTRACT and Endless (first Endless numbers: hp_growth 0.11, atk_growth 0.07, then pass 103's 0.15 and 0.095).
  • The Elite's other tricks and the old bosses' wording are in section 09's history.

Down, death and gold

Down!
The hero falls, the death plays a moment, then the game pauses behind Down!: "★ 1 CHANCE LEFT ★", a count of eight seconds, who put him down, then GET UP · WATCH AN AD (no ads yet, so it is simply free) and STAY DOWN, which asks first while the count holds. Nothing chosen when the count runs out is staying down. There is no gold revive since pass 77 (Garry: "Remove the 'Get up with gold' option"). One chance per run plus the Upgrade board's; he comes back with half his health (more with Temperance Risen), two seconds of grace and the revive's blast.
the revive's blast
A revive detonates where the hero fell (v0.22, "standing back up costs the room"): a wave 30% of the Nuke's radius that kills the crowd outright and leaves an Elite or a boss on 90% of its health. He never comes back into the pile that put him down.
the Phoenix Feather
A one-shot passive item (pass 100; Garry: "should still be a revive item with 50% HP on revive. It can only be used once and doesn't feature any upgrades"): two seconds after he falls he gets back up at half health with the revive's blast, before Down! ever opens. Once a run; it never levels; the pause screen's Build tab marks it "(used)" and its plate rises off him in embers.
the hit log
With the developer menu's hit log on, or the player's debug mode, Down! lists the last 30 seconds under the count, newest first: when, the badge of what did it (a skull for a boss, a crown for an Elite, a gear for a special, a bow for a ranged shot, a heart for a heal), the creature's name in its own colour, what it cost or gave back, and a red X on the hit that ended it; a button holds the count and another copies the log. Every call that hurts the hero says who and how (Player.take_damage(amount, from, info)). The free revive button lives in the developer menu.
gold
Every end of a run banks the whole purse (pass 70): extracting, a death, a fall in Endless, abandoning. Only quitting the app mid-run loses it. The Strongbox (gold_bank), which used to put gold away during the run, is hidden and out of play.
healing
Regen waits three seconds after every hit (regen_delay), so it heals the gap between fights, not the middle of one; lifesteal heals at most 4% of max HP a second (lifesteal_cap) however much damage a build pours out. The Heart gives a fifth of the bar, the Healing Well 35%. Staying alive is about not being hit.
How this got here (history)
  • The gold revive. Down! offered Revive · 150 gold (a flat price out of the run's gold, greyed when the run could not pay) until pass 77, and a dim dbg revive button in the corner; the free revive moved to the developer menu.
  • Gold left in the sand (v0.30): only an extraction banked the run's gold, and only what a Strongbox had put away survived a death; the results struck the lost sum through. Pass 70 made every end bank the purse and retired the Strongbox.
  • What killed you (v0.23.2) began as the middle of the revive screen for everyone, with emoji marks (💀 the killing hit, 🏹 ranged, 🗡️ melee, 👑 a boss); pass 46 moved it behind the hit-log switch and painted badges.
  • Healing cut back (v0.21). A build could out-heal the desert: Mend ticked 2.4 HP a second for nothing, Thirst turned 8% of a laser's damage (forty creatures at once) into health, the heart gave back a third of the bar and Astro patched 8% of it every level, so past the fourth minute the hero simply could not die. The regen delay and the lifesteal cap came in then; Mend went to 0.45 a rank, Thirst to 1.5%, the heart to a fifth, Astro to 5% a level, the well to 35%, Vigour to 8% max HP a rank. The crowd's own numbers did not change.
  • The Phoenix Feather (v0.34, one free revive) climbed the rarity ladder in pass 95 (rungs of health, Evolved a second life) and became one-shot again in pass 100. Its farewell (the plate rising in embers under a banner, "(used)" in grey) dates from v0.40.

Knockback

on the hero
A regular hit never pushes him (pass 88; Garry: "Remove the Knockback from being hit for Player entirely"): any creature's bite, a boss's included, and a plain shot. Special attacks push him at half (pass 89; Garry: "make it so the special attacks still knock player back. Like rocketeer rockets, dynamite, boss attacks, etc. but less than before. The regular melee attacks shouldn't have a knockback still"): charges, every hostile blast, rockets, the dynamite, slams, eruptions, boss attacks, the Sniper's beam and shots with a weight of their own, all at the map's hero_special_knock (0.5). One shove lands, then the next second's are ignored (KNOCK_GRACE), and the Shield power-up nulls them all. The debug switch "Full hero knockback" plays every push whole.
from the hero
Every push his side gives (weapons, perks, the Nuke, a throw) is multiplied by hero_knockback_mult (0.5 since pass 72), then by the creature's own knockback_resist, and in Endless by its growing resistance (above).
being hurt
The HP bar jiggles, never the screen (pass 82). His own moves (the dash, the Thruster's lunge, a leap, a teleport) and the two pulls (the harpoon's leash, the silk line) still move him.
How this got here (history)

v0.40, getting hit. Every creature hit 25% harder (creature_damage_mult 1.25, still the map's number), and a hit threw the hero back by the creature's own knockback, set from its damage (skeleton 2.9 m/s, knight 3.4, fire ant 4.8, mummy 6.4, beetle 6.8, bosses 7; bosses and Elites 1.6 times as hard). The stick went quiet for 0.12 s, the screen's edges flashed red, the game stopped for 45 ms and sand puffed at his feet. Pass 88 took every push off him, pass 89 gave the special attacks half back. The same v0.40 pass made ranged weapons fire only at what is on the screen and brought the crowd mostly from the top of the screen (both below).

On the map

where they come from
A burst arrives on a screen side drawn by weight, 19 to 24 m out (spawn_sides; Red Canyon: top 30, bottom, left and right 20 each), unless its row says ring or in front (section 09).
spawn rise
Every spawn grows out of the ground (pass 73, spawn_rise): 0.45 s, 0.9 s for a big one, with dust. It is not a target until it is 70% up, though area damage hits it.
despawn
Garry (pass 95): "in Vampire Survivors certain enemies despawn if the player walks far enough from them ... only regular enemies ... out of Player's view ... they leave nothing behind." Every half second a regular creature more than 40 m from the hero, off the screen and older than 6 s vanishes: no death, blood, XP, drop or kill (timeline_data.despawn; absent = these defaults). Its row spawns again near him. Never the boss, an Elite, a special type, a coloured special, a boss's summon, or anything held, roped, in the air or rising. The older move-up (a creature far behind put back ahead of him) stays for the kinds the rule never takes.
what the weapons aim at
The nearest special, Elite or boss within 10 m first (pass 73, target_priority; a spread volley sends only its first shot there). Ranged weapons fire only at what is on the screen, with a metre of margin; homing ones (the Rocket Pod, Killer Bees, the seekers) still hunt off it. Settings' Debug panel turns the screen rule off.
water
Two or three oases and a dry river, rolled per run from Run.map_seed (oases: 2, a third one 35% of the time). Wading slows walking by 10% (pass 80, water_slow), never a shove or a pull.
barrels
Thirty-five wooden barrels, placed by the run's seed a few metres apart, clear of the rocks, the water and the structures, and never behind a painting (the camera looks north from above, so each painting hides the ground just north of its foot). Walk into one and it bursts into splinters and dust and spills XP gems. The rare black ones (22% of them, plus two that are always black) hold three times the gems and, since pass 87, 1 to 5 coins (Garry: "The darker barrels should now give between 1-5 coins"); the creatures' first coins of the run hold that much back (40% of each kill's coins until the bill is paid), so smashing them all pays what the kills paid before.
signs and wind
Signposts point the way to structures. Gusts of dust sweep across the sand every ten seconds or so: a look and nothing else.
far zoom
When speed and level pull the camera out past twice its distance, every creature gets a dark-red rim up to two screen pixels thick and a faint warm tint, fading in between 2x and 2.4x (v0.23.2, a uniform on the creatures' billboard shader set from CameraRig.zoom()).
How this got here (history)
  • Where they come from: until v0.40 the crowd came mostly ahead of where the hero faced (kept as the facing mode); v0.40 brought 55% from the top and 15% from each other side; Red Canyon's weights are Garry's since.
  • Barrels (v0.30) were forty-odd, then thirty-three, then thirty-five with two always black (pass 87). The rare ones were light wood until pass 48 painted them black. The tumbleweeds rolling in the wind are hidden since v0.30, their code kept.

Chests and the reel

A chest pauses the game: the chest drops in, jiggles three times with a knock on the lid, cracks open with light leaking out, throws its lid back and sinks away as a reel of item icons spins up out of it (WChest, about a second; a tap skips to the spin). The reel spins for four seconds, fast at first and crawling at the end with a little bounce onto the last cell, and lands on what the chest holds; a tap hurries it four times over.

who drops them
Every Elite and coloured Elite, always. Every boss. A coloured special 35% of the time. Regular creatures rarely, by a chest chance in their drop table: a skeleton one time in 357, a mummy one in 71, a beetle one in 48, a Hammer Giant one in 12.
what they hold
Decided before the reel spins: an evolution if a weapon is ready (level 5 and all three of its path mutations), otherwise one weighted draw (section 10, the odds) over the next level of everything held and every new passive item (a new item at a little under half the weight of an upgrade), otherwise 50 gold. The reel is honest: the result is fixed when the chest opens and the spin is the reveal.
take, skip, banish
Take it applies it. Skip and Banish spend the same per-stage charges as the level-up cards (two skips and one banish a stage, more from the Upgrade board); with no skip left a chest must be taken. Banish strikes the item from the run and the reel spins again; it is locked on an evolution, on gold, on an item the hero already holds, and on any mutation; the header says why in plain words.
pause
The reel runs on the paused game like the card screen; a pending level-up waits until the chest is closed, and after the last one the game waits for a tap before it resumes. Two chests arriving together queue for one reel.
How this got here (history)
  • The chest's look. Modelled in Blender in v0.15 (iron bands, a barrel lid on a hinge that breathed and swung open); v0.17 added a breathing gold ring on the sand and a star glint off the lock; v0.19 made it two thirds the size in dark walnut and black iron; v0.23, after Megabonk's chests, gave it the shine (additive quads on one small shader) and dropped the ring. v0.32, after Garry's animated-chest reference, made it open before the reel spins; v0.33 repainted it in the icons' style (thick black lines, flat colours) and opened it on the lower part of the screen; coins everywhere are a painted coin (WCoin), a third plainer since v0.33.
  • Sell and the old rules. Until v0.33 a chest could be sold for coins (Garry: no more coins for refusing); v0.33 added Skip. v0.44 forbade banishing a held item or a boss chest's path mutation and added UPGRADE / NEW, the stat lines and the brass shine. An evolution once needed level 8 and two mutations.

Structures

Small landmarks to walk to. Each is single use: stand inside its ring and a wedge fills on the ground over three seconds (the Teleport one and a half; a dark hand on its leading edge, so it reads on any ground); step out and it drains. A used structure switches to its spent painting: the well runs dry, the cache stands open, the crystal goes dark, the shop shutters, the drum skin tears, the magnet rusts, the Teleport's runes die, a Totem Pole turns grey. Every structure is painted 2.5D and stands on a ground shadow.

  • The draw: nine a map (structures.count), at least 22 m apart: every drawn kind once, the rest by weight.
  • Standing apart from the draw (params.per_map): three Teleports (four in a third of the runs), at least 40 m apart; ten to twelve Totem Poles spread over the map.
  • Each structure is a folder under content/structures/ (effect kind, numbers, weight, colour, painting); a new effect needs one more branch in Game.use_structure.

Pickups

Gems never fuse and never vanish (v0.47; Garry: "The EXP gems still disappear. Now they combine into a big gem. I'd prefer that they don't disappear ever, but don't affect the performance negatively at all."). The pool grows as it needs to, and a gem lying on the sand costs nothing a frame: it is found through a coarse grid only when a magnet reaches its square. A big pull throws every gem on the screen up in an arc and streams the far ones in from just past the screen's edge; past that stream's limit a far gem is counted when it would have arrived, unseen, so no XP is lost. Gems are faceted octahedrons; the medium and large ones (5 and 25 XP) glint now and then; gems and coins draw under every painted figure (the stencil buffer).

Power-ups drop from kills. Every kill rolls the map's per-kill chance (0.45% on Red Canyon) times the creature's XP worth (an Elite's doubled), and the rarer power-ups weigh more the harder the creature (pickups.roster, a hard_bias each). Mutations

Every weapon has three mutations, cut the same way each time (the bee rule, after the Hornet Nest that started it): an Element that makes the weapon carry fire or poison, a Volume that gives a lot more of it for a little less each, and a Motion that changes what it does in the world.

  • Offered as a level-up when due (above). Mutations never sit among the three cards. So that a minute still brings as many three-card level-ups, levels come faster (xp_need_mult 1.65), and what grows with the hero's level (the creatures' level scaling, his trait, Barrel Rush's gems, Ram, Supply Drop) counts only his card levels, his level less his mutation level-ups (Run.card_level; the map's mutation_levels_scale counts them all). (Run.mutlvl_tick, mutlvl_claim, mutlvl_take, Run.next_path_weapon, CardPool.path_offer, Game._mutlvl_open.)
  • One per weapon per stage (Run.mutation_stage, Run.can_mutate); the boss chest's pick and the Stranger's loophole (at 20% of max HP) go past it.

  • Never banished, anywhere, hero mutations included.
  • Evolutions keep them, and need all three (below).

Evolutions

A weapon at level 5 that carries all three of its path mutations evolves at the next chest (a hero's own mutation is never needed): the reel lands on the evolved form, and Take it swaps the weapon out. The evolved form keeps the mutations (a Killer Swarm that twisted still twists), gets a gold-trimmed mesh and cannot level further. One evolution per weapon, listed with its weapon above. Elements: poison and fire

Poison is a debuff creatures carry: they turn bright green and take a tick of damage every two seconds, with a green-white flicker and a puff so it is never quiet, and only a nudge of knockback. A poisoned creature keeps one entry per item: the same item hitting again resets the clock instead of stacking, a different poison item adds its own. Levelling a poison item raises its tick damage and, at a couple of levels, adds a stack; never the speed. The base pace is deliberate: a saw's worth of damage takes a poison ten seconds. Poison weapons prefer creatures that are not poisoned yet, so the debuff spreads instead of piling up on one creature.

fire, the design

Fire is poison's loud cousin. Where poison is patient (a small tick every two seconds for ten seconds), fire is a burst: a creature that catches fire takes 4 damage every 0.6 seconds for 1.8 seconds, three ticks and done, 12 damage in all against poison's 15. Hotter per second, gone sooner, worth less on one body. Its worth is in the crowd: every tick, every burning creature rolls to set alight each creature it is touching (the two bodies' radii plus a hand's width). A fire lit by a weapon jumps with the weapon's fire_spread chance, 50% at level one; a fire caught from another creature is a child fire and jumps at 40% of that, so 20%, and so do its children, so a pack goes up like a fuse and then peters out at the edges. A child fire never jumps back to the creature it came from. Bosses do not catch fire from a neighbour (they can be lit directly).

The rules match poison's where it matters. One entry per item on a creature: the same item hitting a burning creature resets its clock; a second fire item adds its own. Levelling a fire item raises its tick damage and, at a couple of levels, its spread chance or its burn time; never the tick rate. Fire weapons aim at creatures that are not burning yet, so the flames go where there is something left to burn.

You can wield both (v0.26): the Molotov and the Skull Rain can burn and poison the same crowd. Each item still carries its element in its data (element on a passive, derived from fire_damage / poison_damage on a weapon, Run.element_of): a fire bomb looks for the crowd that is not burning yet, a poison bomb for the one that is not poisoned.

Bombs spread (v0.26). Every bomb weapon, and every bomb of a volley, picks a crowd no other bomb is headed for or came down on in the last two seconds (ProjectileManager.claimed_spots, EnemyManager.densest_point); they double up only when there is no other crowd in range, and then land round it. With the Grenade, the Molotov and the Skull Rain against four packs, the share of bombs landing within two metres of another fell from 47% to 24%. The Singularity's black holes take part too. Since pass 79 the dynamite and the Golden Pineapple are thrown where their crowd will be when they blow (params.lead).

How it looks. A burning creature turns ember red and flickers, tongues of flame lick up it the whole time it burns (camera-facing quads in one pooled MultiMesh, a small cel fire shader after Minionsart's stylised fire and the papaya-games devlog's toon bands: a teardrop mask eaten by two scrolls of baked noise, three hard bands of pale gold, orange and dark red), each damage tick flashes it orange (poison's tick is green, a plain hit white), and catching fire flares three tongues with a ring of sparks and a match-strike sound. The Molotov leaves a black oil spill, see-through (55% at the middle fading to nothing at its lobed rim), with tongues of fire licking over it; the poison puddle is a muted see-through green.

Each of these adds one reusable hook in the systems (a stat the sheet carries but a system consumes), so the data can vary them and later items can reuse them:

crit (The Eye)
crit_chance and crit_damage on the player sheet. When a weapon hit lands, EnemyManager.damage rolls the chance and multiplies the damage (×2 by default); poison and fire ticks never crit. A crit squashes the creature harder and pops a gold flash. Every hero starts with 1% crit. The Eye (id deadeye) is a card, four levels of +8% (+32%), evolving into The Eye Risen (+45% chance and crits for ×2.5).
execute (Guillotine)
execute_frac. After a weapon hit that leaves a creature alive but below the fraction of its max HP, the creature dies anyway, with a red burst. Bosses are exempt (Elites are not). It is the answer to the fat late creatures.
thorns (The Thorn)
thorns. On every hit the hero takes, every creature within 2.3 m takes that damage and a knockback. The tank's hook: nothing for a kiting build, everything for a hero who stands in the crowd. The Thorn card (id bristle), The Thorn Risen, the Thorns mutation and the Fire Eater carry it.
adrenaline (Adrenaline)
adrenaline_speed and adrenaline_might per stack. Run.add_kill adds a stack (twenty at most) and restarts a 4 s clock; when the clock runs out all stacks go together. The stacks fold into the sheet as one "Adrenaline ×n" line, rebuilt at most four times a second.
overkill (Overkill)
overkill. When a hit takes a creature below zero, the overflow times the fraction is dealt in a 2.5 m radius around the body, once (a splash cannot splash). It turns big single hits into crowd damage.
ricochet (Ricochet)
ricochet. When a straight shot (never a lob, never a missile) kills, it rolls the chance and fires one more shot of the same kind from the body at the nearest creature not yet hit, at 60% damage, and that shot cannot bounce again: bounded by construction.
homing agility
homing_turn, a multiplier on the turn rate of every missile and bee. Its passive (Guidance Fins) was folded into the mutations long ago; the hook stays.
weapon tags
A weapon's JSON may carry tags (homing, explosive), and an effect may aim at a tag (target: "tag:homing") the way it aims at one weapon or all of them; the player's sheet never folds such effects into the hero's own numbers. Since pass 103 the explosive tag also exempts a weapon's pushes from Endless's knockback resistance.
XP gain (The Sage)
xp_gain, a multiplier applied in Run.add_xp. It decides whether a run is card-rich or card-poor. The Sage (id field_notes) is a card, evolving into The Sage Risen.
How this got here (history)

Until v0.13 every passive was a number on the stat sheet, and a level-up read like a spreadsheet; seven hooks came in then. Ricochet replaced Trigger Happy in v0.14 (it took a slice off every cooldown per kill with no ceiling, so in a crowd every weapon became a hose). Cactus Coat (thorns) became a line and Guidance Fins and Payload (an extra missile for the homing tag) were folded into mutations in the paths rework. The same v0.13 balance pass fixed Multishot's levels that did nothing and raised the Tommy Gun's rounds to 3; in v0.14 Astro's suit lost half its armour (two armour took a third off a goblin's bite in the first minute). Guillotine had three levels ("21% is already a fifth of every creature's health") until pass 95's ladder gave it four.

The spawning language

Since pass 84 everything that spawns regular creatures is a roster row in timeline_data.roster (Garry: "better add too many controls than too few"); the events keep only the bosses and the Elites. A row opens at t and closes at until, and says:

pace
continuous (drawn by weight whenever the cap has room, in bursts of 3 to 12 every 0.7 s), waves (a pack every N seconds, or a fresh gap in [min, max] each time) or once (one wave of count).
creature
One id, a list with enemy_weights, or "roster" (one drawn from the rows live at that second).
pattern
pack (together at one spot), scatter (one by one), ring (evenly round the hero 11.7 m out, or the classic even ring of ring_radius), in front (from the top of the screen whatever the sides say), or lone.
flags
elite (every creature wears a modifier, or a random one), special (every creature comes as its coloured special), banner (a toast such as SWARM!), chance and t_jitter (rolled once a run), respect_cap and respect_hard_cap, channel (regular or hell), an art variant, stats overrides, presence keyframes (weights), size and colour ranges.
role and name
The editor's colour, grouping and label (fodder, swarm, tank, ranged, elite, special); the game ignores them.
the mix
A row naming one creature, with no elite, no special and not once, is part of the mix: only the mix answers "who is on the roster now" (the portal wave, a coloured Elite's draw, the numbers a once row borrows).

Old maps' swarm / elite / elite_every events and waves[] still play; the Level Editor converts them to rows on load and tools/migrate_timeline.py converts a file. A map may also set floor_curve, a least-alive density floor; Red Canyon sets none. The whole schema is docs/waves_schema.md.

How many: the caps

live cap
How much may be alive, counted in load: a creature counts its cap_share (a skeleton half). Red Canyon's cap_curve is Garry's 500 all the way, and cap_max 150 cuts it, so the live cap is 150 load (the Level Editor's lane says "Cap (load)" since pass 102). Continuous rows and cap-respecting waves spawn only into free room.
hard cap
300 bodies (hard_cap), whatever their load; rows with respect_hard_cap: false (the swarms, the Special Goblin) come on top, stopped only by the engine's body limit (320). Garry's phone held 60 fps up to 300 creatures; pass 102 fixed the slowdown that came after that in late Endless.
hell cap
The hell channel spawns into its own room: hell_cap × the live cap, less the hell load ([0, 0.3, 0.4] by stage; only Blood Moon has hell rows).
while the boss lives
The live cap drops to 0.6 of itself (boss_cap_frac). After it dies, the portal wave (12) and then nothing.
live limits
A creature JSON may say max_alive: its row is skipped while that many stand (the Hammer Giant 3; the harpooner, the Vulture Raider and the Saiga Sniper 1).

How strong: the numbers

A creature's HP = its base × the stage multiplier × the HP curve at its row's start × (1 + 0.05 × (hero card level − 1)) × creature_hp_mult 1.3, then the row's own stats. Its damage is the same with the damage curve, 0.03 a level and creature_damage_mult 1.25.

stage multipliers
stage_mults [1, 2.2, 3.34] on HP, damage and the coins' worth (a coin pays ×2 in Nightfall, ×3 in Blood Moon); creatures are 10% faster a stage (speed_per_stage).
the HP curve
A power formula on the map clock, (1 + 0.0977 × minutes)1.25: ×1.94 at 7:10, ×3.42 at 17:10, ×5.56 at 30:10 (hp_curve).
the damage curve
Points (damage_curve): ×1.21 through Sundown; Nightfall from ×0.36 rising to ×0.95 at 900 s and ×0.8 at its end; Blood Moon from ×0.28 to ×0.72 at 1700 s and ×0.56 at its end. The openings stay soft so a fresh hero can find his feet.
fixed per row
Garry (v0.44): "While I want the HP of enemies to increase over time, I don't actually want it to change for the same 'wave' of enemies on the fly." Every creature of a row gets the curves read at the row's start and the hero-level factor from its first spawn, so the twentieth skeleton of a row dies to the same shot as the first. A row that sets its own HP or damage takes no curve. In Endless each wave fixes its numbers afresh.
everyone's pace
move_pace (0.9 on Red Canyon, Garry's hand) moves the hero, every creature, charges and rolls; hostile shots, rockets, boulders and rains fly at it too and every red warning lasts 1/move_pace as long, so dodging stays fair. creature_speed_mult 0.9 on top for the creatures.
one touch
The most one bite takes is contact_cap (0.22) of the hero's max HP.

Specials and special types

specials
The coloured ones: a creature with a variance.special block may roll one, once the run has 50 kills (colour_after_kills). A special is ×1.5 size, ×15 HP, ×2 damage and ×3 XP, with a glowing rim in a colour Garry sets per creature, and drops a chest 35% of the time; at most three alive (special_defaults). A forced special (a row with special ticked, such as the Special Goblin at 2:00) always comes. Garry (pass 72): "Specials should be more noticeable. They should be bigger ... higher HP", "way more of a chance of dropping a box", "There should be a limit to how many of them are there".
special types
Creatures with a trick of their own and a gear badge, on a clock of their own (specials): from 215 s in Sundown (from the first second of a later stage run), whenever none is alive, one arrives after a fresh 64 to 86 s. The pool: the Vulture Raider, the Saiga Sniper and, from Nightfall, the Harpoon Troglodyte. Never Elites, never in Endless, never despawned.
elite modifiers
Explosive (blows up when it dies), Shielded, Speedy and Splitter (splits in four), each with its own outline and a 1.4 to 1.6 size; worn by a coloured Elite.

Reading a charge

Every mark on the ground that means damage is red (v0.15): the bandit's landing ring, the dwarf's wedge, the harpooner's line, the beetle's charge, the Sniper's lines, the bosses' rings and fans, the toad's burst. Green is kept for the two things that are not an attack: your own poison clouds and the shaman's mend.

A charger's wind-up only warns you if you can see where it points. The Bull Beetle and the Dune Scorpion carry side and diagonal sets besides front and back, and while winding up or charging they face their charge direction rather than their drift. The charge direction is fixed the instant the red line appears and the creature runs that line even if the hero has moved: the telegraph is what makes the dodge fair. Any creature gets eight directions by setting "directions": 8 in its JSON. Charges and rolls run longer at a slower pace so they still end where their red line ends.

Crowd behaviour

steering
Separation steering on a spatial grid, so a crowd flows around rocks and each other instead of stacking into one sprite. Pass 101 made it cheaper: finer separation cells (checked for no overlaps with twelve neighbours), a slower steer far from the hero, and only what is seen drawn.
despawn and move-up
Regular creatures far off and out of view despawn (section 07); the older move-up puts the kinds the rule never takes back ahead of the hero. The boss and the Elite are never culled.
every spawn rises
Out of the ground over 0.45 s (section 07).
outlines
A creature's outline shows only behind scenery (the stencil buffer, pass 72), so the crowd stays clean in the open and readable behind a saguaro.
