Art

Clean anime. Heroes, creatures, bosses, the scenery and the structures are painted 2.5D billboards; weapons, pickups, the ground, the fort wall, barrels and signs are 3D. Icons are one bold object each with a black outline, made to read at 72 px on a phone. All of it is painted on our own art server from words in the content files, and since pass 66 Garry queues every art job himself from the Level Editor.

What is 3D and what is a sprite

The rule: anything with a face, and anything that only has to stand still and be seen, is a painting; anything that spins, flies, arcs or must look right from above is 3D.

The hero's sprite never holds a weapon

Weapons float at marker points around the character: a hip point for the shotgun, a ring for the saws, an orbit for the pea pod and the dart. That keeps the sprite one image per pose and lets any weapon pair with any hero. A held gun fires from its muzzle as drawn (pass 95).

Looks: several finished sheets, one played

A creature or hero can carry several finished sheets at once, called looks (art variants), each with a code such as GOB-D. Garry compares them side by side in the debug arena (ENEMY and HERO tabs) or in the Level Editor's look grid, and the one the game plays is one word in the JSON, "variant". A look that has just landed is marked fresh until he approves it; one he does not want is shelved out of the chips. A roster row may name its own look (Red Canyon's r16 mummies wear DM-F).

Sprite animation

heroes
Pose-driven. A Mixamo motion is rendered as an OpenPose skeleton with hands from each of the five game views in Blender (tools/pose_driver.py), and Wan 2.2 Animate moves the painted still along it: 24 frames a stride at 36 fps, the rates kept in the hero's JSON. Every frame is measured and fitted to the standing figure's height, so idle, run and hit share one head height. Since pass 71 a hero can also walk on a 3D puppet of his own painting (section 15b). The method, with before-and-after clips of the Drifter, has its own page.
the hit
Painted, not driven. The image model edits each view's painting into a flinch (leaning back, shoulders hunched, a hand to the chest; Astro's visor kept opaque), fitted to the standing height; four frames at 12 fps wince around it. The Mixamo pain gesture never looked right on any hero.
deaths
Everyone dies on screen. A painted "dead" still (the front painting laid flat on its back) and a few frames of the standing painting tipping into it; the body holds, then sinks into the sand, 1.4 s from the killing blow to gone. Bodies stop blocking shots and steering the instant they fall, and since pass 82 every kill throws its body along the hit. Other deaths: an animated fall between the two paintings ("death": "clip"), a Mixamo death through Wan Animate, a squash, a collapse into a heap, a shatter into about fourteen pieces (pass 91), and a 3D model's own death clip. shadow.die_drop sinks a death strip (the Red Goblin 0.2, pass 88).
creatures
Humanoids walk on fitted Mixamo drivers like the heroes (the goblin on the Orc Walk, the runt on the plain walk, the mummies on raised-arm drivers). Creatures with a 3D model walk on that model's depth video through Wan Fun Control (the scorpion, the beetle, the fire ant, the spider). The rest walk on free image-to-video clips cut to a loop, or on no video at all (a hop, or a lean and a bob, for blobs and tumbleweeds).

Painted scenery, and reading through it

painting a prop
tools/gen_props.py on Flux.2 dev with the Turbo LoRA, strictly one render at a time (the model and its 18 GB text encoder do not fit the server's memory twice: the tool unloads them after every render and restarts ComfyUI when the memory stays full). Greenery is painted on a blue screen and the blue agave on a green one, so the keyer never eats the prop's own colour. Every render is checked for enclosed holes and, by the vision model, for a face (the first prickly pear had eyes and read as a creature), and painted again on either. props.json records each prop's height and collider radius (62% of the painted foot, so the hero brushes past a cactus).
variants
Painted variants (pass 83, tools/gen_prop_variants.py): Klein from the style anchor and the original's raw render, picked by eye over several rounds (agave_b, barrel_cactus_b, ocotillo_b, boulder_b, desert_bush_b, dead_shrub_b, joshua_tree_b/c, the new brittlebush; pass 85 added fan_palm_b and the lopsided joshua_tree_d). props.json variant_of and weight register one; PropField picks by a hash of the spot, so every seed's layout and colliders stand.
tints
A natural random tint per spot (pass 85): props.json tint per kind (a hue window, saturation and value ranges), applied in prop_billboard.gdshader; outlines and greys are untouched and no draw call is added.
ground shadows
Every painting, barrel, sign and structure stands on a soft shadow (pass 83): ground_shadow.gdshader, its size per painting in props.json shadow (measured by tools/prop_shadows.py, tuned by eye), leaning away from the paintings' upper-left light. All of the scenery's shadows are one draw.
the sand
Five sands (pass 82, tools/gen_sand.py: Klein paintings described from Garry's reference in words, the seam repainted, laid over the old sand's mean colour): the old one, soft dunes, dunes with pebbles, wind ripples, ripples with pebbles. The map's sand key picks one; Red Canyon plays ripples with pebbles (Garry's pick, pass 83). The Level Editor's World card and the arena's WORLD tab switch it.
wall and water
The fort wall and the cliffs are Klein paintings made seamless and neutral grey (tools/gen_wall_textures.py, pass 80), coloured by the map's rock_color and wall_colors. The oases are a cheap water shader (water_lake.gdshader, pass 81) with damp earth and green round them.
the title
A composed scene (pass 83): art/ui/title_scene.json clears a box round the hero and lays the paintings by hand. Since pass 85 it stands on the map's own sand and draws one of six presets per visit; tools/menu_backdrop.gd rewrites the menus' backdrop from it.
the Totem Pole
Each gift's carved segment (pass 83, tools/gen_totem_icons.py): a head painted on green in the pole's style, keyed, laid on one cedar block; the crest repainted the same way (art/ui/totem/).

How the paintings sit in the world. Each prop is an instance in one MultiMesh per painting (core/systems/PropField.gd), a quad pivoting on its base that faces the camera exactly like the creatures (prop_billboard.gdshader). Props and characters are drawn opaque with an alpha cut rather than blended, so the depth buffer sorts them per pixel: a goblin in front of a cactus covers it, one behind is covered, and because every quad is tilted the same way towards the lens the planes never cut through each other. Greenery sways a few degrees at the top; rocks and bones do not.

The pipeline in short

  1. Words. Every content JSON carries an art_prompt (heroes also portrait_prompt, anim_hint, anim_negative), and each look a recipe with its method and settings. The script wraps the words in the house style.
  2. Stills. ComfyUI on the R9700 server paints a front with Flux.2 Klein (about 20 s) or Flux.2 dev (about 4 min), then the other views as edits of the front so the character stays the same.
  3. Motion. Heroes: pose drivers into Wan 2.2 Animate, or a 3D puppet of the painting. Creatures: a fitted Mixamo driver, a 3D model's depth video into Wan Fun Control, a free image-to-video clip cut to a loop, or no video at all.
  4. Check. Deterministic checks first (empty frames, jumps, seams, foreign colours, the silhouette against the driver), then an eye: Gemini through Garry's subscription since pass 98, the local Qwen2.5-VL in LM Studio when a Gemini call fails. Neither counts legs reliably, so every look is still looked at by a person.
  5. Cut. Key the screen colour, drop specks and ground shadows, lift contrast, add the outline (heavier on creatures than on heroes), fit the cell, pack the grid strip, write the frame counts and rates back into the JSON. Hero portraits are edits of the front still set in a round badge.
  6. Land. A Level Editor job writes the look's entry in place (fresh), the frames into the frames store, and imports it into Godot; Garry compares it in play and approves.
  7. The creature table. Every enemy JSON carries a doc line; the bible's creature table is generated from the content folders and the map's roster, so a creature cannot be added without appearing here.
Pose hints: the still decides the cycle

The pose-driven run keeps whatever the still's arms were doing (Jeżyk's fists stayed at his chest; Dad's back view showed his face). A character's JSON may carry pose_hint (every standing view), back_hint and side_hint, appended to the view prompts. The rule: arms hanging, nothing raised, no face from behind, the same build in every view. No checker catches a face-forward back view, so every re-rendered still is looked at before the sheet packs.

The launcher icon is the Drifter's hat lying on the sand (the plainest of four, picked by Garry), painted on Flux.2 dev with the anchor and his front painting as references (tools/gen_icon_variants.py, tools/gen_app_icon.py); its sand was lightened in pass 61 (tools/icon_sand.py). The lessons are in its prompts: the subject inside the middle two thirds for the launcher's mask, no text or frame (the painted frame the model adds is cropped off), a portrait edited from the hero's own painting so the face holds, and nothing named that the model would draw literally. Section 15b walks through all of it step by step; docs/art_guide.html (Garry's guide to every art method) is the deeper reference.

How this got here (history)
  • Skeletons were pose-driven first (v0.23.4): a creature JSON with "pose" names a driver folder and gen_art.py paints its walks on it; the skeletons' driver was the Mixamo walk with both arms pinned forward (pose_driver.py ... arms=zombie). They left the roster in v0.23.5 and came back later as SK-A and KN-A, arms down.
  • The goblin and the runt moved from free clips to fitted humanoid drivers in v0.23.9 (Mixamo's orc walk and plain walk, refitted to a big head and short legs with pose_driver.py body=...), and their deaths became an animated fall ("death": "clip").
  • The launcher icon: until v0.13 it was Astro's portrait badge on a gradient, whose mirrored visor read as a black disc at launcher size; then a painted Astro illustration (chest up, a thumbs-up, the visor reflecting a sun and a cactus); then in v0.23.4/v0.23.5 the Drifter's hat (variant A, picked over the hat against a low sun) and his serious portrait (variant B), each from four variants on lettered sheets, installed with --use hat=X drifter=Y.
  • Scenery: since v0.12 every prop and structure is painted on Flux.2 dev one render at a time, each looked at, with a spent painting for a used structure; colliders at 62% of the foot since v0.13. The boxy 3D landmarks went in v0.10; Red Canyon (v0.11) kept nothing in 3D but the ground and the mountains.
  • Outlines through scenery used an inverted depth test until pass 72, which also outlined a creature behind another creature; the stencil replaced it at the same cost.
  • Checking: until pass 98 the only eye was Qwen2.5-VL in LM Studio, which re-rolled a failed render up to three times with a new seed and kept its grade in <id>_ai.json.

How the art is made

Nobody draws the cast by hand. Every hero, creature and icon starts as words in its data file; AI models on our own art server paint it and make it move, checks and an AI eye look at every frame, a Python script cuts it out, and it lands in Godot as a sheet of frames. Since pass 66 Garry runs all of it from the Level Editor: pick a method, fill the recipe, queue the job, compare the look in play, approve it. Why green and blue backgrounds

Every still is painted on a flat, bright screen colour that the scissors can take away cleanly, like a film's green screen. Green things (goblins, cacti) go on blue, blue things on green, and heroes on blue, because olive, khaki and brown clothes sit too close to green. For the video clips the script picks whichever screen is furthest from the figure's own colours: a hero in blue jeans came back with no legs on blue and green legs on green, so he goes on magenta. A look's screen: auto picks blue when the figure leans green, and since pass 91 the keyer no longer cuts a green figure's feet on a green screen (the cast-shadow cut is off when the figure wears the screen's colours).

The Level Editor runs the art

Every art job goes through one queue. On the Enemies, Heroes or Items tab Garry picks a target, adds a look, chooses a method and fills its recipe; the job joins the Jobs tab. The server runs one ComfyUI job at a time (the ComfyUI lock), each as a subprocess through tools/asset_job.py, and merges the result into the look's entry in place, marked fresh. The page is live (pass 97): cards update in place with ComfyUI's node progress, and a reload reopens the job.

  1. Pick a method

    From the catalogue below (methods.json): each card says what it paints, how long it takes and where it goes wrong.

  2. Fill the recipe

    The words, the references, the drivers, the painter and its steps, the death, the screen. Empty boxes show the value that applies and where it comes from.

  3. Queue it, pick what it asks for

    Candidate jobs stop for a pick on the job card (an A-pose, a side painting, an icon); the rest run through.

  4. Look at it in play

    The look lands fresh: compare it in the arena or the look grid, fix frames in the Frames view, then set it as the played look.

Checking, repairing and the art-production loop

A look is checked frame by frame before it can play. Three layers sit on the queue (passes 95-98); none of them can put a look into the game without Garry's one approval.

the Frames view
Pass 95. Every frame of every look, with every version ever made of it (labels like SP-A / Run / Side / Frame 07 / v2), frames copied from other looks, several changes staged, then one Export behind a confirmation. Pins are re-applied at every landing; no version is ever deleted.
VACE repair
Pass 95. Wan 2.2 Fun VACE repaints chosen frames from the kept ones, the driver and the painting (tools/vace_repair.py). Each method class has a benchmarked window profile in methods.json repair.profile; a repair that fails the checks escalates from a single window to a double one, then to re-rolling the view.
art requests

The Puppet method: a 3D figure of the painting

A hero whose body is not a Mixamo man walks on a 3D copy of himself. A flat stick figure carries a man's proportions, so a big-headed or long-limbed hero comes out the wrong size from view to view. The Puppet method (pass 71, the Level Editor's hero_puppet, tools/puppet.py + tools/puppet_blender.py) builds the guide from the painting itself. Its explainer: the Puppet explainer (source in docs/puppet/).

  1. An A-pose

    Klein (or Flux.2 dev) edits the look's front still into an arms-out A-pose (candidates; one picked on the job card), then the side and the back the same way.

  2. A 3D shape

    Hunyuan3D 2 multi-view turns the three into a mesh on the server (about 90 s a try, ComfyUI's built-in nodes).

  3. A rig and a run

    Blender cleans it into one piece, rigs it with Mixamo bone names and retargets a Mixamo clip onto it; the GLB is previewed in the page.

  4. Guides from the puppet

    Its own stick figures and depth renders in the five views, sized from the 3D model (run_fit), framed by the soles, the face kept to the viewer; depth or stick per view.

  5. Walk the painting on them

    Wan walks each view's painting along the puppet's guide, as in hero_pose5.

How Solenne was made

Garry's own turnaround is her art; only what it lacks was painted. He handed over the sun-headed woman front and back on transparency, and a Mixamo walk he picked for her (Walking_sun.fbx, one stride with a sway and both arms swinging wide). The front of the picture is her title painting and her badge as it is (tools/make_solenne_static.py), and the two halves on the blue screen are her front and back stills; Klein painted the side and three-quarter views as edits of the front, the hurt stills, the cheer and the fall. The walk became a 24-frame pose driver with the back views' wrists pushed clear of the body (pose_spread.py), and Wan Animate walked every view on it.

wide arms
Her arms swing so wide that the back and three-quarter-back figures touched the clip's edges, so those views' drivers were shrunk to 85% about the figure's middle (tools/pose_scale.py) and the frames scaled back to her height when packed.
glossy latex
Her black latex reflects the screen, and the keyer cut the reflections out, leaving hollow boots. Three keyer settings in her JSON fix it at the source: a screen-coloured pixel darker than 0.8 is figure (key_min_value, with key_sat 0.85), no cast-shadow cut (key_cut_shadow false), and the screen's colour held down in every figure pixel instead of unmixed (key_clamp_screen), so a reflection turns into grey shine.
the back hurt
The back-view hurt still turned her to face the camera (as Ironwood's had), so it was painted again as an edit of her back still, flinching from behind, four seeds, one picked.

Her walks, fitted to her body (Garry: walking down her hands were too large and her sun head grew and looked aside; walking up it shrank; walking sideways she seemed to walk up the screen with her rays split). Two causes: the stick figure was a man's, a third taller in the body than hers, so the video model painted her on his proportions; and the clip's forward was read off its feet, which a toes-out walk puts 20° wrong. The fix is in the tools: pose_driver.py ... fwd=body squares the views to her hips and shoulders, and tools/pose_fit.py scales the stick figure to her painted body (tools/pose_templates/solenne.json), pins the face on her sun disc so it faces the viewer and never tilts, and makes the hands smaller. Her side still is composed, the side painting's body under the three-quarter-front painting's head, because the image model drew the disc edge-on six times out of six. On those drivers she walks three ways: B on his walk (in play), C on Mixamo's catwalk, D on the heroes' run; SOL-F and SOL-G (pass 87) walk on her puppet.

Making art yourself: a step-by-step guide

The easy way is the Level Editor (tools\level_editor.cmd): every method below is a card on its Enemies, Heroes or Items tab, and docs/art_guide.html (Garry's art guide) explains each one, its settings and where things show in the game. The scripts underneath, for the terminal (the same guide as ART_ASSET_PIPELINE.md in the repo); the game never reads a prompt, the prompts are what these scripts paint from.

you need
The ComfyUI art server (192.168.137.54:8188) switched on, Python, and Blender 5.2 for anything built from a 3D model. One ComfyUI job at a time: the Level Editor's queue, or tools/locks.py from a shell. After new art, Godot re-imports it (open the editor, or --import then import_json.gd); bash tools/ship.sh does both before it builds.
the one rule
Look at it yourself. Every script paints several candidates and writes a review sheet (review_*.png): pick by eye, then install the pick. No checker reliably catches extra ears, arms, tails and missing legs.
the words
In the item's JSON: art_prompt (what is painted), back_hint, view_hints, dead_hint, pose_hint (the other views), motion, anim_negative, anim_seed (the walk clip), variants.<CODE>.recipe (the same, for one look), outline_frac, outline_color, remap_hues (clean-up). Edit the words, run the script again, pick again.
enemies
python tools/gen_art.py --only skeleton --force repaints one from its JSON (front, back, dead, walk, fall, strip and icon); --variant=SK-B paints one look from its recipe, which you then compare in Debug · Arena → ENEMY → Art variants and switch to with "variant" in the JSON. Creatures with a 3D model walk the model in Blender first and paint along its depth video: tools/blender_depth_drivers.py (or the older blender_scorpion.py, blender_beetle.py), then tools/gen_ctl.py --id=dune_scorpion --variant=SC-A (side candidates, then --use-side=k1); the ant has tools/gen_ant.py. Hopping creatures ("walk": "hop") need no video. A floating shadow is the JSON's shadow block, not a repaint. About 20 minutes a creature on Klein, up to three hours for a 3D-driven one on Flux dev.
heroes
python tools/gen_art.py --only drifter: five views, a run in each driven by a Mixamo stride (pose_drivers in the hero JSON picks one per view), hit and dead paintings, the portrait icon. Paint the front as 4-6 candidates first: every other view is an edit of it. A puppet: python tools/puppet.py <hero>. Badges: tools/gen_badge_cands.py --id <hero> --seeds 4, then --pick k. The title painting: tools/gen_title_heroes.py --only drifter --seeds 4, then --pick drifter=2.
icons
python tools/gen_icon_cands.py --only beer --seeds 4, look at tools/dl/icons47/review_beer.png, then --pick beer=2 installs it into the item's folder. A new item is one entry in JOBS in that script. Anything green in the subject goes on the blue screen ("screen": "blue"), or the keyer eats it. Check each icon on its plate colour first. Weapons with a 3D model are painted from a render of it ("ref"). The same script paints badges, UI art (the coin) and the Results screen's source icons; the app icon has tools/gen_app_icon.py. The Level Editor never overwrites an icon and asks before changing a played look's.
props and structures
python tools/gen_props.py --only st_teleport --force (Flux dev by default, --klein for the fast model). The list and sizes are PROPS in that script; a used structure has a second painting st_<id>_off.png. Structures stay paintings, never 3D. Variants: tools/gen_prop_variants.py, then tools/prop_shadows.py for their shadows.
ground and walls
python tools/gen_sand.py (the sands), tools/gen_wall_textures.py (the fort wall and cliffs), tools/gen_textures.py (the older textures and the sky).
UI art
python tools/gen_ui_art.py --only mirror --seeds=4 then --pick (the Stranger, the Strongbox, the portal, the Mirror); the chest frames are a closed painting and two edits of it (cracked, open); badges tools/gen_badges.py; the totem segments tools/gen_totem_icons.py.
3D models
"$BLENDER" -b --python tools/blender_turret.py and the other blender_*.py scripts build the GLBs in game/art/props/, several from Garry's references in _REFERENCES/, each with a render the icon is painted from. For any FBX, GLB or OBJ: tools/blender_ref.py ... mode=probe|icon|views|all.
where it lands
Strips and icons in the content folder; raw paintings cached in tools/dl/ai_raw_anime/ (delete one to repaint it); clips in tools/dl/ai_anim/; drivers in tools/dl/drivers/ (previewed in the Level Editor's Drivers tab, pass 93); frame versions in tools/dl/frames/; jobs in tools/dl/jobs/; review sheets in tools/dl/review/, tools/dl/icons47/, tools/dl/title_heroes/; replaced art in tools/dl/backup/.

Sound

Cartoon western: boings, splats and twangs over casino-western jingles and upbeat arcade music in spaghetti-western colours. Every sound has an id that content files reference, a trigger and a prompt for a sound AI; almost every one is now a real recording from Stable Audio 3 on the art server, and one music track plays at a time.

direction
Nothing realistic. Hits are "bonk" and "splat", deaths are comedic, UI is wooden and warm. Saturday-morning cartoon foley over a spaghetti-western guitar. The UI and reward jingles are casino-western (the HYPE table in tools/ai_sfx.py).
the engine
Stable Audio 3 on the server's ComfyUI (tools/comfy_audio.py): the small model for effects, the medium one for music and jingles. tools/ai_sfx.py paints candidates per id, picks or takes --pick id=k, sets volumes and binds them in the sound map; tools/make_sfx_review.py builds the review page. Garry's own Stable Audio app (http://127.0.0.1:7861/) is the second engine, AudioGen the alternate.
synthesised
A few sounds are made in code because exact timing matters more than texture (tools/synth_sfx.py): the gem dings, the hero's hurt, the slot machine, the salt shaker, the decoy, the Saiga Sniper's aim, lock and shot.
mixing rules
Voice limits per id, so forty fire ants dying is one sound with a pitch spread. A non-killing hit sounds at most once every 120 ms per id (a crit always; pass 102). Player and UI always audible. Creature sounds attenuate with distance from the camera.
music
Six looping tracks (tools/ai_music.py, pass 64; Garry: "as close to MegaBonk as possible, but slightly more Western style"): the title, one run track per stage (mu_run_1 to mu_run_3, climbing in energy), the boss's over them while a boss lives, and the results. Each is cut to a seamless loop of whole bars and levelled. Audio.gd plays one track at a time, crossfading 1.5 s when the stage or the boss changes it.
placeholders
A brand-new id gets a synthesised placeholder from tools/gen_sfx.py so the game is never silent: correct in timing and volume, ugly on purpose. Five still play one: the basketball's throw, bounce and catch, the Timeout perk and the buzzer.

Tech & performance budgets

Godot 4.7.2 with the Compatibility renderer, held at 60 fps on a phone. The crowd is packed arrays drawn by one MultiMesh per creature type, only what the camera can see is drawn, and a slow frame slows the world instead of spiralling (passes 101-102).

pass 101-102 · the phone at 300 creatures

Garry's Pixel 8 Pro held 60 fps up to 300 creatures until late Endless, then fell to 15-27 fps: Endless creatures stop dying, crowd round the hero burning and poisoned, and once a frame passes 16.7 ms the 60 Hz physics catch-up spiralled. Pass 101 drew only what is seen and capped the frame rate; pass 102 capped the catch-up and made hits in a huge crowd cheaper. No damage, burn, poison or Endless number changed.

engine
Godot 4.7.2, GDScript. The Compatibility renderer for the widest Android reach. Phones render the 3D view at 80% and the UI at full size.
targets
Android first, on Garry's phone through a signed release APK in Google Drive, and a Windows exe beside it; PC for testing with WASD. A phone plays at a 60 fps cap (pass 101, Settings.frame_cap; the world moves 60 times a second, so a 120 Hz phone drew every picture twice), the PC at its screen's rate; the developer menu's Frame rate row switches it.
frame pacing
While the game plays, a long frame runs at most two world steps (Game.CATCH_UP_STEPS, pass 102; Godot's 8 in the menus), so an overloaded frame slows the world instead of spiralling: modelled on the pinned late-Endless crowd for a phone 2.2 times slower than the PC, about 13 fps became 24 fps at 79% world speed, and nothing at 30 fps or better slows. Every gameplay timer reads the frames' own clock (Run.play_clock), and core/systems/WorkClock.gd records each second's world steps and milliseconds into the run record.
creatures
The live cap counts load (a creature counts its cap_share): Red Canyon's cap curve is cut at cap_max 150; the hard cap counts bodies, 300; the arrays and MultiMeshes are sized for 320. Logic is data-oriented: positions, velocities, HP, burn and poison sources and frame index live in packed arrays, not one node per creature. A regular creature more than 40 m from the hero, off the screen and older than 6 s despawns with no drop (pass 95), and its row spawns again near him.
drawing the crowd
One MultiMeshInstance3D per creature type, billboarded in a shared shader, with the frame, flip, flash and squash as per-instance data. Since pass 101 only creatures whose whole drawn picture can reach the view are drawn (drawn_vis: a sphere round the quad at its own size, squash, lift and badge, and one round its shadow, against the camera's view grown by a border, held 6 ticks after leaving); proven on 304 captures with 0 differing pixels. On the PC at 300 creatures the crowd's draw went from 1.55 to 0.92 ms.
movement
Creatures are not physics bodies. Separation runs on its own grid of 1.25 m cells (pass 101; neighbours checked fell from about 49 to 15, with no one-sided overlap); the weapons' queries keep the 2.5 m grid. Half the crowd steers each tick; a creature that is not drawn steers every 4th tick and pushes at half rate for as long. Each creature checks only the obstacles whose circle reaches its cell. The hero is one CharacterBody3D against the terrain and the static colliders.
hits in a crowd
Pass 102. From 40 damage numbers in the air a hit joins its creature's number or is let go (crits always show; the hero's never thin); burns and poisons run their clocks lazily; flames are drawn every 0.18 s up to 80 burning creatures and none off screen; the hero's crit and execute numbers are read once a tick. Measured at Endless wave 48: 10.13 to 9.64 ms a frame on the PC.
art in memory
Pass 101. A look's strip loads only when a run will show it (core/data/LookArt.gd, core/systems/RunLooks.gd): boot no longer puts every look into the GPU (about 2.3 GB down to about 0.3 GB); the run's set is pinned under the spawn beam and other strips kept up to 160 MB.
effects
Every burst, puff, flame, spark and flash is an instance in one of three pooled MultiMeshes (400 particles, three draw calls, no nodes). Lightning bolts are ribbons in two shared meshes. Tracers are one MultiMesh of thin fading lines (the wisp, pass 80). Point lights on chests, structures and pickups are skipped on phones; the campfire and the portal keep theirs.
projectiles
Pooled 3D meshes, 400 of them. Straight shots hold their muzzle height above the ground; arcs ignore contact and explode where they land. Hit checks are sphere tests against the same grid, and a round hits a big body where it is drawn. Hostile shots drop a fading trail dot every 55 ms into a second pool.
pickups
Gems and coins are MultiMeshes over a growable pool (up to 2,048) with a grid of lying pickups, the magnet and the vacuum.
warm-up
The Compatibility renderer compiles a shader the first time something is drawn with it, a hitch on a phone. Under the spawn beam everything the run can draw is drawn once in a pit under the hero for a third of a second (core/systems/Warmup.gd): the run's creature looks, every projectile kind, every weapon visual, the effect pools, the ground marks, chests, mines, power-ups, the portal, and the fonts at every size the overlays use.
scenery
Painted props are MultiMesh billboards (one per painting, up to 200 instances each), opaque with an alpha cut so they depth-sort against creatures per pixel; all their ground shadows are one more draw; the outline of whoever stands behind them is a stencil test (pass 72).
sprites
Every sprite faces the camera fully (a quad pivoting on its feet, tilted towards the lens), so nothing is seen edge-on from the high camera. The hero is one quad with the sprite_billboard shader; creatures use the MultiMesh twin with the same maths. Both read grid strips.
ground marks
Telegraph rings, lines, cones, blast rings, oil spills, poison puddles, zones and scorch marks drape over the dunes: each is one instance in a MultiMesh of a ready-made shape, laid over the terrain vertex by vertex by core/shaders/ground_mark.gdshader from the map's height grid, one draw call per kind.
draw calls
Creatures, shots and gems are drawn once per shown frame, after the physics tick has moved them. The orbiting saws and the wall's posts are one MultiMesh each; a weapon model's plain parts that share a material are merged as it loads (core/systems/MeshMerge.gd: 142 draw calls down to 104 across the 40 weapon models).
terrain
One heightmap mesh, 140 m, noise terrain with one dry river and two or three oases rolled per run, a height-blended shader with the chosen sand, the sandstone fort wall and the cliffs at the edge. Collision from the heightmap.
save
One JSON save file: gold, the Upgrade board's picks, heroes owned, stages beaten, quest progress, settings, best runs, hint flags, one-time migrations.
audio
A sound map resource binds every id from section 16 to a file. Voice-limited pools per id; one music track at a time.
testing
bash tools/ship.sh runs the import, import_json, a parse check of every script and scene, all gdUnit4 tests (about 1,731 in game/tests/passNN_*_test.gd), a headless smoke run, the debug-arena check and the touch audit, then builds the signed APK and the PC exe and drops both in Drive. Beside them: hell_check.gd after stage-3 changes, the late-game benchmark tools/perf_late.gd, steady_check.gd (pass 102: game time, world time and the rules' clock agree in every phase), frame times every second in the run record (pass 101), and a silent screenshot pass for this document.
How this got here (history)
  • v0.23.8, the first late-game pass: the grid became two flat arrays rebuilt every tick; half the crowd steered on each tick instead of all of it on every other one (the steering tick had cost three times the one between, a judder); each creature checked only nearby obstacles; ground marks became MultiMesh instances draped by a shader (before, every mark was a node with its own material, and a growing blast ring was rebuilt twenty times a second: close to a millisecond a frame and 60 draw calls); lightning went from two nodes per strike to two shared meshes; the crowd was drawn once per shown frame instead of once per tick. On a desktop the benchmark's late fight (six evolved weapons, level 45, 150 creatures) went from about 7.8 to 5.4 ms a frame at 60 fps and from 12.7 to 9 at 30 fps; draw calls from about 310 to 210.
  • The crowd budget grew from 25-60 alive (hard cap 80 during swarm rings) to the load cap and the 300-body hard cap of today; until pass 95 creatures more than 30 m behind the hero were moved back onto the spawn ring ahead instead of despawning (that move is kept for the kinds the despawn rule never takes).
  • The 2.5 m separation grid capped its list of wide bodies at 16, which left 0.8-1.1% of creatures with a one-sided overlap; pass 101's 1.25 m cells removed the cap (Garry: "Test that there are no overlaps with 12 neighbours and increase if necessary").
  • Testing began with 33 gdUnit4 tests and a perf mode of the smoke run (perf=1).
  • The path emblems that hung over the weapons were removed in v0.45.

Open questions

Decisions taken so the build could exist, which more playing could still overturn, and the questions only playing can answer. Each decision is a small change in data if it turns out wrong.

Decisions that could still change

  • Death ends the run. One life; the Down! screen offers GET UP once on a watched ad (a stand-in: no ads yet) and the revives stat adds chances (the Phoenix Feather, one-shot since pass 100). A death still banks the purse (pass 70).
  • Weapons are 3D, characters are sprites. The hero never visibly holds a weapon in the sprite; a held gun fires from its muzzle as drawn (pass 95).
  • Chests pause the game. The reel is the price of making a chest an event; the regular-creature odds are set so a run sees a handful, not a dozen.
  • Endless grows by the lead's numbers. Pass 103's HP ×1.15 and ATK ×1.095 a wave are an assumption (Garry asked for a harder ramp and gave no numbers).
  • Game name. "88fits" is the folder name, the working title and the production build's label.
