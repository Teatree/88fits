XP & levelling

Creatures drop XP gems; the hero walks over them, the bar fills, and a level-up offers three cards. When a mutation offer is due, that level-up shows the weapon's mutations instead. How much XP a level needs is one curve on the map, xp_curve × xp_need_mult; the XP Beacon and the chest reel add levels outside it.

pass 95 · passive items climb the rarity ladder

Garry: "the number of Upgrades is reduced to the number of upgrades they have left to reach Legendary + Evolved Rarity ... Evolved is a 'Rarity' for Passive Items". A passive item now goes up one rarity a level and ends on Evolved: a common item has six levels, a legendary one two (see "The rarity ladder" below). Since pass 100 the Phoenix Feather is the one exception: one level, and never offered again once held.

The XP curve

Level L needs XpCurve.need(xp_curve, L) × xp_need_mult XP to reach level L + 1 (Run.xp_to_next, core/data/XpCurve.gd, pass 82). Red Canyon plays the quadratic type with its own quad of 0.9 and an xp_need_mult of 1.65, so a level needs (15 + 6·L + 0.9·L²) × 1.65: about 36 XP at level 1, 272 at level 10, 817 at 20, 1,658 at 30 and 2,797 at 40.

XP table, levels 1 to 40

The map's xp_curve names a growth type and keeps each type's numbers in a block of its own ({"type": …, "quadratic": {…}}). A number a block leaves out is the type's default; a missing or unknown type is quadratic. Every default lands near the old curve's total by level 30 (7,322.5 XP before the multiplier):

quadratic
base + linear·L + quad·L². Defaults 15, 6, 0.5: the curve every pass before 82 played, to the bit.
linear
base + step·(L − 1). Defaults 21.5, 16.5.
exponential
base · growth^(L − 1), never above cap (0 = no cap). Defaults 40, 1.11, 0.
power
base · L^power. Defaults 6.5, 1.33.
table
[[level, XP], …]: straight lines between the listed levels, the first's XP before it; past the last level its XP grows by after_growth a level (default 2.5%; 0 repeats it).

A level always needs at least 0.01 XP and at most 10¹² (no zero that levels forever, no infinity). The Level Editor picks the type from a dropdown and keeps the other types' blocks when it switches (Garry: "I want that section to include types of EXP growth displayed in a dropdown"); tools/level_editor/xp_model.js is a copy of the maths for its charts. The XP each gem gives is multiplied by the hero's xp_gain (the Upgrade board's Field Notes form, The Sage card); the Stakes talent pays a hidden +5% XP a rank from the creatures it strengthens; in Endless gems give 0.35 of their XP (endless.xp_share).

How this got here (history)
  • The first curve was 15 + 6·L + 0.5·L² with no multiplier, aimed at a level-up every 20 to 30 seconds early and every 40 to 60 seconds late.
  • Pass 79 (v0.65): mutation offers became level-ups, so xp_need_mult went from 3.1 to 1.65 (fitted on the run sim so the same XP buys the old curve's level plus the mutations taken by then; it had been 4.1 at one point and 2.1 before 2026-10-15). A shape key xp_curve {base, linear, quad} was built then and dropped, since one number held within about two levels over 0 to 25 minutes.
  • Pass 82 (v0.68): xp_curve came back as growth types, for Garry: "Say I want to make the EXP requirements exponential instead of linear, it doesn't look like I can." Red Canyon's quad 0.9 was set later in the Level Editor.

Gems, the magnet and gold

gems
Small (1 XP), medium (5), large (25), scaled by the creature's xp_mult and by four for an Elite (a creature with an Elite modifier or an Elite kind: a mini-boss). Each creature's drops holds a chance for each size; luck adds 0.1 × luck to the medium chance, and Prospector's chance gives an extra gem. The gems are faceted 3D octahedra that float, hop now and then and glint.
magnet
Base pickup reach 1.5 m (magnet). Gems inside it first hop away from the hero, then fly in; the XP counts only when they land. The Magnet card (+40% reach a level) and the Upgrade board's Lodestone form (+18% a rank) raise it; the Magnet passive item instead pulls every gem within 6 m to him every few seconds (farther and more often up its rungs).
never lost
Gems never merge and never vanish (pass 61, Garry: "I'd prefer that they don't disappear ever, but don't affect the performance negatively at all"): the pool doubles when full, and a gem lying on the sand costs nothing until something calls it.
big pulls
The Uber Magnet structure, the boss chest's opening and the Elite's vacuum gem collect every gem on the map: each is thrown up, hangs a beat and dives into the hero, charged. The best feeling in the genre.
Magnet power-up
Since pass 95 the Magnet pickup is a timed wide pull: for 10 s every gem and coin within 2.5 × the hero's magnet reach comes to him, with a 2D magnet circling his feet, dashed field lines to what it pulls and a HUD plate (core/systems/MagnetBuff.gd).
feedback
Gems collected within a quarter second are batched into one rising "+N XP" label and the bar pops, so a sweep through a field reads as one reward.
gold
Separate from XP. 3D coins drop rarely from creatures (their drops.gold), black barrels hold 1 to 5 (pass 87), the Loot Cache gives 40, and Endless drops coins at 5% (endless.coin_chance). Elites and bosses pay five times what they did before pass 70: an Elite's purse is 450 + 300 a stage (450, 750, 1,050), a boss's 1,300 / 2,000 / 2,700, and a boss spills 60 (the King), 150 (the Gila) or 200 (the Brander) coins. Coin gain (greed) scales all of it. Coins picked up rise off the hero as one "+N" with a coin. A chest is never sold for gold. Gold is spent in the in-run Shop and, between runs, on the Upgrade board and on heroes (section 13).

A level-up

A level is an occasion, as Megabonk and the rest of the genre never let you forget. The run dims to sepia and the XP rail along the top (36 px, plain purple) runs rainbow across its whole width; LEVEL UP sits over LEVEL N on a brass sunburst with a turning shine, two bursts of rays and confetti go off, a short brass fanfare plays, and the three cards drop in one after another with a tick each.

  • Three cards, always. Luck only lifts the rarer items' odds (below).
  • Reroll, skip, banish. A run (one stage since pass 87) starts with two skips and one banish, shared by the level-up cards, the mutation offers and the chests; the Upgrade board's Skip form (Nerve) and Banish form (Choice) add one more each a rank. Rerolls are one a run, more from Choice's Reroll form. Skip takes nothing and pays nothing. Reroll redraws the three cards. A spent button stays on screen but dim, and tapping it says where more come from.
  • Banish is a mode: tap it and the title turns to a red BANISH with a line in plain words ("Tap a new item to strike it from this run. It will not be offered again."), every card that may go wears a red stamp, and every card for something the hero already has (a weapon, card or passive item he holds, an evolution, a path mutation of a weapon he holds) is dimmed with "YOU HAVE THIS" and cannot be banished, so no one locks themselves out of an upgrade; a tap on one says why and spends nothing, and a table with nothing banishable locks the button. The struck card is stamped BANISHED and only its slot is redrawn. In a chest, Banish strikes the landed item and the reel spins again (never on an evolution). One rule for both: Run.banish_refusal.
  • Card text is generated from data. The description is built from the item's effect list, so a new item never needs hand-written card copy unless we want flavour. For an owned item it is only the next level's change ("Next: +20% poison per tick"). The same generator (TextGen) feeds the pause screen, the Collection, the hero's sheet and the chest reel.
How this got here (history)
  • v0.30: the reveal (a wanted-poster sunburst, paper scraps and brass stars, a ring of dust, the title stamping in, a brass fanfare; the chest's landed plate gets the same sunburst and a run of bells).
  • v0.33 (Garry: no more gold for skipping): the per-stage skips and banish; skipping had cost gold.
  • v0.38: always three cards. Luck had added a fourth now and then; Garry did not want it.
  • v0.40: the rainbow rail and the bigger reveal; the mutation offer's spinning reel (below); the mutation chest's MUTATION CHEST header under "★ A path opens ★", its beam and glow violet.
  • v0.42: the XP bar twice as thick (36 px), everything under it moved down with it (the level, clock and gold row, the slots, the boss bar under the power-up plates); the weapon's corner glyph on the cards became a single solid revolver (two crossed pistols read as one dark blob); a tap on the top strip to resume after a pick no longer opens the pause menu too.
  • v0.44 (Garry: "Level Up Banish? What are we banishing?" and "Banish option should not allow Banishing items that Player already has"): banish mode says what it does, stamps what may go and refuses what the hero has.

Mutation level-ups

Garry (pass 79): "Mutations are supposed to be level ups, not just random occurrences." A weapon's three path mutations (its upgrades that change how it plays; all three and level 5 evolve it at a chest) are offered one weapon at a time, and each offer comes as a hero level-up in place of the three cards (Run.mutlvl_*):

  • When. An offer falls due when the stage clock passes one of the map's path_times (seven on Sundown: 1:19, 2:20, 3:20, 4:20, 5:15, 6:05, 6:45; eight on Nightfall and on the Blood Moon). One that falls due while no weapon has a pick open is spent with nothing.
  • How. A due offer waits for the hero's next level-up, which becomes a mutation level-up: "Level N! Pick a mutation". A reel of the weapons' plates spins past a brass window, slows with a tick per plate and lands on a weapon drawn at random among those with a pick open (a tap skips; with one weapon it wobbles and stops on it), then that weapon's path cards follow, the hero's own mutation after them, with a single SKIP. Due offers queue, one a level-up. If no weapon has a pick open when the level-up comes, it is an ordinary one.
  • The mark. While the next level-up is a mutation, the HUD's LV plate turns plum with paper letters and MUTATION NEXT sits at the XP rail's right end.
  • Card levels. What grows with the hero's level (the creatures' level factor, his trait, Barrel Rush, Ram, Supply Drop) reads Run.card_level(): his level less the mutation level-ups, about the level the curve before pass 79 gave at the same minute. The map's mutation_levels_scale counts every level-up instead.

The offer and the odds

  • At most one new weapon card in an offer, and only while a weapon slot is free (five).
  • Cards (the lines, tarot-named since pass 73): a new one only while a card slot is free (five); levels of the ones held any time; a maxed card offers its evolution, "<card> Risen", at three times the weight, which takes its slot.
  • Passive items: any not held (no cap), levels of the ones held up to their ladder's top.
  • Perks (the hero's own, section 13): at most one in five offers, weighed ×4.
  • Mutations are never mixed into the three cards: they come as mutation level-ups (above).
  • Never offered: anything banished this run, anything behind an unlock quest (unless held), hidden items, another hero's own weapons, a one-shot item already held, and stage-3 items before stage 3 (from_stage). The hero's favourite is offered whatever its quest, at its bonus level.

Every card that can be offered gets a weight, and the cards are drawn one at a time in proportion to it, without repeats (CardPool.weight_of, the one formula for level-ups and chests):

weight = the item's weight × rarity × luck boost × 1.5 if held × 0.88 per level held past the first × 3 for a card's evolution

the item's weight
Its JSON weight, 10 for most (5 to 9 for a few strong ones).
rarity
common 1, uncommon 0.75, rare 0.55, epic 0.3, legendary 0.15.
luck boost
1 + luck × the tier's share: common 0, uncommon 0.5, rare 1, epic 1.5, legendary 2. The Star card gives 0.1 luck a level (0.6 as The Star Risen), the Omen form 0.12 a rank.
held
×1.5, so builds converge; then ×0.88 for every level the item already has past the first (Garry: "every consecutive Upgrade of an item slightly less likely to drop in Chests or Level Ups"): level 1 → 2 at ×1.5, 3 → 4 at ×1.16, 4 → 5 at ×1.02. Weapons stop at 5, cards at 4.

A worked example: Venom held at level 3 (uncommon, weight 8) weighs 8 × 0.75 × 1.5 × 0.88² ≈ 7.0; a new Hot Streak (rare, weight 6) 6 × 0.55 = 3.3, or 4.6 with The Star maxed (luck 0.4). A chest holds an evolution when a weapon is ready (level 5 and all three of its mutations); otherwise one draw over the next level of everything held and every new passive item (those at 0.45 of their weight), with the same formula; a boss chest holds a mutation.

The rarity ladder

A passive item climbs one rarity a level from its own: an item of rarity r has the rungs from r to legendary, then Evolved, so common 6 levels, uncommon 5, rare 4, epic 3, legendary 2 (PassiveDef.ladder_levels, pass 95). Evolved is a rarity for passive items alone: the dark plate in the brass ring every evolution wears, the item's Evolved icon (<id>_icon_evolved.png, a white outline round the subject), the EVOLVED tag in ember and "Evolved" in every generated line. Each rung's effects are written out in the item's JSON (tools/passive_ladder.py wrote them), so each rung can be tuned: level 1 is 1.2 to 1.5 times the old first level, each rung about 0.8 of an old step, the Evolved rung the biggest. The Collection says "Passive · Rare → Evolved · 4 levels". Cards and evolved forms keep their own levels.

One-shot items

An item with "single": true has one level, stands off the ladder (no rungs, no Evolved rung) and is never offered again once held. The Phoenix Feather is the first (pass 100, Garry: it "can only be used once and doesn't feature any upgrades"): "Two seconds after you fall, you get back up at half health (more with Second Wind) with the revive's blast. Once a run; it never levels up." Its Build row reads "(used)" once spent, and the Collection says "no upgrades".

Run start & first play

Every run is one stage, and it starts with a landing: the hero comes down a beam of light onto the stage he chose, his own weapon already in hand. A new save plays stage 1, Sundown; each stage beaten opens the next in the Stages menu. On the very first run the landing doubles as the tutorial's opening shot.

pass 87 · every stage is its own run

Garry: "Now Player Unlocks Stages and is only able to enter them after having completed the previous one." A stage picked from the Stages menu is a fresh run: level 1, the start weapon, the stage's own clock. After its boss falls, the portal counts down 60 seconds with only EXTRACT, then Endless begins (section 07).

The landing, every run

when
Straight after PLAY (or a stage card), before the clock starts: half a second of the beam alone on the empty arena, a second and a half of the hero coming down it lit and facing the camera, a soft squash and a small shake as he lands in a ring of dust, then a second of the HUD fading in and the clock starts. A press or a drag after the first 0.2 s runs the rest three times as fast (no jump to the end), and a finger still down when he lands becomes the stick, so the player who tried to move walks off at once. The shaders warm up under the beam.
the stage's look
Since pass 100 the stage's look is set before the beam (Game._show_stage_look_now): Nightfall lands in its blue, the Blood Moon in its red, and the beam draws over the filter so it reads as light.
what
Slot 1 holds the hero's own weapon at level 1 (start_weapon in the hero's JSON): the Drifter's Revolver, Ironwood's Spear, Astro's Gravity Gun, Calavera's Guitarra, Solenne's Sun Scepter, Fadeaway's Basketball, Jeżyk's Frisbee, May's Big Mouth. No card, no choice.
favourite
Each hero also has a favourite (a weapon, or a card he favours): it is offered to him whatever its quest, arrives at its bonus level when picked from a card, and wears FAVOURITE. Heroes do not lock you into it.
why
The hero's own weapon is what makes them that hero from the first second, the run starts moving instead of reading, and the level-up cards still teach the weapon list one card at a time.
How this got here (history)
  • v0.31: the hero lands with his own weapon; the starter pick is gone.
  • Pass 64 (Garry: "the hero descending from the sky look janky ... if the player tries to move or press anything while the sequence is happening, it will jerk to the end ... let's try to speed up the sequence"): he faces the camera all the way down and until he first moves, arrives on a smooth ease with no drop, and input hurries the sequence instead of ending it. Before, a tap after the first second landed him at once, white-hot.
  • Pass 100 (Garry: "the into cutscene is not in dark"): the stage's look came only after the landing.

The very first run

  1. Boot. The game's own splash (the hat painting in an ink ring on Wanted paper, "88fits" in Alfa Slab), drawn on plain paper edge to edge so it melts into any screen, with a loading bar that fills in brass while the title loads behind it (ui/Boot.gd, tools/make_splash.py); Android's system splash shows the same disc on the same paper.
  2. Title. A clean save opens on the Drifter, the one free hero, on Red Canyon. One big PLAY; Upgrade, Collection and Settings are there but quiet. A moment after the title comes in, the play-data card asks once whether anonymous play data may be sent.
  3. The landing. The beam, the hero coming down it, the dust; then "Drag anywhere to walk" until he moves, and "He shoots by himself" (she, they: the hero's own pronoun) for a few seconds after.
  4. Walk over gems. "The purple is XP" until the first gem is picked up.
  5. Level up. The first card screen says that the plate's colour tells what an item is; the first banish says a little more about banishing.

Menus & pause

Outside a run: the title (with play setup on it), the Stages menu, the Upgrade board, the Collection and Settings. Inside: the level-up and mutation cards, the chest reel, the shops, the pause screen, Down! and the Results show. Pause matters most: at any moment the player can read exactly what every weapon, card, passive and stat is doing, in words and numbers, not icons.

The Upgrade board

The Upgrade board (ui/HomesteadScreen.gd, "the Mirror" in code; content/talents) has eight talents, each with two mutually exclusive forms and six ranks. A rank costs gold and is bought in one form or the other, and keeps that form (Garry: "each Upgrade is a choice ... and that choice stays"), so one talent can hold Padding and Plate ranks side by side. Take it all back empties the board and returns half the gold spent on it, and is the only way to choose again.

The loop

Run → gold (kills, chests, Elites and bosses pay purses, banked at every end) → a rank of one form of a talent → a run that plays a little differently. Changing your mind costs half, so the interesting question is which half of each pair this build wants, not only whether you can afford a rank.

Heroes

A hero is a trait that grows, a weapon he lands with, a favourite, two perks and one hero mutation. Garry's rule (2026-10-09): "Heroes have 1-2 unique stats, and they scale as they level up." The trait is only that: trait_per_level in the hero's JSON, folded by Run.hero_sheet as one effect per stat worth N × the hero's card level, so it shows from level 1 and grows by the same step every level (the pause screen names it "Long Road (Lv 12)"; the title plaque shows the step, "+1% CRIT / LV"). Creatures grow +5% HP and +3% damage per hero level (level_hp_per_level, level_damage_per_level), so no trait outpaces them. Hero perks and hero mutations

Two kinds of card only that hero is offered. A perk (content/perks, a mechanism the systems run under special): at most one perk card in five offers, weighed ×4, taken once. A hero mutation of one of his weapons (the favourite, or the weapon he lands with): offered after the three path mutations on that weapon's table and taken once; taking it is that weapon's pick like any other, and it is never one of the three an evolution needs. The Collection lists it with the weapon's other mutations, marked with the hero who alone can take it ("CALAVERA ONLY"), and leaves it out until that hero is owned. Content is data, in Godot

The one architectural rule: content is both a data file and something you open, see and tweak, in the Godot editor or in the Level Editor. Code changes are only for new reusable behaviour primitives.

The rule

A new weapon, passive, evolution, creature, elite variant, hero, structure, pickup, shop upgrade, quest, sound or wave timeline is a new content folder and nothing else. It appears in the game the moment the folder exists. Code changes are allowed only to add a brand-new behaviour primitive that no existing file could express, and that primitive must then be reusable by any future content.

Test for every content addition: could Garry make this in the Godot editor, without asking Claude? If not, the system is wrong, not the content.

A content folder

Godot's own resource format is the data format. A custom Resource class with exported fields shows up in the Inspector as a form: sliders for numbers, dropdowns for enums like targeting, file pickers for the icon and the visual scene, and an "Add element" button for the effect list. Each content item is a folder, game/content/<type>/<id>/, the types being weapons, evolutions, mutations, passives, perks, pickups, structures, enemies, elites, bosses, boss_attacks, characters, talents, quests, maps and sounds:

Stats every effect can touch

Player stats include max_hp, damage_reduction, regen, regen_delay, move_speed, might (attack damage), cooldown, area, projectile_speed, duration, debuff_duration, buff_duration, amount, magnet, luck, greed (coin gain), lifesteal, evade, revives, revive_hp, rerolls, skips, banishes; the full list with every default is StatSheet.PLAYER_DEFAULTS. Weapon stats: damage, cooldown, amount, pierce, area, speed, range, duration, knockback, chains, poison_damage, poison_stacks, poison_duration, fire_damage, fire_duration, fire_spread. Any effect names a stat by string, so adding a stat is adding a key to a list, and the pause screen lists whatever keys exist. damage_reduction is a share taken off every hit (the hero's stat capped at 80%, and every creature's own field): the Plate form takes 6% a rank, Ironclad 15%, the Horned Toad 40%, a Shielded Elite 40%.

Mechanism stats, carried by the sheet and consumed by a system: crit_chance, crit_damage, execute_frac, thorns, adrenaline_speed, adrenaline_might, overkill, cooldown_on_kill, xp_gain, haste, phoenix_revive, the Stakes danger stats and many more, each read by the system its comment names. Any passive, talent, pickup or hero trait can set them.

How this got here (history)

v0.13: the mechanism stats. v0.29: armour is gone, in its place damage_reduction, because a flat point of armour meant nothing next to a hit's size (Astro's suit took 5% off then). The JSON example above had eight levels until weapons went to five in pass 48 (tools/compress_levels.py: level 5 = the old level 8), and named evolves_with while an evolution paired a weapon with a passive.

Where the line is

data only
New weapon using an existing behaviour (poison or fire included: the element is a few stats). New mutation, evolution, card or passive item. New creature using an existing brain (chase, flock, kite, charge, artillery, caster, sniper, kamikaze, support, boss). New elite variant. New hero, perk using an existing special, hero mutation. New structure with an existing effect. New pickup. New talent form. New boss attack of an existing kind (rain, volley, ring, slam). New quest with an existing condition type. New stage, roster row or wave. New look. New sound assignment. New icon.
needs code
A behaviour that does not exist yet (the Dragon's Breath needed FireBreath, pass 95). A brain that does not exist yet (the Saiga Sniper's sniper, pass 73). A structure effect, a perk special or a quest condition type that does not exist yet (pass 100's build quests). Each is built once as a reusable primitive with exported parameters.
never code
Card text, icons, balance numbers, rarity, chest odds, unlock conditions using existing checks, which mesh a weapon uses, which look plays, which sound plays.

Tuning the game: where the numbers live

Every balance number is in a JSON file under game/content/, one folder per thing; BALANCING.md says where each lives and why it is what it is. Change the number in the Level Editor (Save imports it), or edit the JSON and then press JSON → .tres in the Content dock or run tools/import_json.gd (the game reads the generated .tres before the JSON, so a JSON edit shows only after that step); bash tools/ship.sh does it on its own before it builds. To feel a change at once, open the arena (debug mode): spawn any creature, hand yourself any item at any level, call any wave. The Level Editor's Sim tab plays a stage with a bot, and analytics/dashboard.html charts a played run second by second.

a creature
game/content/enemies/<id>/<id>.json (bosses in content/bosses): hp, speed, damage (a touch), damage_reduction, knockback (its push) and knockback_resist, radius and size, xp_mult, drops (gem chances, gold, chest chance, a pickup), pack_size, max_alive, cap_share (how much of the live cap one takes), variance (size and speed jitter, the tints, the special block: its chance, multipliers and rim colour), flee_on_hit, and its brain's block (charge, ranged, artillery, caster, sniper, boss.attacks with every attack's damage, wind-up and cooldown). Custom boss attacks are content/boss_attacks/<id>; Elite modifiers (their extra HP, speed, damage reduction) are content/elites/<id>.
everyone at once
The map's timeline_data in game/content/maps/red_canyon/red_canyon.json: creature_hp_mult (1.3 today), creature_damage_mult (1.25), creature_speed_mult (0.9), stage_mults (1, 2.2, 3.34), hp_curve and damage_curve (HP and attack over the run's clock; hp_growth and hp_power are the legacy formula), level_hp_per_level (0.05) and level_damage_per_level (0.03), contact_cap (the most one touch can take, 0.22 of max HP), move_pace (everyone's speed, 0.9 today: the hero, every creature, the hostile shots and boulders, every red warning lasting 1/move_pace as long), hero_knockback_mult (0.5: every push the hero's side gives) and hero_special_knock (default 0.5: how hard the special attacks push the hero), special_defaults (a special's chest chance 0.35, at most 3 alive), boss_hp_scaling (a boss comes with at least 25 / 40 / 60 s of the hero's full DPS: King, Gila, Brander), power_hp_scaling (1 = none), target_priority (10 m) and despawn.
aim
Within 10 m, weapons take the nearest special, Elite or boss first (target_priority). Beyond that, single-target weapons ("targeting": "priority": the Revolver, Peashooter, Tommy Gun, Tumbleweed, Spear, Sabre, Basketball, the Turret and the Killer Bees) aim at the creature with the smallest distance ÷ weight, its weight being its max health over the crowd's average, from 1 to PRIORITY_CAP (3, in EnemyManager.gd), bosses and Elites at the cap. Area weapons pick the thickest crowd.
bringing things back
Anything with "hidden": true stays in its folder and is never offered. Delete the line to bring a mutation back (and hide the one that took its slot, or the weapon has four); for an old evolution, delete its hidden and point the weapon's evolves_into at it. The old Golden Revolver and Earthshaker are kept as golden_revolver_v47 and earthshaker_v47.
an item
Weapons in content/weapons/<id>: base (damage, cooldown, amount, range, pierce, area, knockback, speed, the element's stats), levels (one list of effects per level: {"stat": "damage", "op": "mul", "value": 0.2} = +20%), params, rarity, weight, evolves_into, and its looks (variants, variant). Evolutions in content/evolutions, mutations in content/mutations (their effects and params). Cards and passive items in content/passives/<id>: levels (a passive item's one per rung), line, evolves_into, single, rarity, weight, from_stage. Heroes in content/characters (trait_per_level, start_weapon, the favourite and its bonus, availability, price, looks), their perks in content/perks, the Upgrade board in content/talents (cost per rank, effects per rank).
the waves
