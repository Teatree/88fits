XP & levelling

Creatures drop gems, the hero walks over them, the bar fills, three cards appear. The cadence is a level-up every 20 to 30 seconds early and every 40 to 60 seconds late; the XP Beacon and the reel add a few levels outside the curve.

v0.40 · the bar goes rainbow, the mutation offer spins

While the level-up cards are up, the XP bar at the top of the screen runs rainbow colours across its whole width (the cue Megabonk and Vampire Survivors give), and the reveal is bigger: two bursts of rays and confetti, a brass sunburst behind LEVEL N, the cards dropping in one after another with a soft flick. A mutation offer now picks its weapon at random among those with a path still open this stage, and shows it: a reel of the weapons' plates spins past a window, slows with a tick per plate and lands on the chosen one with a flash (a tap skips; with one weapon it wobbles and stops on it). The mutation chest says so too: its header reads MUTATION CHEST in purple under "★ A path opens ★", and its beam and glow turn violet.

v0.42 · a thicker XP bar, a revolver on the cards

The XP bar along the top is twice as thick (36 px, a plain purple fill), and everything under it moved down with it: the level, clock and gold row, the slots, the boss bar and the power-up plates in a run, and the header and cards of the level-up and mutation screens. In a boss fight the boss bar now sits under the power-up plates instead of over their seconds. The weapon's corner glyph on the level-up and chest cards is a single solid revolver (it was two crossed pistols, which read as one dark blob next to the line's scroll). A tap on the top strip to resume after a pick no longer opens the pause menu too.

XP table, levels 1 to 40
gems
Small (1 xp), medium (5), large (25), scaled by the creature's xp_mult and by four for elite variants. 3D gem meshes that spin. Drop table per creature. The beetle, the mummy and the Elite drop large.
magnet
Base pickup radius 1.5 m. Gems inside it fly to the hero. The Magnet passive, the shop and Astro's trait raise it.
gem merging
When too many gems sit on the ground, the oldest merge into one bigger gem, the Vampire Survivors trick that keeps performance flat with a hundred kills a minute.
vacuum
A rare drop from elite variants collects every gem on the map; the Magnet Pulse pickup and the Uber Magnet structure do the same on purpose. The best feeling in the genre.
feedback
Gems collected within a quarter second are batched into one rising "+N XP" label and the bar pops, so a sweep through a field reads as one reward.
gold
Separate from XP. 3D coins drop rarely (a goblin one time in fifty), bosses spill a dozen, the Loot Cache gives 40, and coin gain scales it (a chest is never sold since v0.33). Coins picked up rise off the hero as one "+N" with a coin, like the XP (v0.33). Gold is spent at the travelling shop inside a run and in the permanent shop outside it.

Level-up cards

Since v0.30 the cards come in under a reveal: a wanted-poster sunburst turns behind the title, paper scraps and brass stars rain past, a ring of dust snaps out, the title stamps into place with a bounce and a short brass fanfare plays; the chest's landed plate gets the same sunburst and a run of bells. A level is an occasion again, which Megabonk and the rest of the genre never let you forget.

  • Pool. Weapons not yet owned while a weapon slot is free, plus any owned weapon or passive below max level. Nothing outside the pool can appear.
  • Three cards, always (v0.38: luck had added a fourth now and then; Garry did not want it, so luck only lifts the rarer items' odds). Rarity weights favour upgrades to items you already own so builds converge.
  • Reroll, skip, banish (v0.33, Garry: no more gold for skipping). Every stage gives two skips and one banish, shared by the level-up cards, the mutation offers and the chests, and refilled when the next stage starts (unused ones do not carry over); the Upgrade board's Skip form (Nerve) and Banish form (Choice) add one more per stage a rank. Rerolls stay one a run, more from Choice's Reroll form. Skip takes nothing and pays nothing. Reroll redraws the three cards. Banish is a mode: tap it, then tap the card; that item leaves the run's pool for good and only its slot is redrawn, the other cards stay. In a chest, Banish strikes the landed item from the run and the reel spins again (never on an evolution). A spent button stays on screen but dim, and tapping it says where more come from, so the loop explains itself.
    • v0.44 (Garry: "Level Up Banish? What are we banishing?" and "Banish option should not allow Banishing items that Player already has"): banish mode says what it does in red over the cards ("Tap a new item to strike it from this run. It will not be offered again."), stamps every card that may go with a red BANISH, and dims every card for something the hero already has with "YOU HAVE THIS": a weapon, a line or a passive item he holds, an evolution, or a path mutation of a weapon he holds can never be banished, so no one locks themselves out of an upgrade; a tap on one says why and spends nothing, and a table with nothing banishable locks the button. The chest follows the same rule (Run.banish_refusal).
  • Card text is generated from data. The description is built from the item's effect list, so a new item never needs hand-written card copy unless we want flavour. For an owned item it is only the next level's change ("Next: +20% poison per tick"); the current level and the ladder are the chip and the pips. The same generator feeds the pause screen, the collection and the chest reel.

The offer

  • Three cards (v0.38: always three), with reroll, banish and skip.
  • At most one new weapon card in an offer, and only while a weapon slot is free (six).
  • Lines: a new one only while a line slot is free (six); levels of the ones held any time; a maxed line offers its evolution at three times the weight.
  • Passive items: any not held (no cap), levels of the ones held.
  • Mutations are never in a level-up: a weapon's mutations come together at the stage's mutation slots (seven on stage 1, eight on stages 2 and 3, so a sixth weapon still gets its pick).
  • Perks at rare weight, at most one in five offers.

The odds

Every card that can be offered gets a weight, and the cards are drawn one at a time in proportion to it (no repeats):

weight = the item's weight × rarity × luck boost × 1.5 if held × 0.88 per level held past the first × 3 for a line's evolution

the item's weight
Its JSON weight, 10 for most (5 to 9 for a few strong ones).
rarity
common 1, uncommon 0.75, rare 0.55, epic 0.3, legendary 0.15 (v0.34: Gas Mask and Sinner's Greed are the first epics).
luck boost
1 + luck × the tier's share: common 0, uncommon 0.5, rare 1, epic 1.5, legendary 2. Clover gives 0.1 luck a level (0.6 as the Golden Horseshoe), the Omen talent 0.12 a rank.
held
×1.5, so builds converge; then ×0.88 for every level the item already has past the first (v0.33, Garry: each consecutive upgrade a little less likely): level 1 → 2 at ×1.5, 3 → 4 at ×1.16, 4 → 5 at ×1.02 (v0.34: weapons stop at 5, lines at 4).

A worked example: Magnet held at level 3 (uncommon, weight 10) weighs 10 × 0.75 × 1.5 × 0.88² ≈ 8.7; a new rare passive 10 × 0.55 = 5.5, or 7.7 with Clover maxed (luck 0.4). A chest holds an evolution when a weapon is ready (level 5 and all three of its mutations); otherwise one draw over the next level of everything held and every new passive item (those at 0.45 of their weight), with the same formula; a boss chest holds a mutation.

Run start & first play

The hero lands in a beam of light with their own weapon already in hand (v0.31; the starter pick is gone). The first seconds are a landing, not a menu, and on the very first run the landing doubles as the tutorial's opening shot.

v0.40 · our own splash

The Godot logo is gone: the app opens on the game's own splash, the hat painting in an ink ring on Wanted paper with "88fits" in Alfa Slab, and Android's system splash shows the same disc on the same paper (tools/make_splash.py; boot_splash in the project settings, splash_screen in the Android export).

v0.44 · no lines on a tall phone, and a loading bar

On Garry's Pixel 8 Pro (20:9) the boot picture was fitted by its width and centred, so the ink bands at its top and bottom floated inside the screen as two brown lines, and they stayed up for a second or two because the title loaded in one blocking step. The splash is now drawn on plain paper edge to edge (tools/make_splash.py), so the boot picture melts into the paper behind it on any screen. The splash itself holds an empty loading bar and LOADING under the subtitle, so the boot picture is the loading screen at 0 %, and the first scene (ui/Boot.gd) draws the very same picture in the very same place as one draw, filling the bar in brass while the title loads in the background (ResourceLoader.load_threaded_request, with a fallback if it fails or stalls); the title is built under it, and it dissolves away evenly. The camera-cutout strip fades in with the title.

The landing, every run

when
Immediately after PLAY, before the timer starts: half a second of the beam alone on the empty arena, a second and a half of the hero coming down it white-hot, a ring of dust, the HUD fading in, the clock starting. A tap after the first second lands them at once. The shaders warm up under the beam.
what
Slot 1 holds the hero's own weapon at level 1 (start_weapon in the hero's JSON: Astro's Gravity Gun, the Drifter's Revolver, Calavera's Guitarra, Ironwood's Spear (the Pike since v0.33); a hero without one lands with the favourite). No card, no choice.
character bias
Each hero still has a favourite weapon: it gets its bonus when picked from a level-up card, and the hero path sits on it. Heroes do not lock you into it.
why
The hero's own weapon is what makes them that hero from the first second, the run starts moving instead of reading, and the level-up cards still teach the weapon list one card at a time.

The very first run

  1. Title screen. One big PLAY. Shop, collection and settings are visible but quiet. Astro and Dry Gulch are preselected.
  2. The landing. The beam, the hero coming down it, the dust; then "Drag anywhere to walk" and the hero's own weapon already firing by itself.
  3. Touch and drag. A hint panel says "TOUCH and DRAG anywhere below to move", then "Drag in any direction. Lift to stop." once the hero has moved.
  4. Walk over gems. "Walk over GEMS to level up" after the first kill. Cleared on pickup.
  5. Level up. The first card screen carries a one-line explainer per card type, weapon versus passive.
  6. Pause exists. At 1:30, a toast: "Tap the top bar any time to pause and see your build". Nothing else interrupts the run again.

Every hint is skippable by tapping, appears once, and can be reset from settings. The run itself is the tutorial; the chest reel and the structures explain themselves by being watched.

Menus & pause

Four screens outside the run and four overlays inside it (level-up, pause, the chest reel, the travelling shop). Pause matters most: at any moment the player can read exactly what every weapon, passive and stat is doing, in words and numbers, not icons.

Look. Survivor.io is the target, and the whole look is one small file, ui/UiKit.gd. Navy panels with a lighter blue rim, one gold for the thing to press, purple for XP and passives, green for good news, red for danger. Headers, numbers and buttons are set in Lilita One, body text in Baloo 2 (both OFL, in ui/fonts); anything over the world gets a dark outline. Buttons carry a soft shadow and a darker bottom edge so they read as physical; primary buttons are white on gold with a brown rim.

Cards that cannot overflow. Every tappable card is a TapCard: a panel that sizes itself to its content (icon left, text column right, generous padding) and fires pressed once per tap, on touch or mouse, giving the tap up when the list starts scrolling. Icons are always square and mouse-transparent, so a tap on the picture is a tap on the card. Every overlay anchors its header, its scrolling list and its buttons to the screen as three bands, so a long list can never push the buttons off the bottom.

The debug arena

Debug · Arena (v0.23.4), a small entry under the title's menu row, opens the map with the hero, his favourite weapon and god mode, and nothing coming: the director is set up but holds its clock and its spawns. A column of quick buttons at the right edge does the frequent things without opening anything: ARENA opens the panel, AGAIN repeats the last spawn, CLEAR takes every creature and shot away, WAVES runs or holds the real director, LV +1 levels the hero; under them a status line (the arena clock, the stage, how many are alive, whether the waves run, what AGAIN will do). The panel is a bottom sheet with six tabs, and the game waits while it is open:

ENEMY
Every creature in the game as a tile, grouped: the roster in arrival order, the specials, the Elites, the bosses, the retired (goblins, crab, jackalope) and any other (the centipede's young). Options on top: how many (1, 5, 20, 50), where (a few metres up the screen, a ring round the hero, out past the screen), any elite modifier, and whether they hold still. A boss comes as a real boss, bar, attacks, chest and portal.
WAVES
The arena clock (every spawn scales as at that minute, stage and HP growth included), a crowd drawn from the roster at that minute by its weights (30, 100, 200 or the hard cap), a swarm of the last creature picked, the real director run from the clock (its spawns, its events, its Elites and bosses, the specials' clock) or held, and an Elite or a special on demand.
ITEMS
Every weapon, passive, line and perk in folding sections, each with − / + / MAX applied at once and past the rules the cards keep (the stage's weapon cap, a path's one pick a stage); "Max all held" and "Remove every item" (Run.debug_set_item). v0.35 (Garry: the mutations and evolutions stacked under their weapon, easier to find): a weapon's row opens its own stack, indented under it: its element, volume and motion mutations, the heroes' own, its evolution, and the hidden ones last (old evolutions such as golden_revolver_v47, marked hidden). There are no separate Evolutions and Mutations sections any more; hidden weapons have their own closed section.
ITEMS · INFO (v0.37)
Every row has an INFO toggle (a weapon has two: EVO for its stack and INFO) that opens, under the row, exactly what a player reads about the item, built by the same calls the screens make and read from the run as it stands: a weapon held shows its description as it fires now (its level, its mutations, the hero's stats), the Build tab's numbers, how far its evolution is and the next level's card; one not held, the card that offers it (an evolution, the chest's words). A passive or a line held shows its Build-tab words and the next level's card (a maxed line, its evolution's); a mutation or a perk, its card. Setting any level in the tab fills every open INFO again, since one item's level reaches the others. The EVO toggle counts only the three path mutations an evolution needs ("3 + EVO"); a hero's own mutation sits in the stack marked optional, and a hidden hero's with the hidden ones.
HERO
+1, +5, +10 levels or straight to 30, level-up cards on or off (off, the levels come without the cards), god mode, a full heal, a hit of 10 (the shield, the evade and the damage log see it), going down (the revive screen), gold, rerolls and banishes.
DROPS
Every power-up put on the sand by the hero, a chest, a boss chest, forty gems, forty coins, and the vacuum.
WORLD
Game speed (a quarter, a half, 1x, 2x), freezing the crowd, killing (with drops) or removing everything, clearing the shots, the camera's zoom held at 1x to 2.6x, the stats overlay, and leaving.

Training dummies (v0.35, Garry's seventh list). Besides the field (below), the WORLD tab and the developer menu now have: Patrol, the field's dummies walking left and right two metres either side of their spot; + MELEE DUMMY and + RANGED DUMMY, which add a bigger dummy that fights back (the melee one chases you within 12 m of its spot and bites for 10, the ranged one spits like the Spit Cactus for 8: the same red ring, lob and splash), each on its own spot by the hero, with their own health and REMOVE ATTACKERS; and two dummies standing behind the field: an Elite dummy (bigger, the crown over its head) and a Boss dummy (bigger still, the skull), each with its own health (up to 50,000 and 200,000) and behaviour (stand, patrol, melee, ranged). Heroes and looks (v0.41, Garry). The HERO tab starts with a tile for every hero (the out-of-play ones too, dimmer, in debug mode): a tap swaps the hero in place, with the new hero's trait; the items stay, the old hero's perks go and the new one's are handed out in ITEMS. 13

Meta progression

Megabonk's loop between runs: gold buys permanent stats, and in-run challenges unlock heroes, weapons and modes. Small but complete, because "do I want another run" is half the test.

Upgrade (the Mirror)

v0.47 · the choices as icons

Garry: "The choices in the Mirror should be shown as little icons ... Each of those icons should be distinctly different from their counterpart." Every form has its own painted icon, each pair different in shape and colour (Grit: a quilted cushion or a steel breastplate; Drive: a flexed arm or a stopwatch; Stride: a cowboy boot or a horseshoe magnet; Fortune: a crystal ball or a coin sack; Choice: a die in arrows or a struck-out card; Nerve: fast-forward or a winged heart; Study: a notebook or a mortar of herbs; Spite: a barrel cactus or a blood drop; content/talents/<id>/<id>_<form>_icon.png). Each tile is taller and carries a rank bar of six slots: a bought rank shows the chosen form's icon, an empty one a dashed slot; before the first rank both forms sit beside the name ("[icon] or [icon]"). A YOUR UPGRADES strip at the top sums the build: one chip per talent with ranks, its form's icon and "x3". Switching form still moves every rank (the rule is unchanged), so a bar always shows one form.

The meta is the Upgrade board (v0.22, as the Mirror, cut down from the Mirror of Night in Hades; renamed Upgrade in v0.30 so the button says what it is for). Eight talents, each with two mutually exclusive forms, six ranks apiece (three until v0.32: Garry asked to double the depth; ranks 4 to 6 keep the per-rank values and cost 4.5, 6.5 and 9 times the first; Second Wind alternates a revive and +15% health on revival instead of six revives). A rank costs gold; switching a talent to its other form is free and keeps the ranks; and Take it all back empties the board and returns half the coins spent on it (v0.30; until then all of them, which made the board a free respec). So the meta is a build you can re-decide, at a price.

The loop

Run → gold (kills, chests, Elites and bosses pay purses now) → a rank of one form of a talent → a run that plays a little differently. Nothing is lost by changing your mind, so the interesting question is which half of each pair this run wants, not whether you can afford anything. It replaced the farm of v0.17 (five producers, a market, silver, stock, bones and relics), which was a second game rather than a frame around this one, and the flat gold-buys-stats shop before it, which was a list.

One more thing to spend a run's gold on before it ends: the Strongbox (v0.22), an eighth structure on the map. Stand in its ring and every coin you are carrying goes to the homestead at once, whether you extract afterwards or die in the next minute. It is the only way to bank mid-run, and the results screen counts what you sent. Since v0.23 it is a chest on a balloon (the first painted strongbox read as a heap of brown): a banded wooden chest with a gold lock and a gold coin on the front and a red balloon tied to its lid, painted 2.5D like every other structure (v0.23.1; a Blender model tried for one build was the only 3D structure on the map). A hero is a named trait plus a favourite weapon. The trait has a name and one line in the data (trait_name, trait_text), shown on the hero card in play setup, in the collection and on the pause screen, because a trait with a mechanic behind it (Buck's momentum, Astro's suit) cannot be generated from its effect list. Every hero's icon is a painted close-up doing something (the Drifter tips his hat, Astro gives a thumbs-up with a planet in her visor, Dad a double thumbs-up), made as an edit of the hero's own front still and set into a round badge that fills the plate.

v0.47 · one stat that grows

Garry's rule (2026-10-09): "Heroes have 1-2 unique stats, and they scale as they level up." A playable hero's trait is now only that: "trait_per_level" in the hero's JSON (CharacterDef.level_trait), folded by Run.hero_sheet as one effect per stat worth N x the hero's level, so the trait shows from level 1 and grows by the same step every level (the pause screen names it "Long Road (Lv 12)"). Start weapons, favourites and perks stay. Creatures already grow +5% HP and +3% damage per hero level, so no trait outpaces them; heroes are a little weaker than before in the first minutes and stronger late. The title plaque shows the step ("+1% CRIT / LV") and opens the hero's sheet: a Wanted-poster CV with the badge, the slogan, the signature stat at levels 1/10/20/40, every stat a run starts with, and the items the hero is built around with their in-game words (ui/wanted/WHeroSheet.gd). Content is data, in Godot

The one architectural rule: content is both a data file and something you open, see and tweak inside the Godot editor. In Godot those are the same thing.

The rule

A new weapon, passive, evolution, creature, elite variant, hero, structure, pickup, shop upgrade, quest, sound or wave timeline is a new content folder and nothing else. It appears in the game the moment the folder exists. Code changes are allowed only to add a brand-new behaviour primitive that no existing file could express, and that primitive must then be reusable by any future content.

Test for every content addition: could Garry make this in the Godot editor, without asking Claude? If not, the system is wrong, not the content.

How it looks in the editor

Godot's own resource format is the data format. A custom Resource class with exported fields shows up in the Inspector as a form: sliders for numbers, dropdowns for enums like targeting, file pickers for the icon and the visual scene, and an "Add element" button for the effect list. Each content item is a folder:

FileWhat it holdsHow you edit it
boomstick.jsonThe authoring source: stats, level table, targeting, pairing, rarity, the art prompt.Edit the text, press "JSON → .tres" in the Content dock (or run import_json.gd).
boomstick.tresThe same numbers as a Godot resource.Click it, edit in the Inspector. Sliders and dropdowns, no code.
boomstick_visual.tscnWhat it looks like in the world: the shotgun mesh (Held), the pellet (Projectile), the muzzle point; the Peashooter's pod is a Familiar node the same way.Open it in the 3D editor, move things, swap the mesh, press play to preview.
boomstick_icon.pngThe icon used by cards, the HUD, pause, the reel and the collection.Replace the file, or re-run gen_art.py --only boomstick.

Creatures follow the same shape: goblin.json for stats, brain choice, drops (including its chest chance), the variance block, how many directions it is painted from, the badge it wears and whether it is retired; goblin_sprite.png is the strip. A map is a folder with its arena scene, its wave timeline (roster, events, pickups, structures), its music and its creature list.

The Content dock

A small editor plugin adds a "Content" panel to Godot. It lists every item by type with its icon, warns about missing files, and has "New from template" per type: it duplicates a template folder and renames the id. "JSON → .tres" regenerates the resources. A Test button launches the game with that item already equipped, so a change is playable in ten seconds.

Stats every effect can touch

Player stats: max_hp, damage_reduction, regen, move_speed, might, cooldown, area, projectile_speed, duration, amount, magnet, luck, greed, lifesteal, revives, rerolls, skips, banishes. Weapon stats: damage, cooldown, amount, pierce, area, speed, range, duration, knockback, chains, poison_damage, poison_stacks, poison_duration, fire_damage, fire_duration, fire_spread. Any effect names a stat by string, so adding a stat is adding a key to a list, and the pause screen lists whatever keys exist. v0.29: armour is gone; in its place damage_reduction, a share taken off every hit (the hero's stat, capped at 80%, and every creature's damage_reduction field), because a flat point of armour meant nothing next to a hit's size: Astro's suit takes 5% off, the Plate talent 6% a rank, Ironclad 15%, the Horned Toad 40%, a Shielded Elite 40%.

Mechanism stats (v0.13), carried by the sheet and consumed by a system: crit_chance, crit_damage, execute_frac, thorns, adrenaline_speed, adrenaline_might, overkill, cooldown_on_kill, xp_gain. Any passive, shop tier, pickup or hero trait can set them.

Where the line is

data only
New weapon using an existing behaviour (poison or fire included: the element is a few stats). New passive. New evolution. New creature using an existing brain (chase, flock, kite, charge, boss). New elite variant. New hero. New structure with an existing effect. New pickup. New shop tier. New quest with an existing condition type. New wave timeline. New sound assignment. New icon.
needs code
A behaviour that does not exist yet, such as a boomerang. A brain that does not exist yet, such as a burrower. A structure effect that does not exist yet. A quest condition type that does not exist yet. Each is built once as a reusable primitive with exported parameters.
never code
Card text, icons, balance numbers, pairing recipes, rarity, chest odds, unlock conditions using existing checks, which mesh a weapon uses, which sound plays.

Tuning the game: where the numbers live

Every balance number is in a JSON file under game/content/, one folder per thing. Change the number, then either press JSON → .tres in the editor's Content dock or run tools/import_json.gd (the game reads the generated .tres before the JSON, so a JSON edit shows only after that step); bash tools/ship.sh does it on its own before it builds. To feel a change at once, open Debug · Arena from the title (debug mode on): spawn any creature, hand yourself any item at any level, call any wave. analytics/dashboard.html charts a played run's damage against the crowd's pressure, second by second.

a creature
game/content/enemies/<id>/<id>.json (bosses in content/bosses): hp, speed, damage (a touch), damage_reduction (a share of every hit), radius and size, xp_mult, drops (gem sizes, gold, chest chance, a pickup), pack_size, max_alive, cap_share (how much of the live cap one takes), variance (size and speed jitter, the tints, special_chance and the special's multipliers: the coloured ones), and its brain's block (charge, ranged, artillery, caster, boss.attacks with every attack's damage, wind-up and cooldown). Elite modifiers (the coloured Elites' extra HP, speed, damage reduction) are content/elites/<id>.
everyone at once
The map's timeline_data in game/content/maps/red_canyon/red_canyon.json scales the whole crowd: creature_hp_mult (×1.3 today), stage_mults (stage 2 and 3), hp_growth and hp_power (HP over a stage's minutes), level_hp_per_level and level_damage_per_level (creatures grow with the hero's level), contact_cap (the most one touch can take, as a share of max HP), and move_pace (v0.34: everyone's speed, 0.8 = 20% slower, 0.6 since v0.38; the hero, every creature, and to keep dodging fair the hostile shots and boulders too, with every red warning lasting 1/move_pace as long).
aim
Single-target weapons ("targeting": "priority": the Peashooter, Revolver, Tommy Gun, Tumbleweed, Spear and Sabre, the Turret and the Killer Bees) aim at the creature with the smallest distance ÷ weight, its weight being its max health over the crowd's average, from 1 to PRIORITY_CAP (3, in EnemyManager.gd), bosses and Elites at the cap: run up to something big and they hit it. Area weapons still pick the thickest crowd.
bringing things back
A mutation or an evolution with "hidden": true stays in its folder and is never offered (v0.34 hid eighteen mutations and seven evolutions this way). Delete the line to bring a mutation back (and hide the one that took its slot, or the weapon has four); for an old evolution, delete its hidden and point the weapon's evolves_into at it. The old Golden Revolver and Earthshaker are kept as golden_revolver_v47 and earthshaker_v47.
an item
Weapons in content/weapons/<id>: base (damage, cooldown, amount, range, pierce, area, knockback, speed, the element's stats) and levels, one list of effects per level ({"stat": "damage", "op": "mul", "value": 0.2} = +20%). Evolutions in content/evolutions, mutations in content/mutations (their effects and params). Lines and passive items in content/passives/<id>: levels, max_level, line, evolves_into, and for the offers rarity and weight. Heroes in content/characters (trait, start weapon, favourite and its bonus), their perks in content/perks, the Upgrade board in content/talents (cost per rank, effects per rank).
the waves
The same timeline_data: stage_length (430 s), cap_curve (how many creatures may be alive at each second: [[0, 10], [60, 16], …]), roster (who joins and when: {"t": 0, "enemy": "skeleton", "weight": 10, "weight_late": 3}, with pack and the hell channel), events (swarms, the Elites at 215 / 645 / 1075 s, the bosses at the stage ends), specials (the rocketeer and the harpooner), elite_every, colour_after_kills, burst_min/burst_max/spawn_interval (how they arrive), path_times (when mutations are offered), xp_need_mult (levelling speed), the boss and portal caps, portal_wave (v0.34: the one small wave when a boss dies, then nothing until you go through), and the endless settings.
