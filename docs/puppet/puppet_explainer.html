The Puppet Method

One painting of a hero becomes a small 3D puppet: an AI model builds her shape, Blender gives her a skeleton, and the same Mixamo run our heroes use makes her move. Renders of that puppet then guide the video model that paints her walks, so each walk follows her own body instead of a stranger's stick figure. Everything on this page is the real trial on May: her pictures, her files and the times we measured.

Before the show

Start it from the Level Editor

Since pass 71 the Puppet method is a look job like the others: you start it from a hero's page, it runs on the AI server and this PC by itself, and it stops only for your three picks. Shown here on Jeżyk, the second hero to get a puppet (his arms-out paintings, shape, rig, clips and guides took about 9 minutes of machine time).

  1. Choose the method. Open the Level Editor (tools\level_editor.cmd), go to the Heroes tab, press Edit on the hero, then + Generate art… in his Looks. Pick the card Hero: a 3D puppet of the painting walks him (the Puppet method). Like every card it says what it needs, what it makes, how long it takes (about 21 minutes) and what to watch for.

  2. Say what the puppet starts from. "Start from" offers the puppet he already has (whatever is on disk is kept), a new puppet, his .blend after you fixed the rig by hand, or a Mixamo-rigged FBX. A hero who already has a puppet goes straight to the walks: about 11 minutes.

  3. Choose the picture each side of the 3D shape is made from. For the front, the side and the back: paint arms-out locally (an edit of the look's own still, the default), any of the hero's stills from any look as it is, or a picture you uploaded. The words can be changed; empty means the template the trial found. Below them: the painter (Klein, about 20 s a candidate, or Flux.2 dev, 4–5 min a candidate with a restart each), how many fronts to pick from, the screen colour, how many Hunyuan3D tries, the mesh's size.

  4. Set the walk. The Mixamo clip and the idle; which views walk on depth and which on the stick figure (ticked = depth; the default is the trial's best: depth for fd, side, bd and back, the stick figure for the front); how the stick figures are framed (by the soles, so the boots stay in the picture); run size from the puppet (each view's walk sized by how tall the 3D puppet's own run stands, instead of a hand-tuned run_scale); the fringe remap; and whether the walks reuse a look's stills or paint new ones.

  5. Queue it, then pick when it asks. The job card shows every stage as a chip that fills in as it lands: the arms-out front, side and back, the 3D shape, the rig, the clips and colours (the GLB), the guides, then each walk. When it needs you it stops with Waiting for pick: click the candidate you like, then Pick, or Re-roll 4 more. Every ComfyUI render is linked from the card (↗) and sits in ComfyUI's Workflows sidebar. A stage can be run again from the card; its outputs and everything after it are moved aside first.

  6. Look at the puppet itself. The hero's Art section shows The 3D puppet: the GLB turning and running in the page (it works offline), with the clip (run or idle), play and pause, the speed, and the camera at front, three-quarter, side or back. Below it: where the .blend is and how to change it, the arms-out picks, the shape in clay, the rig seen through, the turntable, and the guides made from it.

What runs where

  • The job runs in two lanes. The comfy lane does the paintings, the 3D shape and the walks on the AI server, one ComfyUI job at a time (tools/dl/locks/comfy.lock). The local lane does the Blender stages on this PC, so a Blender stage never holds up another job's painting.
  • Godot runs one process at a time across everything (tools/dl/locks/godot.lock), and only one Level Editor runs the queue (tools/dl/locks/jobs.lock); a second one opens read-only.
  • Without the Level Editor, the same steps run from the command line: python tools/puppet.py may runs every stage still missing and stops at each pick (--pick front=1, --pick side=1 back=1, --pick shape=1); --stage <name> --force redoes one stage.

1st step

Paint her with her arms held out

ComfyUI · Flux.2 Klein 9B4.4 minstage aposeyou pick twice

Goes in

MAY-G's front still, then her side and back stills.

Happens

Klein edits the front into a relaxed A-pose: arms 45 degrees out, legs a little apart. Four seeds; you pick one. Then the side and the back in the same pose, three seeds each; you pick again.

Comes out

tools/dl/puppet/may/apose_front.png, apose_side.png, apose_back.png: 1024 × 1024 PNG on a blue screen.

Why the arms must be out: an arm that hangs against the body fuses with it in the 3D model, and a fused arm cannot be given its own bones. The same goes for legs pressed together.

The technical part

  • tools/puppet.py uploads the stills to the AI server's ComfyUI (input folder, 88fits/puppet_may_front_ref.png and so on) and builds Klein's edit graph in code (comfy.klein): the 9B model, the Qwen3 8B text encoder, each reference picture fed in as a ReferenceLatent, 4 steps, cfg 1, 1024 × 1024.
  • One picture at a time, under the ComfyUI lock (tools/dl/locks/comfy.lock), so nothing else on the server runs in between. Each candidate is saved as apose/front_cN.png with a .json beside it (seed, words, references, seconds) and a contact sheet front_sheet.png.
  • A helper score is printed for each front (how many rows between shoulders and hips show arm, torso and arm as three separate pieces). It helps; your eye decides.
  • Your picks: python tools/puppet.py may --pick front=1, then --pick side=1 back=1.

2nd step

Build her 3D shape

ComfyUI · Hunyuan3D 2 multi-viewBlender clean-up3.4 minstage shapeyou pick once

Goes in

The three arms-out paintings, keyed onto white. The side counts twice: as the model's "right" view and, mirrored, as its "left".

Happens

Hunyuan3D 2 multi-view turns four views into a grey 3D mesh. Two tries on two seeds; you pick. Blender then tidies the pick.

Comes out

shape/try_0.glb, try_1.glb (about 4.5 MB each), the pick as shape_raw.glb, and the cleaned shape.glb: one piece, 60,000 vertices, 1.61 m tall.

The technical part

  • The model file: hunyuan3d-dit-v2-mv.safetensors (4.93 GB, Tencent's Hunyuan3D-2mv on Hugging Face, public) in the server's ComfyUI/models/checkpoints/. Nothing was installed into ComfyUI: it already has the nodes.
  • The graph: ImageOnlyCheckpointLoader → one CLIPVisionEncode per view → Hunyuan3Dv2ConditioningMultiView → EmptyLatentHunyuan3Dv2 (resolution 3072) → KSampler (euler, normal, 30 steps, cfg 5) → VAEDecodeHunyuan3D (octree 380, 20,000 chunks) → VoxelToMesh (surface net, threshold 0.6) → SaveGLB.
  • Memory: ComfyUI is restarted before and after, because the server's RAM cannot hold Hunyuan3D next to the video models. Your own queued jobs wait their turn.
  • The GLB comes back over ComfyUI's API to tools/dl/puppet/may/shape/ and Blender renders each try in clay for your pick (python tools/puppet.py may --pick shape=1).
  • Clean-up (Blender in the background, tools/puppet_blender.py mode=clean): keeps the largest piece, remeshes it on a 4.5 mm grid, decimates to 60,000 vertices and 80,000 faces, puts the feet at height 0, faces her toward −Y and scales her to her game height. That height is measured from her own sprite: the figure in her back idle cell over the 256 px cell, times the 1.9 m the game draws a cell, = 1.611 m.

Watch out

  • The licence does not cover the EU. Tencent's Hunyuan3D licence excludes the EU, the UK and South Korea. Before a commercial release, check it with someone who knows licensing or swap this step to an MIT model such as TripoSG. Nothing after this step depends on which model made the shape.
  • Thin parts (the beret's tip, the fingers) come out lumpy. That matters less than it sounds: the video model repaints over the renders.

3rd step

Give her a skeleton

Blender 5.2, in the background7 sstage rigautomated; you can do it by hand

Goes in

shape.glb.

Happens

A script finds her joints by slicing the mesh, places a skeleton with Mixamo's bone names there, and lets Blender work out how the mesh follows each bone.

Comes out

may_puppet.blend: mesh May, skeleton May_Rig (65 bones, mixamorig:Hips …), plus check pictures in rig/.

How the joints are found, in plain words

  • The script cuts the mesh into thin horizontal slices and measures each slice. The neck is the narrowest run between 62 % and 90 % of her height; the shoulders start where the body gets 1.6 times the neck's width; the arms are found where they stand apart from the body (that is why she needs her arms out).
  • The hand tip is the arm's farthest point, the wrist 9.5 % of her height back from it, the elbow half way. The crotch is where the two leg runs merge (0.76 m, 47 % of her height), the hips 4.5 % above it, the ankles at 5.5 %, the knees half way (pushed 1.2 cm forward so they bend the right way).
  • Fingers get bones that do not move the mesh: they only feed the hands of the stick figures in step 5.
  • Then Blender's automatic weights (its "bone heat"): 0 vertices were left without a bone. The head region (9,746 vertices: hair, beret, face) is then tied to the head bone alone so the bob does not stretch with the neck, and no vertex follows more than 4 bones.

Doing it by hand in Blender

This step is automated: you only need what follows when the automatic rig is wrong, for example an elbow in the wrong place, the torso dragged along by an arm, or the beret not following the head. These are real screenshots of Blender 5.2 with tools/dl/puppet/may/may_puppet.blend.

  1. Open the file. Double-click may_puppet.blend, or File > Open in Blender 5.2.

    • 1Object Mode: where you select whole objects.
    • 2May_Rig: the skeleton.
    • 3May: the mesh, a child of the skeleton.
    • 4The bones, drawn over her body. "Left" is HER left: on your right when she faces you.

  2. Move a joint. Click May_Rig in the list, press Tab for Edit Mode, click the end of a bone and press G to move it; X, Y or Z after G keeps it on one axis. Tab again when done.

    • 1Edit Mode.
    • 2The elbow: where mixamorig:LeftArm meets mixamorig:LeftForeArm.
    • 3The bone's name. Keep it: the Mixamo run finds every bone by name.
    • 4Where the bone starts (Head) and ends (Tail), in metres. You can type exact numbers here.

  3. Let Blender redo how the mesh follows. In Object Mode click May, then Shift-click May_Rig (the skeleton last), press Ctrl+P and choose With Automatic Weights.

    • 1With Automatic Weights: recomputes every bone's share of the mesh from your joints in one go.
    • 2May_Rig highlighted: it was clicked last, so it is the parent.

    This undoes the trial's "hair and beret on the head only" fix. Paint that back (next step), or rebuild the whole rig instead with python tools/puppet.py may --stage rig --force, which finds the joints again and discards hand edits.

  4. Or paint a fix by hand. Click May, switch to Weight Paint (Ctrl+Tab), pick a bone's group in the list and paint over the mesh.

    • 1Weight Paint mode.
    • 2The group you paint: mixamorig:LeftArm, the left upper arm. For the beret it is mixamorig:Head.
    • 3Red follows that bone fully, blue not at all, the rainbow in between.
    • 4The brush: Weight 1.0 paints red, 0 paints blue; Strength is how fast.

    Typical fixes: the chest moving with an arm (mixamorig:LeftArm too strong on the chest), the overalls stretching with a leg (mixamorig:LeftUpLeg on the hip), the beret left behind (paint it red on mixamorig:Head).

  5. Check that she moves right. Click May_Rig, switch to Pose Mode, set the bottom editor to the Action Editor and pick run (or idle). Space plays, the arrow keys step.

    • 1Pose Mode.
    • 2The Action Editor.
    • 3The clip: run (22 frames at 30 fps, one stride) or idle (61 frames, 2 s).
    • 4Frame 7 of the run, shown here from the side.
    • 5Pose Position plays the run; Rest Position shows her A-pose.

  6. Save and run the rest from your rig. Ctrl+S under the same name, then:

    python tools/puppet.py may --from-blend

    It skips steps 1 to 3, puts the Mixamo run on your edited skeleton again (moved joints carry the run), recolours, exports the GLB, redraws the guides, re-queues MAY-P and MAY-Q's walks and compares them. Hand changes to the run or idle keys are replaced: change the skeleton and the weights, not the keys.

Or let Mixamo's website rig her

Export the mesh alone (select May, File > Export > FBX, Selected Objects only, or use shape.glb as it is), upload it to mixamo.com, let its auto-rigger place the markers, and download "FBX Binary, Original Pose, With Skin" (not T-pose: her colours are projected from the arms-out paintings). Then:

python tools/puppet.py may --from-fbx <the downloaded file>

The armature and mesh are renamed May_Rig and May, the bones get the mixamorig: prefix if they lack it, and the rest runs as with --from-blend. Tested on an FBX export of our own rig; a real mixamo.com download has not been tried yet.

4th step

Make her run, colour her, save the GLB

Blender, in the background7 sstage anim

Goes in

may_puppet.blend, the Mixamo run tools/dl/mixamo/running.fbx (the clip our heroes' run drivers come from), the arms-out paintings.

Happens

The run is moved onto her skeleton, an idle is added, and her colours are painted onto the mesh from the arms-out pictures.

Comes out

may_puppet.glb (4.2 MB, clips run 0.7 s and idle 2 s), may_puppet_preview.png, a copy of both in Drive: My Drive/88fits builds/puppets/.

The technical part

  • Retarget. Mixamo's run was made for a T-posed body and May rests in an A-pose, so each bone's motion is carried over as a turn of direction rather than copied. Her hips travel 0.838 as far as Mixamo's (her legs are shorter), the stride is lifted 2.3 cm so the lowest sole touches the ground, and she runs in place.
  • Idle. There is no Mixamo idle on disk, so it is made in code: arms down at her sides, a breath, a 1 cm sway and a nod over 2 seconds. A downloaded Mixamo idle FBX can replace it.
  • Colours. Each arms-out picture is projected onto the mesh; a ray from each vertex decides whether that picture really sees it. The side pictures skip the band of the torso the hanging arm covers, and a vertex no picture sees takes its nearest neighbour's colour. They are stored as vertex colours (the GLB's COLOR_0), not a texture.
  • The GLB is glTF's one-file form: the mesh, the 65-bone skin and both clips. It opens in Windows' 3D Viewer on the PC; on the phone, download it from Drive into a browser glTF viewer such as gltf-viewer.donmccurdy.com. GLB_READY.json is written next to it so the lead can tell you it exists.

Choices here: the clip (any Mixamo FBX in tools/dl/mixamo/; running.fbx by default) and the idle (made in code, or an FBX). Neither changes the time noticeably.

5th step

Draw the guides the video model follows

Blender + Python41 sstage guides

Goes in

The rig and the GLB.

Happens

Her run is rendered in our five views (back, back-diagonal, side, front-diagonal, front) through the game camera's 25-degree tilt, 24 poses a stride, as stick figures from her own bones and as depth videos.

Comes out

tools/dl/drivers/may_puppet_run/ (stick figures), tools/dl/drivers/may_puppet_run_depth/ (depth + clay), silhouette.json.

The technical part

  • Stick figures from her own bones. Same camera, framing, frame count and folder layout as our Mixamo drivers (tools/pose_driver.py), so a look's recipe can name the folder per view in pose_drivers. Each view is a folder of driver_00.png … driver_24.png plus poses.json (the joint positions); meta.json and contact sheets sit beside them.
  • Hidden joints are dropped. A ray from each elbow, wrist, knee and ankle toward the camera checks whether her own body is in the way; if it is, the joint is left out so the video model does not paint a hand through her back. In the back view her right wrist is hidden in 4 of 25 frames and her left in 11.
  • Depth videos come from the GLB (tools/blender_depth_drivers.py, the same tool the scorpion and the beetle walk on): ctl_00.png … ctl_48.png per view (two strides and one frame, so the video model can settle on the second) and a clay.png.
  • silhouette.json: how tall her run stands against her idle in each view, measured on the 3D puppet itself (side 0.938, back 1.015). It is the ground truth for the size of the painted walks in step 7.

What went wrong in the trial: the side face

The first side walk painted May with her face turned to the camera on a body running sideways. A profile rule for the face points did not fix it, nor did levelling the shoulders. Swapping in Mixamo's head points did, which showed the cause: on her own head bone the ear dot sat straight above the neck (her bob pulls the bone back), while Mixamo's sits well ahead of it. The guide now places the ears from her measured nose, the way pose_driver.py does.

6th step

Paint the walks

ComfyUI · Wan 2.2 Animate / Fun ControlGodot import10–12 minstage looks

Goes in

MAY-G's five view stills and the guides from step 5.

Happens

A look job in the Level Editor's queue walks each still along its guide, one view at a time, packs the strip and imports it into the game.

Comes out

Two new looks in may.json, marked fresh: MAY-P (may_sprite_p.png) and MAY-Q (may_sprite_q.png), 62 columns each.

MAY-P walks every view on the puppet's stick figures with Wan 2.2 Animate. MAY-Q walks the back, back-diagonal, side and front-diagonal on the depth videos with Wan 2.2 Fun Control, and the front on the stick figure (the depth video carries no face, see below). Both have no run_scale: whether the puppet keeps her size by itself was part of the test.

The technical part

  • The recipe. The new looks copy MAY-G's recipe (its stills "g", its seeds and words) minus run_scale, and name the guides: pose_drivers = may_puppet_run in every view; for MAY-Q also ctl_drivers = may_puppet_run_depth for fd, side, bd and back, plus words for the face and hair (anim_hint, anim_negative).
  • The walks. tools/gen_art.py sends one ComfyUI graph per view. Wan Animate makes 25 frames on 25 stick figures. Fun Control makes 49 frames on the 49 depth frames and keeps the second stride. Each clip is cut out of its blue screen and fitted to the cell (tools/dl/ai_anim/may_mayq_ctl_run_side/full_00.png …, cycle.json, cycle_sheet.png). A clip whose figure leaves the frame is rolled again on another seed automatically.
  • Into the game, done by the Level Editor after a landing (1–3 minutes):
    1. The strip lands in game/content/characters/may/ and the look's entry in may.json (fresh, never played until you choose).
    2. tools/lossy_imports.py sets the new picture's .png.import file to lossy WebP 0.75, so a 62-column strip does not add megabytes to the APK.
    3. --import (through tools/godot.sh, which lets only one Godot run at a time) turns the PNG into the texture the game loads, under game/.godot/imported/.
    4. tools/import_json.gd writes may.tres again from may.json: the Inspector-editable resource the game reads first.

The verdict

The method works end to end with no human step but the three picks. As a guide for the video model it ties MAY-G's hand-tuned Mixamo drivers and wins where her own proportions matter: the side run keeps her size without a size fix, and the depth walks keep her turned away in the back-diagonal. It is not a clear win yet: MAY-P's front head runs big, P and Q have more rainbow fringes on the sleeves than G, and the depth video carries no face. Those gaps are now settings of the method, all on by default: size from the 3D model, frame by the soles, clean colour fringes and keep the face angle. MAY-R was made with all of them: every view now runs at the 3D model's own height (an average error of 0.005, against 0.035 to 0.052 for the others) and the fringes are gone. Its front and back heads read a little bigger than the others', because framing by the soles makes the figure smaller in the picture.

To see them: install the build from Drive, own every hero from the developer menu, turn on debug mode, open the arena's HERO tab, choose May and tap MAY-G, MAY-P, MAY-Q and MAY-R.
