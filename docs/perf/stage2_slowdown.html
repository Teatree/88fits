Performance report, for your decision

Why Stage 2 got slow on your Pixel 8 Pro

In your run of 4 November (Drifter, Stage 2, 3:04 long) the game held 60 fps for 2:28, then fell to about 26 fps within 8 seconds. Three things stack up: too many creatures for the phone to run at 60 fps, a catch-up rule that turns a slightly late frame into a very late one, and the phone itself slowing down. I've changed nothing yet. Your options are in the middle of this page.

The three causes, biggest first

1. The crowd: 15 more spiders every 5 seconds, with nothing to stop it

Stage 2's cap is 500 from the first second, and your row r22 sends 15 Desert Spiders every 5 s from 0:50. That's 3 a second against your level 9 hero's 1 kill a second, so the crowd hit 318 at 3:01, 241 of them spiders. Almost all the game's script time scales with this number: moving and colliding creatures and writing them to the GPU. A warm phone has room for about 210 at 60 fps.

2. The cliff: one late frame costs twice the work

The world runs in fixed 1/60 s steps. When a frame takes longer than 16.7 ms, the next frame runs two steps to catch up, which adds 10 ms, which makes it later still. So the phone doesn't get slightly slower, it falls off a step: from 17 ms to 38 ms with only 27% more creatures. It's why it felt sudden. Pass 102 capped this at two steps so that it can't spiral further, but the drop to half speed remains.

3. The phone slows down when it's warm

From 2:29 to 2:52, moving the crowd, drawing it and everything else all took about twice as long together, then all recovered together, with more creatures on screen than before. No game system behaves like that; a CPU changing speed does. Earlier runs show the same: the same crowd cost 2.4 ms a step at the start of a fresh run and 4.4 ms a few minutes in. Reviews measured the Pixel 8's chip losing 58-60% of its CPU speed under long load. This is my best reading, not a measurement: the record doesn't log the phone's temperature or clock speed. I'd like to add that.

A Set a crowd budget for the phone

This can also be automatic: hold new spawns only while recent frames run over budget, so a cool phone and the PC keep the full crowd. That's a small rule, and it would be named in BALANCING.

Risk: Balance: fewer kills means slightly less XP in that stretch

B Take away the cliff

With one world step a frame the extra 10 ms never lands. The world then runs at about 60% speed during the overload instead of stuttering at full speed, the same trade EVE Online makes with its time dilation.

Risk: Replays record the steps, so they stay correct for new runs but need a re-test

C Rewrite the crowd's loops in compiled code (GDExtension, C++)

I found no trustworthy public benchmark for this exact case: the 5x to 20x figures around are marketing. The trial is what gives you a real number.

Risk: Moderate: every new build must keep replays exact, and a crash can no longer be read as easily from the phone. I would first do a one-pass trial on the separation loop only and measure it on your phone

D Move the crowd less often

Today creatures on screen steer every 2nd tick and those off screen every 4th, but every creature still moves and separates every tick.

Risk: Low. Rules change slightly, so old replays need their own build, which they already have

E Draw far-away creatures half as often

The game writes every drawn creature's position and frame to the GPU every frame, one call at a time.

Risk: Low

F Fewer draw calls for scenery

The PC probe counts about 170 draw calls and 700-790 objects. Scenery, walls and shadows could merge further. It also lowers power use, which helps with heat.

Risk: Low

G Keep the phone cooler

Android has a thermal API that tells a game how close it is to throttling (ADPF). NCSoft's Lineage W went from falling to 32 fps on a Pixel 6 to a steady 60 by lowering quality before heat hit. Godot has no support for it, so it needs a small Android plugin. It could drive option A's automatic budget.

Risk: Low

H Change the sprite sheets' file type or compression

See the section below.

Risk: Low. It's per file and easy to undo

What others do

I found little written specifically about Godot survivor games on phones, so treat these as nearby experience. Reliable sources are the Godot and Android docs; the rest are reports from individuals.

  • Godot docs: Optimization using MultiMeshes: We already draw this way. It says GDScript is fine for a few thousand instances, and other languages for far more.
  • Godot docs: importing images: Lossy quality affects disk size only; VRAM compression lowers memory and sampling bandwidth; on the Compatibility renderer it means ETC2, since the high-quality ASTC option is ignored there.
  • Godot docs: General optimization: Measure first; the frame is set by the slower of CPU and GPU, so a GPU fix can't help a CPU-bound frame.
  • Horde add-on devlog (Spare Parts Studio): Thousands of Godot enemies in plain GDScript, using flat arrays and one draw call per type. 3,300 enemies at 150 fps against 7 as nodes in a browser demo: the seller's own number, desktop. We already do both techniques.
  • Ezcha: Rendering a million objects in Godot: A million static objects at 160 fps through server calls. It shows drawing isn't the limit when nothing moves: the cost is updating moving things from script, which is our case.
  • Godot forum: native code instead of GDScript: The common advice: write it in GDScript first and move only proven hot spots to native code. No hard numbers.
  • r/godot: C# vs GDScript with large for loops: Community view that a hybrid works best. Anecdotal.
  • Viblo: Godot 4 on Android, 5 tips: ETC2/ASTC, profiling on the device, MultiMesh for hundreds of sprites. An anecdote; its numbers aren't verified.
  • Beebom: Tensor G3 benchmarks and thermals: A 15-minute CPU stress test lost about 60% of peak.
  • Notebookcheck: Pixel 8 stress tests: A 58% CPU drop under sustained load. 2023 launch-era tests; updates may have changed this.
  • Android: ADPF story, NCSoft Lineage W: 60 fps fell to 32 on a Pixel 6 in 30 minutes; with thermal-aware quality it held 60.
  • Imagination: Do Not Use Discard: Alpha test is costly on tile-based GPUs. This is PowerVR's guidance, not Mali's; I didn't find Arm numbers.

My recommendation

A and B now. Both are small and neither costs anything you can see in normal play. Together they should take the worst stretch from 26 fps to a steady 50-60 on your phone. I'd also add the phone's temperature to the run record, so the next report can show cause 3 as a fact.

Then a trial of C on your phone. If it gives the speed I expect, it's the option that lets you keep 500 creatures and removes the need for D, E and most of G. If you'd rather not take on compiled code, D and E give most of the same gain in smaller steps.
