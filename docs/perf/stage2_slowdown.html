The causes, re-ranked

1. The phone slows down

Your phone runs at full speed for the first few minutes of a run, then dips to about two thirds for 10 to 40 seconds at a time, and later stays near 60%. Heat is the most likely reason. Reviews of the Tensor G3 measured its CPU losing 58-60% under long load, and the windows start 2:30 to 6 minutes into a run. It could also be battery saver or charging, which I can't see from here. The replay proves that the phone changed speed; it doesn't prove why. Option G's plugin would log the heat and settle it.

2. The cliff makes every dip about twice as bad

When a frame runs over 16.7 ms, the next one runs two world steps to catch up, which makes it later still. In Stage 1's slow windows, a frame with one world step took 22 ms and a frame with two took 31 ms, and two thirds of the frames ran two. Without the cliff, a phone at two thirds of its speed would give about 45 fps, not 30.

3. The crowd, in Stage 2 and Endless

Stage 2's 15 spiders every 5 seconds pushed the crowd to 318, and Endless in this run reached 300 at 29 fps. That's still too much for a warm phone at 60 fps, as the first version found. But it isn't what slowed Stage 1.

What others do

I found little written specifically about Godot survivor games on phones, so treat these as nearby experience. Reliable sources are the Godot and Android docs; the rest are reports from individuals.

  • Godot docs: Optimization using MultiMeshes: We already draw this way. It says GDScript is fine for a few thousand instances, and other languages for far more.
  • Godot docs: importing images: Lossy quality affects disk size only; VRAM compression lowers memory and sampling bandwidth; on the Compatibility renderer it means ETC2, since the high-quality ASTC option is ignored there.
  • Godot docs: General optimization: Measure first; the frame is set by the slower of CPU and GPU, so a GPU fix can't help a CPU-bound frame.
  • Horde add-on devlog (Spare Parts Studio): Thousands of Godot enemies in plain GDScript, using flat arrays and one draw call per type. 3,300 enemies at 150 fps against 7 as nodes in a browser demo: the seller's own number, desktop. We already do both techniques.
  • Ezcha: Rendering a million objects in Godot: A million static objects at 160 fps through server calls. It shows drawing isn't the limit when nothing moves: the cost is updating moving things from script, which is our case.
  • Godot forum: native code instead of GDScript: The common advice: write it in GDScript first and move only proven hot spots to native code. No hard numbers.
  • r/godot: C# vs GDScript with large for loops: Community view that a hybrid works best. Anecdotal.
  • Viblo: Godot 4 on Android, 5 tips: ETC2/ASTC, profiling on the device, MultiMesh for hundreds of sprites. An anecdote; its numbers aren't verified.
  • Beebom: Tensor G3 benchmarks and thermals: A 15-minute CPU stress test lost about 60% of peak.
  • Notebookcheck: Pixel 8 stress tests: A 58% CPU drop under sustained load. 2023 launch-era tests; updates may have changed this.
  • Android: ADPF story, NCSoft Lineage W: 60 fps fell to 32 on a Pixel 6 in 30 minutes; with thermal-aware quality it held 60.
  • Android: ADPF overview: Mobile chips change clock speed and core type as they heat. ADPF's Thermal API lets a game adjust before that, and its performance hint describes the game's frame work to Android.
  • Android: best practices for ADPF: Scale individual settings (resolution, particles, effects) in small steps as heat rises, rather than whole presets, and test sessions of 15 minutes or more.
  • Android: Thermal API: Thermal headroom runs from 0 (no throttling) to 1 (heavy throttling), and a game can ask for it ahead of time.
  • Android: sustained performance mode: A phone maker can offer steady, lower clocks instead of a fast start that throttles. Their example: a steady 45 fps instead of 60 falling to 30. Support depends on the device.
  • Defold: ADPF extension: Another engine's wrapper for both APIs: a hint session for the main thread and a headroom forecast. The closest model for a Godot plugin; I found none for Godot.
  • Imagination: Do Not Use Discard: Alpha test is costly on tile-based GPUs. This is PowerVR's guidance, not Mali's; I didn't find Arm numbers.

My recommendation, updated

First, play the new build. C is in it. Try Stage 2 or Endless, where the crowd is big. The next report can then use real numbers from your phone, because each run record says whether the compiled crowd ran.

Next, B. It's small, costs nothing you can see, and helps every slow stretch on every stage, which C can't fully do for a small crowd. Stage 1's slow windows should go from about 30 fps to about 45, and more with C on top.

Then G, as a small Android plugin, which goes after the main cause. First it writes the phone's heat into the run record, so we know rather than guess. Then it tells Android each frame's deadline, and eases the game off when the phone is close to slowing down.

Then more creatures. The pool still stops at 320, so the map's cap of 500 can't be reached. Raising it is one number, but it changes how hard the stages are, so I'd rather you see the phone's numbers first and choose. A automatic budget and I are still there if you want them.
