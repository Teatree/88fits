# Review of the reference: "Blitz Brigade: Krazy Robots, PHD Creation Kick-off v.002"

The reference is `_REFERENCES/BB-KrazyRobots_KickOff_v002.pdf`: a 15-slide, 16:9 PowerPoint deck from Gameloft Kharkiv
(November 2016) that pitches a mobile mech shooter before production starts. This note says what in it is right for us,
what is weak, and what we left out. The 88fits deck built from it is `88fits_OnePager.html` (and its PDF) in this folder.

## The slides, in order

| # | Slide | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | Logo, a mech render, "PHD creation kick-off, v.002" |
| 2 | Positioning | Two-sentence pitch, a phone with a screenshot, a spec line (platform, tech, business model, audience), and a Venn of the market: mobile shooters x robot games, "only 2 titles with 1M+ installs" |
| 3 | References | Four groups of games, each with what it lends: battle mechanics, simpler level design and camera, touch controls and aim assist, matchmaking and game modes |
| 4 | Visual style | "Blitz Brigade meets Overwatch", two screenshots and a character |
| 5 | Benchmark | A table against three competitors: store size, setting, style, modes, gameplay, roster, customisation, levels, rewards, progression, chart ranks |
| 6 | Core gameplay | The action loop as a circular diagram (mech combat, eject, pilot combat, summon), with the two combat styles described |
| 7-8 | Characters | Four mech classes (role, weapons, ultimate, weakness, four stat bars) and the pilots, with a human-scale silhouette |
| 9-10 | Customisation | Parts and their rarity and upgrade tiers, "3x3 x 2x2x2x2 = 144 visually distinct mechs per class", then decals and procedural paint (Substance) |
| 11 | Controls mockup | The HUD and touch zones drawn on a phone |
| 12 | Game flow | Loading, hangar, matchmaking (with a patent reference), battle |
| 13 | Meta loop | Battle, session goals, gacha, upgrades, leagues, events, IAP |
| 14 | Summary | Three takeaways with pictures |
| 15 | Thank you | Contact line |

## Just right (we kept these)

- **The order.** Each slide answers the next question a reader has: what is it, where does it sit in the market, what does
  it borrow, how does it look, how does it compare, how does it play, who do you play, how do you grow, how do you
  control it, how does a session flow, why come back, so what. Our deck follows the same order.
- **Positioning as two sentences plus a spec line.** Platform, technology, business model and audience on one line is
  the fastest way to frame everything after it.
- **The market Venn.** One picture argues "why this game": the overlap of two crowded circles is nearly empty. We drew
  ours as survivor-likes on phones x Megabonk's kind of run (PC only), after a market scan showed that a plain "3D
  survivor-likes in portrait" circle would be wrong: Archero, Monster Survivors and Zombie Waves are 3D and portrait.
- **References with a job each.** Every game is named for what it lends, never as a mood board. Our bible already works
  this way ("We take / We skip"), so the slide takes it from there.
- **"X meets Y" for the art.** One line a reader can repeat.
- **One benchmark table, the same rows for every game.** Differences jump out without reading paragraphs.
- **Loops as diagrams.** The action loop and the meta loop are pictures, not lists.
- **Character cards with comparable bars and a weakness**, and the small human silhouette that gives the scale.
- **Variety as a number** (144 mechs per class). A count is more convincing than "lots of customisation".
- **Controls drawn on the device.** For a touch game this is the most important picture after the gameplay.
- **A three-point summary.**

## Weak (we did these differently)

- **The cover has no hook.** It says "kick-off v.002", not why the game is fun. Ours opens on key art, the tagline and
  the one-line pitch.
- **Almost every picture belongs to another game** (Overwatch, Titanfall 2, MechWarrior, Hawken). That is normal before
  production, but the reader never sees Krazy Robots itself. We have a playable game, so our own screenshots, captures
  and paintings carry the deck; other games appear only on the references and benchmark slides, labelled as theirs.
- **Charts without a legend or a scale.** The class stat bars use three colours that are never explained. Ours label
  every bar and axis and say where each number comes from.
- **Claims without sources or dates.** "Only 2 titles with 1M+ installs" and the chart ranks have neither. Every market
  figure in our deck carries its source and date.
- **Readability.** Long paragraphs in an italic comic face, light grey text on pale paper, small type. Ours keeps the
  game's slab face for titles only and sets text in a readable serif at presentation size.
- **The pillars arrive last** (in the summary). Ours states them on slide 3, so the rest of the deck proves them.
- **Missing for a kick-off:** where the project stands, how fast it moves, what comes next and what it needs. Ours adds
  a production slide (velocity, tests, content counts) and next steps.

## Not necessary (we left these out)

- **The thank-you slide.** The summary ends the deck.
- **Matchmaking and the patent.** Too deep for a kick-off, and 88fits has no matchmaking.
- **Two slides each for characters and customisation.** One each is enough; the second slides were detail.
- **Production technology inside the pitch** (Substance for decals). Our art pipeline and tools get their own closing
  section, because they are part of the case for the team (Garry asked for them), not a feature of the game.
- **The "-z" spellings** (Referencez, Characterz, Upgradez). Fun once, but they cost credibility on every slide.
