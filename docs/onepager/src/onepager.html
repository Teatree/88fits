No. {{NO}} · Positioning

Megabonk's run, held upright, under one thumb

88fits is a survivor-shooter for a phone held upright: drag to run, and the guns aim themselves.

A small, goofy hero crosses a painted desert on real 3D ground while hundreds of creatures pour in. Every level-up deals three cards, and the build mutates and evolves. A stage lasts 7 to 13 minutes and ends with a boss, a portal and a choice: bank the gold, or stay for Endless.

No. {{NO}} · Pillars

Six promises the whole game keeps

01

One thumb, zero menus

Every in-run action is hold, slide or tap. Anything that needs a second finger is cut or automated.

02

Portrait is the point

The tall screen shows more ahead than beside. Threat comes from the top, and steering through it is the skill.

03

Cards go brr

Five weapons, five cards, passive items without a cap, mutations and evolutions. Runs feel different because builds do.

04

A crowd, not a queue

Dozens of creatures from the first minute and hundreds in Endless, each one a little different. A problem you can read.

05

Goofy and readable

Anime heroes and creatures painted as 2.5D cut-outs on real 3D ground. Silly on purpose, clear at a glance on a phone.

06

Content is data

A new weapon, creature or hero is a folder of data and pictures, never new code, made and tuned in our Level Editor.

No. {{NO}} · The bestiary

28 creatures, each with its own brain

Brains, not stats

Chasers, flockers, chargers, kamikazes, kiters, casters, healers, ambushers and artillery: each creature behaves, not only hits.

Specials

A rare creature glows in its own colour: 15 times the health, a 35% chance of a chest. The weapons pick it out first.

Four Elite modifiers (explosive, shielded, speedy, splitter), a sniper with a map-wide beam, a harpooner that reels you in.

Many looks

Most creatures have several painted looks (white, gold, rose shells for the night): 99 creature looks so far.

No. {{NO}} · Controls and HUD

Everything happens under one thumb

    {{HUD_LIST}}

The rules we hold to

  • No aiming, no fire button, no jump button.
  • The joystick appears wherever the thumb lands; lift to stop.
  • The camera never rotates: the stick maps straight to the screen.
  • The hero's hurt jiggles the health bar, never the screen.
  • Cards are full-width and sized for a thumb.
  • Touch is read raw, never as a mouse, and every control is proven by a scripted finger: 1,754 controls on every build.

No. {{NO}} · Game flow and meta loop

Every run pays, even a death

Bank goldEvery run's purse comes home, a death too; Elites and bosses pay five times.
UpgradeEight talents, two forms each, six ranks: Grit, Stride, Fortune, Stakes...
Unlock30 quests open 5 heroes, 11 weapons and 12 passive items.
Go deeperBeat a stage to open the next; raise Stakes for more gold and XP.

↺ and back to the title, one step stronger

No energy, no timers, no gacha: every unlock is earned in play. The Collection shows only what you have met, so it fills as you explore. On the map, totem poles, a shop, a stranger's deals and teleports give each run its own detours.

No. {{NO}} · Behind the scenes: the art workflow

Any 3D model becomes a painted, animated creature

Our own method, here on the Fire Ant: the model's walk is rendered as depth, and the painting follows it frame by frame.

  1. 2

    Our Blender tools rig six legs, add antennae and render clay in the five game views.

  2. 3

    A walk is made for the rig and rendered as depth, frame by frame.

  3. 4

    Flux.2 repaints the side view in our house style; four seeds, one picked by eye.

  4. 5

    The other four views are painted to match the pick.

  5. 6

    Wan 2.2 walks each painting along its depth video; the loop is cut, keyed and packed.

Blender, Flux.2 and Wan 2.2 on a local GPU server, queued from the Level Editor; a new look takes 10 to 15 minutes
