You played SP-E and SP-F and wrote: "the results are actually awful. Lots of artifacts, side views are all messed up, pretty much every view looks wrong ... the only view from which either of these 2 spiders look kind of ok is the front". You were right: they are bad, and below is why. Then you asked for two more, both light purple, as close to the 3D model as possible, one on Klein and one on Flux.2 dev, each with many iterations; and, when the first try came out photoreal: "I meant not fully realistic, but the realistic version that is still in our style ... like SP-A level of realism." That is what SP-G (Klein) and SP-H (Flux.2 dev) are: SP-A's real spider anatomy, small eyes and no cartoon face, in the game's cel-shaded style with its outline, light purple, every view painted over the model itself.

Before: SP-B (pass 92) and SP-E, SP-F (pass 93). Kept in the JSON, fresh, not to be played.

1. Where the artifacts came from

For every view, the clay render of the model, the painting the walk starts from, and four frames of the finished walk, side by side. If the damage is already in the painting, more walking iterations cannot fix it; if the painting is fine and the walk is not, it is Wan.

Both, and the painting first.

  • The paintings were not the model. The painter was told to "use the clay only for the anatomy, the pose, the camera angle", and it did not. The three-quarter views (rows 2 and 4) came out as near side views; the back view as a top-down abdomen; every view with a big round cartoon abdomen and short thick legs, where the model has a small low body hung between long legs that arch high. Only the front painting is roughly the model's shape, and that is the view you said looks kind of ok.
  • Wan then had to make two different spiders into one. Wan Fun Control takes its shapes from the depth video (the model) and its colours from the painting. Where the two agree it is clean; where they do not, it produces exactly what you saw: the model's thin spiky legs painted lavender on the cartoon body (side, three-quarter views), faded grey and fuzzy ghost legs where the painting had a leg the depth did not (front, side), a shrunken body on the back where the painting's big abdomen had no depth under it. The 4-step speed-up made the smears worse (see Wan below), but it did not cause them.

So the iterations had to go first into a painting that is the model, view by view, and second into the walk.

And a third thing, found only when the new paintings were walked: the model's own legs. Your spider's legs are stacked cones, one per bone, and each cone's wide end stands past the joint. From the three-quarter and side cameras those wide ends read as a row of crystal spikes above the body, and the depth video, which is what Wan follows, is a mass of them. A painting with smooth legs is walked back into spikes whatever the painter did, in every look since SP-A. The front view escapes it (the knees are seen edge-on), which is why the front was always the one view that looked right.

2. The new way to paint: the model itself, repainted

Every look before this pass described the clay to the painter as one of three pictures (style picture first, clay second). The new mode paint_mode: "repaint" turns that round:

  • The clay is the first picture and the one being painted. The words say "Repaint this exact 3D model ... Keep the model's silhouette, its pose and the camera angle exactly: every leg stays where it is, at the same angle and length, bending at the same joints ... nothing is added that the model does not have." The other four views get the chosen side painting as their second picture (colours and markings), and the style anchor last ("the art style only": anchor: true), so the house style stays.
  • The clay is cropped round the spider (clay_fill 0.8), so the painter sees it big; the painting is put back exactly where the crop came from before the walk, so it lies on the depth video's spider.
  • The painting starts from the clay itself (init: img2img). The grey render is coloured lavender on the pure green screen and the painter starts from it, partly noised, instead of from pure noise. This is what really holds every leg where the model has it: measured as the overlap of the painting's outline with the clay's (1 = identical), plain prompting gives 0.5–0.7, starting from the clay 0.75–0.95.

3. SP-G: Klein, many iterations

Klein 9B is a distilled model meant for 4 steps; the non-distilled Klein base exists on the server only as the small 4B, not the 9B. What "many iterations" honestly means for it, measured:

  • More steps: only when starting from the clay. Painted from scratch, 8, 16 and 28 steps gave the same picture as 4 with a little more fine detail (and a duller green screen). Starting from the clay, the steps are what clears the model's flat facets and fins: 4 steps keeps crystal-like legs, 16 is clean, 28 cleanest. SP-G paints at 28 steps (about 100–150 s a picture against 25 s).
  • Many seeds, picked by eye: 8 side candidates (after three earlier sets of 6 that kept the model's fins), 4 per other view. The outline overlap with the clay is printed for each (iou.py), but the pick is by eye: a high overlap can mean the fins were copied.
  • A blurred start (init.blur 10): softens the model's facets before Klein starts, so it keeps the shapes but not the polygons.
  • Refine rounds (the painting painted over again from itself) were tried and dropped: every round turned it pinker and hairier, none removed a wrong shape.
  • The Klein base 4B (50 real steps, cfg 4, a real negative prompt; 2 min a picture) was tried on the side: no better than the 9B, more saturated, and it kept the model's fins; not used.

4. SP-H: Flux.2 dev, many iterations

  • Steps: 40 without the Turbo LoRA (the old full quality was 28). 28 against 50 steps on the same seed gave the same picture with slightly finer hair (5 against 8 minutes), so 40 is enough; above that the time buys nothing visible.
  • Flux.2 dev barely follows the clay as a picture: painted from scratch, it ignores the model's pose (overlap 0.5). Started from the clay it follows it, but only on a knife's edge: unblurred at denoise 0.85 it holds the model (0.87, and copies its facets); blurred (any amount), with the shading softened, posterized, at 0.91, or even at 0.80 with soft shading, it lets go completely and paints the same free spider every time (0.32–0.37, see below). So SP-H starts from the sharp clay at 0.85, and its seeds decide: of four side candidates one copied the fins, one followed the model loosely and cleanly (picked), two ignored it.
  • Seeds: 4 side candidates, 3 per other view, each 9–12 minutes (the server restarts before every dev picture). A dev refine round from its own painting (denoise 0.6) kept the facets and was not used.

5. The walks, part one: the model's legs made walkable

Three things were tried on the side view with the same SP-G painting, each walked on the full Wan (below), against the walk on the raw depth:

  • The model smoothed (driver setting smooth 2: a subdivision for the renders only; spider_walk_v3). The cones get rounder, the spikes stay: they are the cones' wide ends, not sharp edges.
  • The depth softened (ctl_soft 10 px, pass 92's trick for a cartoon face). Much better: the painting's body, face and legs survive, because Wan no longer sees the spikes sharply; the legs step a little more vaguely.
  • The legs rebuilt (driver setting skin capsule, new: spider_walk_v4, spider_death_v3). The legs' own mesh is thrown away and each leg segment, from one bone's head to the next bone's head, becomes a tapered capsule (the leg's radius 6% of its length at the hip, a point at the foot), skinned to its bone; the body, head and fangs stay the model's. The depth video is then a clean spider from every camera, and the clay too. The bones' tails could not be used: the glTF importer invented them, pointing anywhere, which is also why pass 93 measured the feet from the mesh.

Both looks walk and die on the rebuilt legs. The paintings stay the ones painted over the raw clay: each is put back exactly over the new depth video's figure before the walk, and its legs lie where the bones are either way.

5b. The walks, part two: Wan without the speed-up

Every walk so far ran Wan 2.2 Fun Control with the lightx2v speed-up LoRA: 4 steps, cfg 1. At cfg 1 the negative words are never even computed. The bigger Wan (no other Fun Control model is on the server) is the same model without the LoRA: wan: {"lora": false, "steps": 20, "cfg": 3.5}, 10 steps on each of its two halves, a real cfg, and the negative words ("ghost legs, transparent legs, smeared legs, extra legs ...") now act. 6c. Round 2: the fin taken out of the model, SP-G's three-quarter views painted again

The spike. Both looks walked with a spike on the abdomen in the front view, and the three-quarter and side views had fins along the back. They were not in either painter: your 3D model has three small fins glued on its back and a nub on its head, each a loose 3- or 4-corner piece of the mesh, and the depth video Wan walks along showed them in every view. A new driver setting, drop_islands, deletes such small loose pieces that hang on the body or head bones before the renders (the model file itself is not touched; the fangs and legs are bigger pieces and stay). spider_walk_v5 is spider_walk_v4 with drop_islands 4 (16 seconds on the PC, the gait check passes) and spider_death_v5 the same for the death. The model: six legs and a missing walk

Blendertools/blender_ref.py mode=probeseconds

The probe reads a 3D file and says what it can do. For spider._low_poly.glb it found a rig of 31 bones, one clip called Take 001 (10.6 seconds) and a mesh of 639 points. Three things in that file would have spoiled the result if nobody had looked:

  • Six legs. The bones are left_leg_front, left_leg_center, left_leg_back and the same on the right: three a side, plus two short fangs (left_chap, right_chap). A spider has eight, and the painters copy whatever the clay shows.
  • No walk. The clip is seven seconds of standing still and then a short wander that turns in circles. The scorpion and the beetle had a real walk clip to loop; this one does not.
  • It faces +Y. The probe guessed −Y, Blender's default, because its guess only knew how to read a humanoid's feet. Every view would have come out turned round.

The probe now counts the leg chains and reads a creature's forward from its head bone, so the same file says this at once: "It has 6 leg chains (3 left, 3 right): a walk can be made without a clip (driver_depth mode gait) and a death on its back (mode flip_curl); a missing pair is copied by tools/blender_limb_clone.py." and "forward +Y (head)".

Two more legs

Blendertools/blender_limb_clone.py (new)10 s

Each leg of this model is a separate set of mesh pieces carried by its own four bones, so a leg can be copied whole: the bones are duplicated, the mesh pieces those bones carry are duplicated with their weights moved to the new bones, and the copy is turned round the vertical axis through its hip. The middle pair was copied and turned back, the originals turned a little forward, so the four legs a side fan out like a real spider's (two reaching forward, two back).

"$BLENDER" -b --python tools/blender_limb_clone.py -- \
  src=tools/dl/uploads/3d/spider._low_poly.glb \
  out=tools/dl/uploads/3d/spider_8legs/spider_8legs.glb \
  rest=frame:0 forward=+Y \
  "clone=left_leg_center_:left_leg_mid_:24:-0.06;right_leg_center_:right_leg_mid_:-24:-0.06" \
  "turn=left_leg_center_:-18:0.03;right_leg_center_:18:0.03"
  • rest=frame:0 bakes the clip's first pose into the mesh and makes it the rest pose (Blender's Apply Pose as Rest Pose). The old clip is dropped: its keys were made against the old rest pose and would throw the legs about.
  • clone=chain:new:yaw:shift: every bone whose name starts with left_leg_center_ is copied as left_leg_mid_…, turned 24° (counter-clockwise seen from above) and slid back 6% of the leg's reach so the two hips do not sit on one point.
  • turn=chain:yaw:shift turns an existing leg the same way.

The signs took one try to get right: on a model facing +Y a left leg turns forward with a negative yaw. The first run used the rest pose as it was and turned the legs round hips that were somewhere else; the legs crossed. Both lessons are in the tool's text and in the Level Editor's notes.

A walk made in Blender

Blenderblender_depth_drivers.py mode=gaittools/blender_gait.py (new)10 s

With no walk clip, the walk is made from the legs themselves. The tool finds the legs by name (legs=leg_: every bone with leg in its name whose parent is not a leg starts a chain), works out which side each is on and its order front to back, and moves them in two sets half a stride apart, the way spiders walk: left 1, right 2, left 3, right 4 together, then right 1, left 2, right 3, left 4.

Each leg turns round its hip (swing 18° fore and aft), rises while it steps (lift 24°), bends its knee while it is up (flex 16°), and stays on the ground for 55% of the stride (duty). The body dips twice a stride (bob) and turns a little side to side (sway). Every turn is done in world space round the joint, so the rig's own bone axes, often nonsense in a Sketchfab file, never matter. A creature's real step is too small to read at sprite size (the beetle needed its legs swung 1.7 times wider); the made walk is wide from the start.

"$BLENDER" -b --python tools/blender_depth_drivers.py -- \
  src=tools/dl/uploads/3d/spider_8legs/spider_8legs.glb out=tools/dl/drivers/spider_walk \
  mode=gait forward=+Y legs=leg_ frames=12 cycles=2 swing=18 lift=24 flex=16

Fixing the legs: what was wrong and how

Blenderblender_gait.py step=track (pass 93)tools/gait_check.py (new)15 s

You played SP-B and wrote: "currently most legs aren't moving, and the ones that are moving are moving in the wrong direction". You were right, and the fault was not the painter: it was the walk made in Blender (step 3). Every look on spider_walk inherited it.

What was wrong

1. Each leg was turned like a clock hand round its hip. The first walk turned every leg 18° forward and back round the vertical line through its hip. That is the right motion for a leg that sticks straight out to the side: its foot then moves forward and back. But a spider's front legs point forward and its back legs point back, and turning those round the hip sweeps their feet across the body, left and right, not along it. Seen from above, the feet trace arcs at right angles to each leg:

2. The game looks from the front and from behind most of the time, 25° above the ground. From there, a foot moving forward and back moves towards and away from the camera, and on screen that is almost nothing (it shows at about 40% of its real size, as a small up-and-down). So the middle legs, the only ones stepping the right way, looked still; and the front and back legs, the ones that did visibly move, slid sideways. That is your two observations exactly:

The side view, the one view where forward and back is visible, had the opposite problem: there the front and back legs moved along a short arc and slid back only 19–35 px a stride, against 71–98 px for the middle pair; half the legs barely stepped.

3. The painter was not to blame. To be sure, the painted SP-B walk was compared with its depth video frame by frame: at every foot, the painted picture's motion (optical flow) against the driver's foot motion. Where a leg is visible, the paint moves the same way as the driver (a match of 0.6 to 0.98 out of 1, at 50–98% of the driver's distance). The legs that did not follow are the ones hidden behind the big painted abdomen in the side views. Wan 2.2 copied the walk faithfully, sideways sweep and all.

The fix: every foot walks along the body

The new way of moving a foot is step=track (the old one stays as step=swing, the default, so the old drivers are unchanged):

  • A track per foot. Each foot follows the same straight track along the body's length: on the ground it slides straight back under the body at an even speed (it stays planted while the body walks over it), then comes forward through the air on an arc that peaks mid-step. The two sets of four still alternate (L1 R2 L3 R4 against R1 L2 R3 L4).
  • The leg reaches it, not the other way round. For each frame the hip turns round the vertical just enough to face the foot's new place, then the hip and the knee bend in the leg's own upright plane so the foot lands exactly there (two-bone reaching, the knee kept up like a spider's). So a front leg reaching forward stretches out instead of swinging sideways.
  • Room to stretch: crouch. This model's legs rest almost straight, so a front leg reaching forward or a back leg reaching back had nothing to give and lay flat on the ground (left below). Pulling every foot's track in by 15% of its reach bends the knees a little at rest and leaves room.
"$BLENDER" -b --python tools/blender_depth_drivers.py -- \
  src=tools/dl/uploads/3d/spider_8legs/spider_8legs.glb out=tools/dl/drivers/spider_walk_v2 \
  mode=gait forward=+Y legs=leg_ step=track stride=0.22 raise=0.15 crouch=0.15 sway=0 bob=0.03 tips=1
python tools/gait_check.py tools/dl/drivers/spider_walk_v2
  • stride 0.22: how far each foot travels fore and aft, a share of the spider's length with its legs (here 90 px of the side view).
  • raise 0.15: how high a foot lifts mid-step, a share of the same length. From the front and back, a step shows mostly as this lift, so it is bigger than a real spider's.
  • crouch 0.15: the knees' room, above.
  • sway 0 (it was 2.5°): turning the body side to side drags every planted foot sideways with it; a spider's body runs level. bob 0.03: a smaller dip.
  • tips=1: the driver also writes tips.json, where every foot is in every view's frame; gait_check.py draws the paths and names any leg that steps the wrong way.

Softened depth (creature_ctl recipe)

rest=frame:<n>, clone=chain:new:yaw[:shift];…, turn=chain:yaw[:shift];…, forward, shot. Yaw is counter-clockwise from above; shift is a share of the leg's reach along forward.
