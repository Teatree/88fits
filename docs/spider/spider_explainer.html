The model: six legs and a missing walk

Blendertools/blender_ref.py mode=probeseconds

The probe reads a 3D file and says what it can do. For spider._low_poly.glb it found a rig of 31 bones, one clip called Take 001 (10.6 seconds) and a mesh of 639 points. Three things in that file would have spoiled the result if nobody had looked:

  • Six legs. The bones are left_leg_front, left_leg_center, left_leg_back and the same on the right: three a side, plus two short fangs (left_chap, right_chap). A spider has eight, and the painters copy whatever the clay shows.
  • No walk. The clip is seven seconds of standing still and then a short wander that turns in circles. The scorpion and the beetle had a real walk clip to loop; this one does not.
  • It faces +Y. The probe guessed −Y, Blender's default, because its guess only knew how to read a humanoid's feet. Every view would have come out turned round.

The probe now counts the leg chains and reads a creature's forward from its head bone, so the same file says this at once: "It has 6 leg chains (3 left, 3 right): a walk can be made without a clip (driver_depth mode gait) and a death on its back (mode flip_curl); a missing pair is copied by tools/blender_limb_clone.py." and "forward +Y (head)".

One more trap: the file's rest pose is not its stance. Without the clip playing, every leg sits far off to one side (the model came from Sketchfab as an FBX turned into a GLB, and the skin was bound in another pose). Anything that works from the rest pose, like copying a leg, has to fix that first.

2

Two more legs

Blendertools/blender_limb_clone.py (new)10 s

Each leg of this model is a separate set of mesh pieces carried by its own four bones, so a leg can be copied whole: the bones are duplicated, the mesh pieces those bones carry are duplicated with their weights moved to the new bones, and the copy is turned round the vertical axis through its hip. The middle pair was copied and turned back, the originals turned a little forward, so the four legs a side fan out like a real spider's (two reaching forward, two back).

"$BLENDER" -b --python tools/blender_limb_clone.py -- \
  src=tools/dl/uploads/3d/spider._low_poly.glb \
  out=tools/dl/uploads/3d/spider_8legs/spider_8legs.glb \
  rest=frame:0 forward=+Y \
  "clone=left_leg_center_:left_leg_mid_:24:-0.06;right_leg_center_:right_leg_mid_:-24:-0.06" \
  "turn=left_leg_center_:-18:0.03;right_leg_center_:18:0.03"
  • rest=frame:0 bakes the clip's first pose into the mesh and makes it the rest pose (Blender's Apply Pose as Rest Pose). The old clip is dropped: its keys were made against the old rest pose and would throw the legs about.
  • clone=chain:new:yaw:shift: every bone whose name starts with left_leg_center_ is copied as left_leg_mid_…, turned 24° (counter-clockwise seen from above) and slid back 6% of the leg's reach so the two hips do not sit on one point.
  • turn=chain:yaw:shift turns an existing leg the same way.

The signs took one try to get right: on a model facing +Y a left leg turns forward with a negative yaw. The first run used the rest pose as it was and turned the legs round hips that were somewhere else; the legs crossed. Both lessons are in the tool's text and in the Level Editor's notes.

A walk made in Blender

Blenderblender_depth_drivers.py mode=gaittools/blender_gait.py (new)10 s

With no walk clip, the walk is made from the legs themselves. The tool finds the legs by name (legs=leg_: every bone with leg in its name whose parent is not a leg starts a chain), works out which side each is on and its order front to back, and moves them in two sets half a stride apart, the way spiders walk: left 1, right 2, left 3, right 4 together, then right 1, left 2, right 3, left 4.

Each leg turns round its hip (swing 18° fore and aft), rises while it steps (lift 24°), bends its knee while it is up (flex 16°), and stays on the ground for 55% of the stride (duty). The body dips twice a stride (bob) and turns a little side to side (sway). Every turn is done in world space round the joint, so the rig's own bone axes, often nonsense in a Sketchfab file, never matter. A creature's real step is too small to read at sprite size (the beetle needed its legs swung 1.7 times wider); the made walk is wide from the start.

"$BLENDER" -b --python tools/blender_depth_drivers.py -- \
  src=tools/dl/uploads/3d/spider_8legs/spider_8legs.glb out=tools/dl/drivers/spider_walk \
  mode=gait forward=+Y legs=leg_ frames=12 cycles=2 swing=18 lift=24 flex=16

This first walk had a fault (pass 93)

Turning every leg round its hip moves a sideways leg's foot fore and aft, but sweeps a forward or backward leg's foot across the body. In the game's front and back views most legs looked still and the front and back ones slid sideways. SP-B walks on it; SP-E and SP-F walk on the fixed spider_walk_v2: step 4.

4

Fixing the legs: what was wrong and how

Blenderblender_gait.py step=track (pass 93)tools/gait_check.py (new)15 s

You played SP-B and wrote: "currently most legs aren't moving, and the ones that are moving are moving in the wrong direction". You were right, and the fault was not the painter: it was the walk made in Blender (step 3). Every look on spider_walk inherited it.

What was wrong

1. Each leg was turned like a clock hand round its hip. The first walk turned every leg 18° forward and back round the vertical line through its hip. That is the right motion for a leg that sticks straight out to the side: its foot then moves forward and back. But a spider's front legs point forward and its back legs point back, and turning those round the hip sweeps their feet across the body, left and right, not along it. Seen from above, the feet trace arcs at right angles to each leg:

2. The game looks from the front and from behind most of the time, 25° above the ground. From there, a foot moving forward and back moves towards and away from the camera, and on screen that is almost nothing (it shows at about 40% of its real size, as a small up-and-down). So the middle legs, the only ones stepping the right way, looked still; and the front and back legs, the ones that did visibly move, slid sideways. That is your two observations exactly:

The side view, the one view where forward and back is visible, had the opposite problem: there the front and back legs moved along a short arc and slid back only 19–35 px a stride, against 71–98 px for the middle pair; half the legs barely stepped.

3. The painter was not to blame. To be sure, the painted SP-B walk was compared with its depth video frame by frame: at every foot, the painted picture's motion (optical flow) against the driver's foot motion. Where a leg is visible, the paint moves the same way as the driver (a match of 0.6 to 0.98 out of 1, at 50–98% of the driver's distance). The legs that did not follow are the ones hidden behind the big painted abdomen in the side views. Wan 2.2 copied the walk faithfully, sideways sweep and all.

The fix: every foot walks along the body

The new way of moving a foot is step=track (the old one stays as step=swing, the default, so the old drivers are unchanged):

  • A track per foot. Each foot follows the same straight track along the body's length: on the ground it slides straight back under the body at an even speed (it stays planted while the body walks over it), then comes forward through the air on an arc that peaks mid-step. The two sets of four still alternate (L1 R2 L3 R4 against R1 L2 R3 L4).
  • The leg reaches it, not the other way round. For each frame the hip turns round the vertical just enough to face the foot's new place, then the hip and the knee bend in the leg's own upright plane so the foot lands exactly there (two-bone reaching, the knee kept up like a spider's). So a front leg reaching forward stretches out instead of swinging sideways.
  • Room to stretch: crouch. This model's legs rest almost straight, so a front leg reaching forward or a back leg reaching back had nothing to give and lay flat on the ground (left below). Pulling every foot's track in by 15% of its reach bends the knees a little at rest and leaves room.
"$BLENDER" -b --python tools/blender_depth_drivers.py -- \
  src=tools/dl/uploads/3d/spider_8legs/spider_8legs.glb out=tools/dl/drivers/spider_walk_v2 \
  mode=gait forward=+Y legs=leg_ step=track stride=0.22 raise=0.15 crouch=0.15 sway=0 bob=0.03 tips=1
python tools/gait_check.py tools/dl/drivers/spider_walk_v2
  • stride 0.22: how far each foot travels fore and aft, a share of the spider's length with its legs (here 90 px of the side view).
  • raise 0.15: how high a foot lifts mid-step, a share of the same length. From the front and back, a step shows mostly as this lift, so it is bigger than a real spider's.
  • crouch 0.15: the knees' room, above.
  • sway 0 (it was 2.5°): turning the body side to side drags every planted foot sideways with it; a spider's body runs level. bob 0.03: a smaller dip.
  • tips=1: the driver also writes tips.json, where every foot is in every view's frame; gait_check.py draws the paths and names any leg that steps the wrong way.

The check you can run on any made walk

python tools/gait_check.py tools/dl/drivers/<name> writes gait_check.png into the driver folder (each foot's path on the clay, per view) and prints a verdict per leg: in the side view a planted foot must slide back (else BACKWARDS, or barely steps when it moves under a quarter of the best leg); in the front and back views a foot must not travel further sideways than up and down (else SIDEWAYS). The Level Editor's depth driver job runs it after every made walk.

Two more things the new looks needed

  • Ghost legs: the painting bigger than the clay (fit_ref, new). Klein painted SP-E's front 1.54 times as wide as the depth figure (SP-B's front too). Wan keeps the painting's size, so the painted legs outside the depth's legs stayed on as faded grey legs beside the walking ones. fit_ref scales and moves the painting onto the depth figure before the walk. Alone it went too far: Wan then followed the clay, and the face and the striped abdomen went. With the depth softened 10 px (ctl_soft, pass 92) it keeps both and the legs still step. SP-E and SP-F use "fit_ref": ["front"] and "ctl_soft": {"radius": 10, "body": false, "views": ["front", "side"]} (the soft side view kept the face too, which the sharp one lost, as SP-B's side does).
  • Light blue turned royal blue (no_green "rb", new). The strip's green guard holds every pixel's green down to its red, to kill the screen's spill. Sky blue has more green than red, so the whole of SP-F went periwinkle. "no_green": "rb" holds green down to the larger of red and blue instead: spill still goes, sky blue stays.

What else was suspected, and why it was not the cause

Blenderblender_depth_drivers.py mode=flip_curl8 s

A dead spider lies on its back with its legs folded in over its belly (its leg muscles let go and the legs curl inward). The model has no death clip, so the death is made, the same way as the walk, and rendered as a death driver: 25 depth frames of the front view that the video model walks the front painting along.

The words every look shares

anatomy, sent in every painting and every walk:

exactly eight legs, four on each side, each leg one single continuous jointed limb that starts at the body and ends in one pointed foot, exactly one head with two short fangs, exactly one round abdomen, no antennae, no tail, no wings, no extra legs, the left and right sides symmetrical

Each part of it answers a way many-legged creatures go wrong: a number per side (painters count better per side than in total), "one single continuous limb" (legs that split or merge at a knee), "starts at the body" (a leg growing out of the head), "ends in one pointed foot" (double feet), the absent parts named (an insect's antennae and a scorpion's tail creep in), symmetry (five legs on one side, three on the other).

blockout (how the clay is described): a smooth plain clay 3D model of a spider: a small head with two short fangs at the front, one big oval abdomen behind it, and eight long jointed legs, four on each side, spread round the body. The painter is told to take only the anatomy, the pose, the camera angle and where every leg is from the clay.

words.front, written after an earlier look's front walk grew ghost legs (the painting fanned every leg sideways while the depth had the front pair reaching at the camera): walking straight towards the viewer, seen from the front and a little above: its head and two fangs are at the bottom middle of the picture, nearest the viewer; its two front legs reach forward towards the viewer, one on each side of the head, and its other six legs spread out to the left and the right, three on each side; the round abdomen rises behind the head.

motion (SP-B): scuttling in place on its eight legs like a real spider, the legs stepping in two alternating sets of four, the body low and level. SP-E and SP-F say how a foot moves too: … like a real spider, each foot stepping forward through the air and pushing back along the ground, the legs stepping in two alternating sets of four, the body low and level. The words only help; the walk is the depth video's.

Negative prompts: what they do here

The look keeps a negative list (anim_negative): extra legs, nine legs, ten legs, six legs, legs merging, a leg growing from the head, antennae, a tail, wings, two heads, a second spider, standing upright. In this pipeline it changes nothing, and neither did the scorpion's:

  • Klein (the paintings) runs at cfg 1 with the negative side zeroed. At cfg 1 the sampler uses the positive prompt alone.
  • Flux.2 dev (the better painter, --dev) is guidance-distilled: it has no negative input at all.
  • Wan 2.2 Fun Control (the walks and the death) runs the 4-step speed-up at cfg 1, and ComfyUI then skips the negative pass entirely. The negative words are sent and never read.

To see whether a real negative would help, the side of pass 92's near-real look (SP-A, not shown here any more) was painted again on Klein with the negative wired in, at cfg 2 and cfg 3 (the same seed, then another):

So the leg count is held by three things instead: the clay (it must have the right legs: the six-legged model would have taught every painting six), the anatomy words above, and your eye. The vision check cannot count legs; every candidate here was counted by hand, tip by tip, near side and far side. The walks follow the depth video's legs, so a painting with the right legs walks with the right legs, unless the painting and the depth disagree about where they are (the ghost legs in the mistakes).

9

Mistakes and dead ends

The first walk's legs (pass 93)

Every leg turned round its hip like a clock hand: the front and back legs swept their feet sideways, and from the game's front and back views the middle legs' step was too foreshortened to see. SP-B shows it. Found by drawing every foot's path per view (tips=1, gait_check.py) and by checking the painted walk against the driver (the painter copied it faithfully). Fixed with step=track: step 4.

The model had six legs

Nothing in the old probe said so; every painter copies the clay, so a six-legged clay would have made six-legged spiders in every look. Found by counting the bones, fixed with the leg copy (step 2). The probe counts leg chains now.

Facing the wrong way, resting in the wrong place

The probe guessed −Y for a spider facing +Y, and the file's rest pose sits far from its stance. The first leg copy turned legs round hips that were somewhere else and the legs crossed. Both fixed in the tools (the head bone gives a creature's forward; rest=frame:0).

The curl that folded back on itself

The first death recomputed each leg's fold axis at every joint. Once a leg passed straight down the axis flipped and the legs ended bunched out sideways instead of curled over the belly. One axis per leg, read from the standing pose, fixed it (picture in step 5).

Earlier looks, shortened (pass 92)

  • Ghost legs: a near-real look's front painting fanned all eight legs sideways while the front depth had the front pair reaching at the camera; Wan drew two extra legs where only the depth had them. Fix: words.front places the front legs where the model has them (all three looks here carry it).
  • The cartoon face that vanished: two very cartoony looks lost their faces in the walk (Wan follows the depth's shapes over the painting's); the clay given their proportions plus a depth video softened 10 px kept them (body_scale, head_pitch, ctl_soft). SP-B's face fits the model's head and needs none of it.
  • A ninth leg on one look's back painting: painted again as three candidates, the one without it picked.

Green bellies in the death

The paintings never show a spider's underside, so the walk invents the belly the roll shows, and on a green screen it invents green: every death has pale green patches under the curled legs, which the strip's green guard turns grey. Naming each look's belly colour in the death words ("its pale lavender belly … nothing green") did not stop it. A softened death video was worse (the whole spider went to mush) and was dropped. The dead pose still reads: on its back, legs curled up. A cleaner belly would need a painted dead still as the death's last frame, or a blue screen (not for SP-F, which is blue itself).

The middle of the roll smears

Frames 5 and 6 of every death (the spider on its side, legs flung) come out as a blur of legs: the video model cannot keep a figure together through a fast half-turn. death_keep skips them: [3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 11, 14, 24] keeps the stand, the start of the roll, the landing, the curl and the held dead pose. At the game's 12 frames a second the roll takes four frames, a third of a second.

Negative prompts: a dead end

As described above: they reach no model in this pipeline, and wiring a real one into Klein at cfg 2 or 3 did not hold the leg count.

A view painted again kept its old walk

The walk cache was found by name alone, so after a view was painted again its old walk would have come straight back. The cache now checks the painting, the seed and the control video, and picking a repainted view sets its old walk aside.

What worked first time

  • The side candidates followed the clay's legs: each picked side painting has eight, four near and four far, counted tip by tip.
  • The made walk and the made death drove Wan without trouble: the walks loop, the death ends on its back with its legs curled.
  • SP-B walked cleanly on the plain clay in all five views (its legs aside: the walk, not the painting).
  • SP-E and SP-F: the side candidates followed the fixed walk's clay; one in each set of four had eight legs and SP-B's grumpy face (SP-F took four more seeds: painting).
